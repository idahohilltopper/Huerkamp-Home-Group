VA Loans in Minnesota: A Guide for Veterans and Service Members

A VA home loan is one of the best benefits of service: no down payment for many buyers and no private mortgage insurance. Using it well takes a lender and an agent who know the program.

This guide explains the loan, the offer and Minnesota's tax help for disabled veterans. See also buying a home in Minnesota as a veteran. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

How a VA loan works

1

No down payment, in many cases

According to VA, there is no down payment as long as the sale price isn't higher than the appraised value. The loan comes from a private lender, such as a bank, credit union or mortgage company, and VA guarantees part of it.

2

No private mortgage insurance

VA loans don't require PMI or MIP, which can make the monthly payment lower than other low-down-payment loans.

3

The funding fee

A VA funding fee may apply. Some borrowers, such as many with a service-connected disability, don't pay it. Your lender can tell you whether it applies and whether it can be added to the loan.

4

Your Certificate of Eligibility

You need a Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which your lender can usually get for you. You must plan to live in the home, and VA loans can be used more than once.

5

Lenders add their own requirements

VA sets the program rules, and each lender sets its own credit and income requirements on top of them. Compare a few lenders who close VA loans often.

Choosing your team

6

You don't need a "VA realtor"

VA doesn't assign, approve or certify real estate agents, so you can work with any licensed agent. What matters is an agent who has closed VA purchases and knows how to make a VA offer as strong as a conventional one.

7

Bank and credit union agent programs

Programs such as USAA Agent Connection (powered by HomeStory) can give members a credit at closing when they use an agent in that network. Compare the credit with what an agent will do for you on price and terms.

Writing a VA offer that wins

8

Get pre-approved with an experienced VA lender

Sellers and their agents feel more confident when the lender closes VA loans regularly.

9

Plan for the appraisal

If the VA appraisal comes in below your price, you can renegotiate, pay the difference in cash or cancel under the VA clause in your purchase agreement. In a competitive situation, decide ahead of time how much of a gap you could cover.

10

Ask for seller-paid closing costs when the market allows

VA allows the seller to pay some closing costs and concessions, which keeps more cash in your pocket. In a busy market, your agent can help you weigh this against making your offer stand out.

11

Watch the home's condition

A VA appraiser also checks that the home is safe and sound. A home that needs major repairs can be harder to buy with a VA loan, so we flag those before you write an offer.

Minnesota benefits

12

Disabled Veterans' Market Value Exclusion

Minnesota lowers the taxable value of a qualifying veteran's homestead: $150,000 excluded for a service-connected disability rating of 70% or more, and $300,000 for a 100% total and permanent disability rating. A surviving spouse, and in some cases a primary family caregiver, can also qualify.

13

How to apply

The home must be your homestead, and you apply to your county assessor; applications are due by December 31 for taxes payable the next year. Check the Minnesota Department of Revenue for the current rules and form.

14

Apply for homestead too

Apply for homestead classification with your county assessor after you move in (by December 15 to count for the next year); it lowers property taxes and is required for the veterans' exclusion.
