Selling an Inherited or Probate Home in Minnesota

Selling a home after a loved one dies is a legal process and an emotional one, often at the same time. The first question is usually the hardest: who has the authority to sell?

This guide explains the common paths in Minnesota in plain English. It isn't legal advice: an estate attorney can tell you exactly what applies. See also selling a house in probate in Minnesota. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Who can sell the home

1

Find out how the home was titled

The deed decides the path. A home held in joint tenancy usually passes to the surviving owner. A home with a recorded transfer on death deed passes to the named beneficiary. A home in a trust is handled by the trustee. A home in the person's name alone usually goes through probate.

2

Transfer on death deeds

Minnesota allows a transfer on death deed, which passes the home to the named beneficiary without probate. The beneficiary usually records documents with the county to take title before selling.

3

Homes held in a trust

If the home was in a living trust, the successor trustee can usually sell it under the trust's terms, without probate. The title company will ask to see the trust documents.

4

Probate and the personal representative

When probate is needed, the court appoints a personal representative (an executor). Their court papers, called letters, give them authority to sell. Minnesota allows informal probate through the court's registrar in many cases, which is usually faster than formal probate.

The probate timeline

5

Creditors get a window to file claims

After notice to creditors is published, Minnesota generally gives creditors four months to present claims. Ask your attorney how this affects when the home can be sold and when money can be paid out.

6

Talk to the other heirs early

Agree on whether to sell, roughly when and at what price range, and who will handle the house in the meantime. Disagreements are the most common cause of delay.

7

Keep the home insured and secured

Tell the insurance company the home is vacant; many policies change or limit coverage for vacant homes. Keep the heat on in winter, check on the home regularly and keep up with utilities and taxes.

Preparing the home

8

Sort belongings before you list

Give family time to choose keepsakes, then consider an estate sale company, donation and a junk removal service. Keep important papers aside for the estate.

9

Decide how much to fix

An estate can sell a home as-is, make a few repairs or update it before listing. Your agent can compare what each would likely net after costs and time.

10

Disclosures for an estate sale

Minnesota's seller disclosure covers what the seller knows. A personal representative who never lived in the home may know little; say so honestly, and consider a pre-listing inspection so buyers have facts. City time-of-sale rules still apply.

11

Check the city's time-of-sale rules

Some cities require an evaluation or sewer inspection before any sale, including estate sales. See what your city requires.

Selling and taxes

12

Know the stepped-up basis

Inherited property generally takes a tax basis equal to its fair market value on the date of death, which can reduce or eliminate gain if the home is sold soon after. A date-of-death appraisal helps document it. Ask a tax adviser.

13

Property taxes may change

If the home was the owner's homestead, that classification can end, which can change the tax. Ask the county assessor how it applies.

14

Consider a cash sale if time matters

If the home needs a lot of work or the heirs live far away, a cash buyer can close quickly with fewer steps, usually at a lower price. Compare both: cash offer or list?
