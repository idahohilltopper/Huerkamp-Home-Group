Minnesota Time-of-Sale Inspections, City by City

Some Minnesota cities require a housing evaluation, a sewer inspection or both before a home can be listed or sold. Others require nothing. Knowing your city's rule early keeps a surprise from delaying your sale.

This guide lists the cities where we confirmed a requirement on the city's own website, plus two that repealed theirs. If your city isn't listed, confirm with the city: programs change. The same list is at what’s required to sell in Minnesota. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Housing evaluations

1

Minneapolis: Truth in Sale of Housing (TISH)

Sellers must have a full Truth in Sale of Housing report from a licensed evaluator before the home can be shown. Applies to single-family houses, duplexes, townhouses and first-time condominium conversions. Covers sales by owner or agent, contracts for deed and other title transfers. The evaluation can lead to required repairs, which are then reinspected. Not needed if the city has issued a Certificate of Code Compliance (good for a year) or a Certificate of Occupancy for a new home. Contact: Minneapolis TISH, 612-673-5840.

2

Saint Paul: Truth-in-Sale of Housing (TISH)

A licensed evaluator’s TISH report is required within three days of listing or advertising a home for sale. Applies to single-family homes, duplexes, condominiums and townhomes; buildings with three or more units are exempt. The report is for disclosure only. For single-family homes, the one required repair is a working hard-wired smoke alarm. A report is valid for 365 days and for one sale.

3

Bloomington: Time-of-Sale Housing Inspection

A Time-of-Sale evaluation must be completed and filed before the home is listed. Applies to single- and two-family homes, condominiums, townhouses and mobile homes; buildings with four or more units are excluded. The report goes to the city within 7 days of the evaluation. Hazards must be corrected, or assumed by the buyer, and city-inspected. The program includes an energy disclosure (since April 2022).

4

Richfield: Point of Sale Inspection

A city housing inspector must inspect the home before closing. Applies to single-family and two-family homes, including condominiums. The inspection can be done any time during the sale, but must be finished before title transfers. Apply online, then schedule. If the buyer takes on repairs, closing proceeds only under the conditions in the city’s brochure. Contact: Richfield Inspections, 612-861-9882.

5

St. Louis Park: Point-of-sale (property maintenance) inspection

Before listing, sellers apply for a property maintenance inspection permit and have the city inspect the home. Applies to residential, commercial and industrial property. Code corrections found in the inspection are usually made by the seller. Contact: Building and Energy, 952-924-2588.

6

Maplewood: Truth in Housing

A Truth in Housing evaluation by a city-licensed evaluator is required before listing or showing the home. The evaluation is for disclosure; the sale doesn’t depend on fixing what it lists. Contact: City of Maplewood, 651-249-2300.

7

New Hope: Point of Sale Inspection

A city inspector inspects the home and any required repairs are made before the sale. Applies to all residential sales: single-family homes, condos, townhomes and apartment buildings. The fee covers the inspection and one reinspection. Contact: New Hope Inspections, 763-531-5127.

8

Columbia Heights: Time of Sale Program

Sellers need a Certificate of Property Maintenance before selling, gifting or transferring the home. Applies to single-, two- and three-family homes, condominiums, townhouses and mobile homes. Contact: Community Development, 763-706-3670.

9

South St. Paul: Time-of-Sale Housing Inspection

A Time-of-Sale evaluation report from a city-licensed evaluator must be available when the home is shown. Applies to single-family, two-family and multiple-family dwellings. Evaluators set their own prices.

Sewer inspections

10

Golden Valley: Inflow and infiltration (I/I) compliance

A home without a Certificate of I/I Compliance must have its sewer inspected before it can be advertised for sale. The city televises the sewer line from the house to the main and checks the sump pump and roof drains for illegal connections. Repairs are due within 180 days (one 180-day extension); if they can’t be done before closing, escrow is required. The certificate stays with the property after the sale.

11

West St. Paul: Inflow and infiltration (I&I) inspection

A sewer (I&I) inspection must be completed before a property is sold. The city schedules the inspection at no charge: a camera inspection of the sewer and a sump check. A compliance letter is good for 10 years (25 if the whole line was lined or replaced).

12

Orono: Certificate of I/I Compliance

Properties on city sewer need a Certificate of Inflow and Infiltration Compliance before they’re sold. The inspection looks for roof drains, foundation drains and sump pumps connected to the sanitary sewer. Residential fee: $250. Homes on private septic systems aren’t part of this program. Contact: City of Orono, 952-249-4686.

13

Mounds View: Sewer lateral inspection (Ordinance 967)

Before title transfers, the sewer line from the house to the main must be televised and, if needed, repaired. Repairs are due within 90 days, and the property can’t transfer until they’re made; closing in escrow is allowed.

14

St. Anthony: Inflow and infiltration (I/I) inspection

Two inspections are required before a property is advertised or sold: a sump pump check and a televised sewer inspection. Individual condominium sales are exempt. A Certificate of I/I Compliance is valid for 3 years (longer after sewer work). The seller fixes violations before the sale unless the buyer takes them on in writing.

15

Tonka Bay: Inflow and infiltration (I&I) certificate

Sellers must get an I&I certificate before closing, based on a professional video inspection of the sewer line. The homeowner submits a televised scope of the sewer line from the house to the main; the Public Works Superintendent reviews it and approves the certificate. Application fee: $150. Contact: Tonka Bay City Hall, 952-474-7994.

16

Newport: Sewer lateral inspection before sale

Homes on city sewer must have the sewer lateral inspected and certified to city standards before the sale or transfer. The inspection is free; apply through West St. Paul’s permit office (651-259-2622), which handles it for Newport. Unpaved or worn-out driveways and parking areas connected to a paved public road must be paved before the sale (City Code 18-73 and 36-161). Call City Hall at least two business days before closing for a final water meter reading ($25). Contact: Newport City Hall, 651-459-5677.

Programs that were repealed

17

Hopkins: Truth-in-Housing (repealed)

Hopkins no longer requires a Truth-in-Housing evaluation to sell a home. The requirement was repealed effective January 14, 2021.

18

Robbinsdale: Point of Sale Housing Disclosure (repealed)

Robbinsdale no longer requires a point-of-sale housing evaluation to sell a home. The city repealed it by Ordinance 24-02 in 2024.

Statewide rules for every seller

19

Seller's disclosure

Minnesota sellers must disclose material facts they know about that could significantly affect the buyer's use or enjoyment of the property, using a written disclosure or an inspection report, or both sides can agree in writing to waive it.

20

Wells, septic systems and radon

Separate Minnesota disclosures cover private wells, septic systems and radon. Many counties also require a septic compliance inspection before a sale.

21

Lead-based paint in homes built before 1978

Federal law requires a lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet, and gives buyers a chance to test.
