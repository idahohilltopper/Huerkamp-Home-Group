Selling Your Home in Minnesota: 20 Ways to Net More

Selling a home is a project with a deadline, a budget and a lot of rules. The sellers who net the most usually aren't the ones who spent the most; they're the ones who priced it right, fixed the right things and had their paperwork ready before the first showing.

This guide walks through what our agents do with every seller, in order, with the Minnesota rules that catch people by surprise. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Before you list

1

Know your number before you pick a price

Start with what you would walk away with, not the list price. Subtract your mortgage payoff, agent commissions, Minnesota deed tax, title and closing fees, and any repairs or credits. Our seller net proceeds calculator does the math, and what it costs to sell in Minnesota explains each cost.

2

Get a pricing opinion from recent sales

Online estimates can't see your updates, your lot or your layout. Ask an agent for a market analysis built from homes like yours that sold nearby in the last few months, and the ones that are for sale now (your competition). Pricing within the range buyers are already paying draws the most showings in the first two weeks, when interest is highest.

3

Check your city's time-of-sale rules early

Some Minnesota cities, including Minneapolis, Saint Paul and Bloomington, require a city housing evaluation before you list, and some items it flags must be fixed. Order it early so a surprise doesn't delay your listing date. See what your city requires before you sell.

4

Gather the paperwork buyers will ask for

Pull together utility costs, the age of the roof, furnace, air conditioning and water heater, permits and receipts for updates, warranties, and HOA documents if you have an association. Organized records answer buyer questions before they become worries, and make your disclosures easier to fill out.

5

Fill out the seller disclosure honestly and completely

Minnesota law requires sellers to disclose material facts they know of that could significantly affect a buyer's use or enjoyment of the home. There are separate Minnesota disclosures for private wells, septic systems and radon. When in doubt, disclose: a problem a buyer learns about up front is a negotiating point; one they discover later can become a legal claim.

Preparing the home

6

Fix what an inspector will find

Dripping faucets, missing outlet covers, a running toilet, stained ceiling tiles and peeling paint each look small, but a long list of small items on an inspection report makes buyers wonder what else was ignored. Spend a weekend on the easy fixes before you list.

7

Spend on what buyers see, not what you would want

Fresh neutral paint, clean carpets or refinished floors, updated light fixtures and a tidy yard usually return more than a big remodel finished right before you sell. Ask your agent what buyers in your price range are comparing your home with before you spend money.

8

Declutter like you are already moving

Pack the things you won't use in the next two months: extra furniture, collections, out-of-season clothes, most of the photos. Clear closets and counters show buyers how much space there is. Rent a storage unit if you need to; you have to pack anyway.

9

Consider a pre-listing inspection

An inspection before you list lets you fix problems on your own schedule and price, rather than negotiating them under a deadline. It also helps you fill out the disclosure statement accurately. Anything it finds that you don't fix will need to be disclosed.

10

Test for radon before a buyer does

The Minnesota Department of Health estimates about 2 in 5 Minnesota homes have radon levels that pose a significant health risk, and Minnesota's radon law requires sellers to disclose what they know about radon in the home. If a test comes back high, a mitigation system can be installed before you list instead of becoming a last-minute negotiation.

Marketing and showings

11

Insist on professional photos

Most buyers decide which homes to see from the photos. Bright, wide, professional photos (and a floor plan or video tour where it fits) earn more showings than phone pictures. Ask any agent you interview exactly how they will market your home.

12

Make showings easy

Every showing you turn down is a buyer who may buy something else. Keep the home ready, leave during showings, put away valuables and medications, and plan for pets. A lockbox and a showing service let buyers see the home when they are ready.

13

Read the feedback in the first two weeks

If showings are steady but there are no offers, buyers are telling you something about price or condition. If there are few showings, the price or the photos aren't getting attention. Adjusting early usually costs less than waiting.

14

Plan for winter selling in Minnesota

Homes sell all year here. In winter, keep walks and the driveway clear, the home warm and well lit, and have summer photos of the yard ready. Fewer homes are for sale in winter, and the buyers who are out looking are often serious.

Offers to closing

15

Compare the whole offer, not just the price

Look at the financing type and pre-approval, the down payment, the earnest money, the inspection and appraisal terms, the closing date and any requests for help with closing costs. A slightly lower offer with strong financing and fewer conditions may net you more and close more surely.

16

Negotiate repairs with a plan

After the inspection, buyers may ask for repairs or a credit. Focus on safety and major systems, and consider offering a credit or a price change instead of doing repairs under a deadline. Your agent can tell you what is typical for your market.

17

Prepare for the appraisal

If the buyer is financing, the lender orders an appraisal. Give your agent a list of updates and recent sales that support your price to share with the appraiser. If the appraisal comes in low, your options depend on the terms of the purchase agreement.

18

Don't forget the deed tax and the payoff

In Minnesota the seller typically pays the state deed tax, which is 0.33% of the sale price. Your title company will also order the exact payoff of your mortgage and any home equity line; make sure a line of credit is closed, not just paid down.

19

Know how the sale affects your taxes

If you owned and lived in the home as your main home for at least two of the last five years, you may be able to exclude up to $250,000 of gain from income ($500,000 for most married couples filing jointly), according to the IRS. Ask a tax adviser how the rules apply to you.

20

Plan your move around the closing

Decide early whether you will buy first, sell first or ask to stay after closing for a few days. Leave the home clean and empty, with keys, garage openers and manuals, and transfer utilities on the closing date. Final walk-throughs go smoothly when nothing is left behind.
