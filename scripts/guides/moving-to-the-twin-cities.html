Moving to the Twin Cities: A Relocation Guide

The Twin Cities area covers two central cities and dozens of suburbs across seven core counties, each with its own taxes, home styles and feel. Choosing the right place is the hardest part of moving here, and the part most worth doing carefully.

This guide helps you narrow the map, buy with confidence from a distance and get settled once you arrive. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653. You can also explore our community guides.

Choosing where to live

1

Start with your commute

Where you work, and how often you go in, narrows the map faster than anything else. Drive times change a lot at rush hour, especially across the rivers. Compare places with our commute tool before you look at homes.

2

Decide city, close-in suburb or outer suburb

Minneapolis and Saint Paul offer older homes, walkable neighborhoods and transit. Close-in suburbs mix older homes with shorter drives. Outer suburbs tend to have newer homes and larger lots. Minneapolis suburbs compared lays out the trade-offs.

3

Compare property taxes, not just prices

Minnesota property taxes vary by city, county and school district, so two homes at the same price can have very different tax bills. See property taxes by county and the cost of living in Minnesota.

4

Look at the home styles each area offers

Older areas have bungalows, Tudors and Victorians with character and older systems; postwar suburbs have ramblers and split-levels; newer suburbs have two-stories and townhomes with modern layouts. Knowing which style you want narrows the search.

5

Think about lakes, parks and trails

The metro has hundreds of lakes and a large regional park and trail system. If you want to live near water or trails, start there. See lake homes and our community guides.

6

Visit before you commit, if you can

Spend a weekend driving the areas on your list at different times of day. If you can't, ask your agent for video walk-throughs of the neighborhoods as well as the homes.

Buying from a distance

7

Get pre-approved before you travel

A pre-approval lets you write an offer the same day you see the right home. If your income is changing with the move, tell your lender up front; an offer letter from your new employer may be needed.

8

Use video tours and a local agent

Many relocating buyers tour by live video first and visit only the finalists. Ask your agent to point out what a camera hides: road noise, smells, slopes and the condition of the neighbors' yards.

9

Understand winter in a house

Ask about the furnace's age and service history, insulation, windows and ice dams on the roof. In winter, check how the driveway faces and how long it is: you or a service will be clearing it.

10

Sign and close from anywhere

Most of the paperwork can be signed electronically, and many title companies can arrange remote or mail-away closings. Ask early what your lender and title company allow. Confirm wiring instructions by phone, never by email alone.

11

Plan for a rent-back or short-term rental

If your move date and closing don't line up, a short-term rental or a rent-back from the seller can bridge the gap. Your agent can ask for one in the offer.

Settling in

12

Transfer your license and vehicle registration

Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services handles driver's licenses and vehicle registration. New residents should switch both over soon after moving; check DVS for the current requirements.

13

Apply for homestead

If the home is your main residence, apply for homestead classification with your county assessor. It can lower your property taxes; apply by December 15 for it to count the next year.

14

Get ready for snow

Many cities have winter parking rules and snow emergency routes, and expect sidewalks to be cleared after a snowfall. Check your city's website for its rules before the first storm.

15

Set up your utilities and services

Electricity and natural gas providers depend on where you live, and many cities run their own water and sewer billing and organize trash and recycling. Your city's website lists what to set up.
