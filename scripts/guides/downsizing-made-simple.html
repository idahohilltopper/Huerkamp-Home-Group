Downsizing Made Simple

Downsizing is rarely just a real estate decision. It means sorting through years of belongings, deciding where you want to live next and timing a sale and a purchase so you only move once.

This guide breaks it into steps you can take at your own pace. See also our downsizing page and selling a parent’s home. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Deciding what's next

1

Write down why you are moving

Less upkeep, fewer stairs, being near family, lower costs, travel: your reasons decide what kind of home fits next. Keep the list handy when you tour; it keeps a beautiful but impractical home from pulling you off course.

2

Compare your options side by side

A smaller single-family home, a one-level townhome, a condo, an age-restricted (55+) community, renting, or senior living with services each come with different costs and freedoms. See townhomes and condos for sale.

3

Look closely at association fees and rules

With a townhome or condo, the monthly fee may cover snow removal, lawn care, exterior maintenance, insurance on the building and some utilities. Read what it covers, the rules, and the reserve fund, and ask about upcoming special assessments.

4

Estimate what you will walk away with

Your home's value, minus the mortgage and costs of selling, sets your budget for what's next. Our instant home value estimate and seller net proceeds calculator give you a starting point.

5

Ask about taxes before you sell

The IRS lets many owners exclude up to $250,000 of gain on the sale of a main home ($500,000 for most married couples filing jointly) if they owned and lived in it for two of the last five years. A tax adviser can tell you how it applies to you.

Timing the move

6

Decide: buy first, sell first or both at once

Buying first means one move but may require a bridge loan or enough cash. Selling first frees your money but may mean a short-term rental. Your agent can write offers that line the two closings up, or ask the buyer to let you stay a few days after closing.

7

Look at older-adult financing options

Homeowners 62 and older can use a reverse mortgage for purchase (an FHA Home Equity Conversion Mortgage) to buy their next home with a smaller down payment and no monthly mortgage payment. It has costs and rules, and federal law requires counseling with a HUD-approved counselor first.

8

Give yourself more time than you think

Sorting a home you have lived in for decades takes months, not weekends. Start with rooms you use least, and set a target date for each room.

Sorting and letting go

9

Measure your next home first

Know the room sizes and closet space before you decide what furniture comes along. A simple floor plan with your furniture drawn to scale prevents moving pieces that won't fit.

10

Sort into keep, give, sell and donate

Decide on one room at a time. Offer family items early and set a date for pickups. Things that don't fit your next home are easier to let go of when they go to someone who will use them.

11

Consider an estate sale or a senior move manager

Estate sale companies price, sell and clear what you leave behind for a share of the sales. Senior move managers help plan, sort, pack and set up the new home. Ask for references and a written agreement.

12

Keep important papers together

Set aside the deed, mortgage statements, tax records, insurance, receipts for updates and anything you will need for the sale or your taxes, in one box that moves with you.

Selling the family home

13

Make the few repairs that matter

Homes that have been lived in for decades often need updated paint, flooring or fixtures. Ask your agent which updates will pay for themselves and which buyers would rather do themselves.

14

Complete Minnesota's required disclosures

Minnesota sellers must disclose material facts they know about the home, and there are separate disclosures for wells, septic systems and radon. If you have lived there a long time, write down what you have replaced and when; it helps buyers and makes the forms easier.

15

Check your city's time-of-sale rules

Some cities, including Minneapolis, Saint Paul and Bloomington, require a city evaluation before you sell. See what your city requires.

16

Consider a cash offer

If repairs or showings feel like too much, a cash sale can close quickly with fewer showings, usually at a lower price. Compare both paths: cash offer or list?

17

If you are selling for a parent or an estate

Someone needs legal authority to sign: a power of attorney, a trustee, or a personal representative named by the probate court. Start that paperwork early. See selling a house in probate in Minnesota.
