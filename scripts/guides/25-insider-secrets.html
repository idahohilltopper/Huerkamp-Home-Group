Before you shop

1

Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified

A pre-qualification is a quick estimate. A pre-approval means a lender has actually checked your income, savings and credit. Sellers and their agents take an offer with a pre-approval letter more seriously, and you'll know your real price range before you fall in love with a house.

2

Budget for the monthly payment, not just the price

Your payment is more than principal and interest: add property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance if you put down less than 20%, and any HOA fee. Property taxes differ a lot from one Twin Cities city to the next, so the same price can mean a very different payment. Try our mortgage calculator, which uses real local tax rates.

3

Know your cash to close

You'll need the down payment plus closing costs: lender and title fees, plus taxes and insurance paid ahead. After you apply for a loan, the lender must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days that lists these costs. Compare Loan Estimates from more than one lender line by line.

4

Ask about down payment help

Minnesota Housing and many cities and counties offer down payment and closing cost help, mostly for first-time buyers, with income and price limits. Ask your lender early, because some programs work only with approved lenders. See our Minnesota down payment assistance guide.

5

Freeze your credit habits until closing

Don't open new credit cards, finance a car or furniture, change jobs or move large sums of money without talking to your lender first. Lenders often re-check credit and employment right before closing, and a change can delay or even stop your loan.

6

Understand your buyer agreement

Since August 17, 2024, agents who belong to a multiple listing service need a written agreement with you before you tour homes together. Read it: it says how long it lasts, what your agent does for you and how your agent is paid. Ask questions until it makes sense. A good agent will explain every line.

While you search

7

Set up instant alerts

The best-priced homes often go fast. Save your search so new listings come to your inbox the day they hit the market, then be ready to see them quickly. You can set alerts in minutes at huerkamphomes.com.

8

Read the price history and days on market

A home that's been for sale a while, or has had price cuts, may leave room to negotiate. A home that just came on in a busy neighborhood may not. Your agent can show you how long similar homes nearby took to sell.

9

Visit at different times of day

A quiet Sunday showing doesn't tell you about weekday traffic, a nearby business or where the sun hits in the afternoon. Drive by on a weekday evening and, if you can, again on a weekend.

10

Drive the commute at rush hour

Ten miles can take 15 minutes or 45. Try the drive at the times you'd actually make it, or use our commute tool to compare places before you look.

11

Read the HOA papers before you're committed

For a townhome or condo, the association's documents tell you what the monthly fee covers, what you maintain yourself, the rules (rentals, pets, parking) and whether a special assessment is coming. Ask for the budget and the reserve fund balance; thin reserves can mean big bills later.

12

Check property taxes and special assessments

Look up the home's current taxes on the county website, and ask whether the city has planned a street, sewer or other project that could bring a special assessment. Your purchase agreement should say who pays assessments that are pending or levied.

13

Read the city's time-of-sale report

Some Minnesota cities, including Minneapolis, Saint Paul and Bloomington, require a city housing evaluation before a home is sold. If the home has one, read it: it lists items the city requires to be fixed and other conditions it noted. See what each city requires.

Making your offer

14

Price your offer with sold homes, not asking prices

The list price is the seller's opinion. Recent sales of similar homes nearby show what buyers actually paid. Ask your agent for the comparable sales before you decide on a number.

15

Price isn't the only thing sellers care about

A closing date that fits the seller's move, a strong pre-approval, fewer requests and a clean, well-organized offer can beat a slightly higher price. Your agent can find out what matters most to the seller.

16

Put earnest money to work

Earnest money is a deposit that shows you're serious. It's usually held by a title company or brokerage and counts toward your down payment and closing costs at closing. Know the conditions in your purchase agreement under which you would get it back if the deal falls apart.

17

Keep an inspection contingency

An inspection contingency lets you have the home inspected and back out or negotiate if something serious turns up. In a busy market, some buyers shorten the time frame instead of giving it up. Talk through the trade-offs with your agent before you waive any protection.

18

Plan for a low appraisal

Your lender bases the loan on the appraised value, not the price you offered. If the appraisal comes in low, you may need to cover the difference in cash, renegotiate or walk away, depending on your agreement. Decide before you offer how much of a gap you could cover.

19

Read every seller disclosure

Minnesota law requires sellers to disclose material facts they know about that could significantly affect a buyer's use or enjoyment of the property. There are also separate Minnesota disclosures for private wells, private septic systems and radon. Read them all before your inspection so you know what to ask about.

From inspection to closing

20

Go to the inspection

Walk the house with your inspector. You'll learn where the main water shut-off and electrical panel are, what's normal wear and what needs attention soon, and you can ask questions on the spot. The written report is much easier to understand when you've seen it in person.

21

Test for radon

Radon is a natural radioactive gas you can't see or smell. The Minnesota Department of Health estimates that about 2 in 5 Minnesota homes have radon levels that pose a significant health risk. A test during your inspection period is inexpensive, and if levels are high, a mitigation system can bring them down.

22

Scope the sewer line on older homes

Tree roots and old clay or cast-iron pipes are common in older Twin Cities neighborhoods, and the line from the house to the street is usually the owner's responsibility. A camera inspection of the sewer line is a small cost compared with replacing it.

23

Shop homeowners insurance early

Get quotes as soon as your offer is accepted. Premiums and coverage can vary a lot from company to company, and the age of the roof can affect both. Your lender will need proof of insurance before closing.

24

Do the final walk-through

Shortly before closing, walk through the home again. Check that agreed repairs are done, the appliances and items in your agreement are there, everything works and nothing new is damaged. Problems are much easier to fix before you sign than after.

25

Never trust emailed wiring instructions

Scammers send fake emails that look like they're from your agent or title company, with new instructions for wiring your closing money. Before you wire anything, call the title company at a phone number you found yourself (not one in the email) to confirm the instructions. If something feels off, stop and call us.
