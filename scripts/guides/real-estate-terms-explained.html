Real Estate Terms, Explained

Real estate has its own vocabulary, and some of it is specific to Minnesota. This guide explains the terms you are most likely to hear, in the order you are likely to hear them.

Getting ready

1

Pre-approval

A lender's written statement, after checking your income, assets and credit, of how much it is willing to lend you. Stronger than a pre-qualification, which is an estimate.

2

Loan Estimate

A standard three-page form a lender must give you within three business days of your loan application, showing the rate, payment and closing costs.

3

Buyer agreement

A written agreement between you and your agent that sets out the agent's services, how they are paid and how long it lasts. Since August 17, 2024, MLS agents need one before touring homes with you.

4

Private mortgage insurance (PMI)

Insurance that protects the lender, usually required on conventional loans with less than 20% down. It can be removed once you have enough equity.

Making an offer

5

Purchase agreement

The contract between buyer and seller that sets the price, terms, contingencies and closing date.

6

Earnest money

A deposit that shows the buyer is serious, usually held by a title company or brokerage and applied to the buyer's costs at closing.

7

Contingency

A condition in the purchase agreement that must be met for the sale to go forward, such as financing, inspection, appraisal or the sale of the buyer's current home.

8

Comparable sales (comps)

Recent sales of similar nearby homes, used to set a price and by appraisers to judge value.

9

Dual agency

When one brokerage represents both buyer and seller. Minnesota allows it only with both parties' written consent, and the agents can't advocate for one side against the other.

Before closing

10

Appraisal

A licensed appraiser's opinion of the home's value, ordered by the lender to make sure the home is worth at least the loan amount.

11

Seller's disclosure

The seller's written statement of material facts they know about the home. Minnesota also requires separate disclosures for wells, septic systems and radon.

12

Time-of-sale inspection

A city-required housing evaluation or sewer inspection before a home is listed or sold, required in some Minnesota cities, such as Minneapolis' Truth in Sale of Housing.

13

Title insurance

Insurance against problems with ownership, such as old liens or errors in records. A lender's policy protects the lender; an owner's policy protects you.

14

Special assessment

A charge from a city for a public improvement that benefits the property, such as a new street or sewer. The purchase agreement says who pays assessments that are pending or levied.

Closing and after

15

Closing Disclosure

The final statement of your loan terms and closing costs, which you must receive at least three business days before closing.

16

Deed tax

A Minnesota tax on the transfer of property, usually paid by the seller: 0.33% of the price, plus 0.01% in Hennepin and Ramsey counties.

17

Mortgage registry tax

A Minnesota tax on a new mortgage, usually paid by the buyer, based on the loan amount.

18

Escrow account

An account your lender may set up to collect part of your property taxes and insurance each month and pay the bills when due.

19

Homestead

The Minnesota property tax classification for a home you own and live in, which can lower your taxes. You apply with your county assessor.

20

Contract for deed

A way to buy where the seller finances the purchase and keeps the title until the buyer pays in full. Minnesota has specific rules for these contracts; have an attorney review one before you sign.
