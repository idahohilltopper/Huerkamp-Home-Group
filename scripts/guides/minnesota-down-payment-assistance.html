Minnesota Down Payment Assistance, Explained

For many buyers, the down payment is the hardest part of buying a home. Minnesota has more help than most people realize: statewide programs, city and county programs, and loan types built for small down payments.

This guide explains how the help works, who it's for and how to apply, in plain English. Program amounts and income limits change, so confirm current numbers with your lender or on our Minnesota down payment assistance page. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

How assistance works

1

Assistance is usually a loan, not free money

Most down payment help in Minnesota comes as a second loan. Some have no monthly payment and are repaid when you sell, refinance or pay off your first mortgage (often called deferred loans). Others are paid monthly at a low rate. A few programs forgive the loan over time. Ask exactly when and how yours is repaid.

2

It works together with your first mortgage

You apply for assistance through your mortgage lender, at the same time as your home loan. That means the lender you choose matters: many statewide programs work only with participating lenders.

3

Income and price limits apply

Most programs have household income limits and purchase price limits, which can differ by county and household size. Your lender can check whether you qualify before you start shopping, so you know your price range.

4

"First-time buyer" may include you

Many programs use the federal definition: someone who hasn't owned a home in the last three years. If you owned years ago, you may still count. Some programs are open to repeat buyers too.

Statewide and local programs

5

Minnesota Housing's programs

Minnesota Housing, the state's housing finance agency, offers first mortgages for first-time buyers (Start Up) and for repeat buyers (Step Up), along with down payment and closing cost help such as the Deferred Payment Loan and the Monthly Payment Loan. Each has its own rules; a participating lender can tell you which you qualify for.

6

City and county programs

Many cities and counties in the Twin Cities run their own down payment programs, sometimes for a specific area or type of home. They can often be combined with state help. Ask your lender or agent which programs cover the cities you are looking in, and check each city's housing department website.

7

Nonprofit and community programs

Nonprofits and community land trusts offer additional help and lower-cost homes in some areas, often paired with homebuyer coaching. The Minnesota Homeownership Center lists programs and counselors across the state.

8

Loans built for small down payments

FHA loans allow 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher. Some conventional loans, including Fannie Mae's HomeReady and Freddie Mac's Home Possible, allow 3% down. VA and USDA loans can require no down payment for eligible buyers and homes. Assistance can cover some or all of what's left.

Other ways to cover your costs

9

Gifts from family

Most loan types allow down payment gifts from family. Lenders will ask for a signed gift letter and records showing the money moving from the giver's account to yours. Tell your lender before the money moves, not after.

10

Seller-paid closing costs

A seller can agree to pay part of your closing costs, up to limits set by the loan type: up to 6% of the price for FHA loans, up to 4% for VA loans (on certain costs), and 3% to 9% for conventional loans depending on your down payment. In a busy market a request like this can make an offer less attractive, so ask your agent how to use it.

11

Lender credits

Some lenders will pay part of your closing costs in exchange for a slightly higher interest rate. It lowers your cash to close but raises your payment, so compare both on your Loan Estimate.

12

Retirement accounts, carefully

Some retirement plans allow loans or first-time buyer withdrawals, but taxes, penalties and lost growth can make them expensive. Talk to a tax adviser before using retirement money.

Step by step

13

Take a homebuyer education class

Many programs require one. In Minnesota, Home Stretch workshops (through the Minnesota Homeownership Center network) and Framework's online course are widely accepted. Ask your lender which your program needs before you sign up.

14

Choose a participating lender

Ask lenders directly: "Do you offer Minnesota Housing loans and the city and county programs where I want to buy?" A lender who uses these programs often will move faster and catch problems early.

15

Get pre-approved with the assistance included

Your pre-approval should list the assistance program, so your offer shows sellers how the purchase will be paid for. Some programs need time to approve funds, so plan a realistic closing date.

16

Read the assistance terms before you sign

Before closing, read the note and mortgage for the assistance loan: when it is repaid, whether it is forgiven, and what happens if you sell, refinance or rent the home. Keep a copy with your records.
