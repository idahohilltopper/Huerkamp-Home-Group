Selling and Buying at the Same Time

Most homeowners need the money from their current home to buy the next one. That makes timing the hardest part: you don't want to own two homes, and you don't want to have nowhere to live.

There is no single right order. This guide lays out the options so you can choose what fits your money, your market and your nerves. Start with an instant home value estimate. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Know your numbers first

1

Find out what your home will net

Your equity is your down payment for the next home. Start with a realistic price from recent sales, then subtract the mortgage and costs to sell. Our seller net proceeds calculator does the math.

2

Get pre-approved for both scenarios

Ask your lender what you can buy if your current home has sold, and whether you could buy before it sells (carrying both payments for a while). The answer decides which options below are open to you.

3

Learn how busy your markets are

If homes in your price range sell quickly where you live, selling first carries less risk. If the homes you want to buy go fast, a contingent offer may not win. Your agent can show you both.

The options

4

Sell first, then buy

You know exactly what you have to spend and you make a strong, non-contingent offer. The risk is needing a place to live in between. A rent-back or a longer closing can close that gap.

5

Buy first, then sell

You move once, on your schedule, and stage the old home empty. You need to qualify for both payments or have another way to fund the down payment.

6

Make an offer contingent on your sale

A home-sale contingency lets you back out if your home doesn't sell by a set date. Sellers accept them more readily in slower markets, often with a clause that lets them keep showing their home and ask you to remove the contingency if another offer comes in.

7

Line up both closings on the same day

Many Minnesota buyers close on the sale in the morning and the purchase in the afternoon, using the money from one for the other. It takes coordination between both title companies and lenders, and a little luck with schedules.

Ways to bridge the gap

8

Ask to rent back after closing

A rent-back lets you stay in the home you sold for a few days or weeks after closing. Put it in writing in the purchase agreement: dates, any rent or deposit, utilities and the condition you'll leave it in.

9

Consider a bridge loan

A bridge loan borrows against your current home's equity to fund the next down payment, and is paid off when the home sells. It's short-term and usually costs more than a regular mortgage, so compare the total cost.

10

Open a home equity line before you list

Some owners open a home equity line of credit on their current home to use for the next down payment. Most lenders won't open one on a home that's already listed for sale, so it has to happen first.

11

Look at "buy before you sell" programs

Some companies will help you buy first by advancing your equity or making a backup offer on your home. Read the fees and terms closely and compare them with a bridge loan.

12

Plan short-term housing as a backup

A furnished rental, family or a storage-and-stay plan can turn a gap of a few weeks into an inconvenience instead of a crisis.

Making it smooth

13

Get your home ready to list before you shop

Declutter, make repairs and get photos done before you start touring. When you find the right home, you can list within days.

14

Use one team for both sides

When the same team handles your sale and your purchase, the timelines, contingencies and closing dates are planned together instead of by two people who never talk.

15

Know how taxes work on the sale

If you owned and lived in the home for at least two of the last five years, the IRS lets many owners exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for most married couples filing jointly). Ask a tax adviser how it applies to you.
