Pricing Your Home Right

Price is the most important decision you make as a seller. Priced right, a home draws showings and offers while it is new. Priced too high, it often sits, and ends up selling for less than it would have.

This guide explains how agents and appraisers arrive at a price and how to read the market once you're listed. For a starting point, try our instant home value estimate. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

How value is set

1

Buyers and appraisers look at sold homes

Value comes from what buyers recently paid for similar homes nearby: same style, size, age, condition and location. It doesn't come from what you paid, what you put into it or what a neighbor is asking.

2

The best comparisons are recent and close

Sales from the last three to six months within your neighborhood or subdivision tell the most. Older or farther sales need adjusting for how the market has moved.

3

Active and pending listings show the competition

Homes for sale now are what buyers will compare yours to this week. Pending sales hint at where the market is going before those prices are public.

4

Online estimates are a starting point

Automated estimates can't see condition, updates, layout or views, so they can be far off in either direction for a single home. Use them as a rough range, not a price.

Choosing the number

5

Price for the first two weeks

A new listing gets the most attention in its first days. Buyers who have been waiting see it at once. If the price is right, that's when the strongest offers come.

6

Mind the search brackets

Buyers search in round ranges: up to $400,000, up to $500,000. A home priced just above a bracket misses everyone searching below it. Your agent can show where your buyers are searching.

7

Account for condition honestly

If comparable homes were updated and yours wasn't, the price has to reflect it, or the repairs need to happen first. Buyers and appraisers will make the adjustment either way.

8

Think about the appraisal

Most buyers finance, and the lender's appraisal has to support the price. A price far above the recent sales may win an offer and then fall apart at appraisal.

After you list

9

Read the activity in the first two weeks

Lots of showings but no offers usually points to price compared with the competition, or condition. Few showings usually points to price or the photos. Your agent should report feedback each week.

10

Adjust early rather than often

One meaningful price change, made early, usually does more than several small cuts spread over months, which can make buyers wait for the next one.

11

Watch the market around you

New listings, price changes and sales near you change your home's position week to week. Ask for an update on what sold and what came on.

12

Consider terms as well as price

Help with a buyer's closing costs, a flexible closing date or including some items can attract buyers without a price change. Compare what each option nets you.

When offers arrive

13

Compare offers by what you net

Look at price, financing, contingencies, closing date and any requests for help with closing costs. Run each through our seller net proceeds calculator.

14

Multiple offers: set a clear deadline

If several buyers are interested, your agent can ask for best offers by a set time, which gives every buyer the same chance and you a clean comparison.

15

A low offer is still a conversation

Counter with your reasoning and the recent sales behind your price. Many good sales start with an offer that felt low.
