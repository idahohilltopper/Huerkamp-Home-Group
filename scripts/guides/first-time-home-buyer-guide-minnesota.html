First-Time Home Buyer's Guide to Minnesota

Buying your first home is exciting and a little overwhelming. There are new words, new costs and a lot of people involved. This guide puts every step in order, so you always know what comes next and what to ask.

You may count as a "first-time buyer" for many programs even if you have owned before: the common definition is someone who hasn't owned a home in the last three years. Questions? Call or text us at (612) 502-7653.

Getting ready

1

Decide what you can afford, by month

Lenders look at your debts compared with your income, but the number that matters is a monthly payment you are comfortable with: principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and any HOA fee. Try our affordability calculator and rent vs. buy calculator.

2

Check your credit early

Get your free credit reports (annualcreditreport.com is the official site), fix any errors and pay every bill on time. Paying down credit card balances can raise your score. Starting a few months before you shop gives changes time to show up.

3

Learn the loan types

FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down for borrowers with a credit score of 580 or higher. Some conventional loans allow 3% down. VA loans for eligible veterans and service members and USDA loans in eligible rural areas can require no down payment. Ask a lender which fits you; the lowest down payment isn't always the lowest monthly cost.

4

Understand mortgage insurance

With less than 20% down on a conventional loan you will usually pay private mortgage insurance (PMI). Under federal law it ends automatically when your balance reaches 78% of the home's original value, and you can ask to remove it at 80%. FHA mortgage insurance works differently, so ask how long it lasts.

5

Find out what help you qualify for

Minnesota Housing, along with many cities, counties and nonprofits, offers down payment and closing cost help for first-time buyers, usually with income and purchase price limits. Some require a homebuyer education class. See our Minnesota down payment assistance guide and first-time buyer programs.

6

Take a homebuyer education class

Many assistance programs require one, and it is worth taking even if yours doesn't. Classes cover budgeting, loans and the buying process, and are offered online and in person across Minnesota.

Getting approved

7

Talk to more than one lender

Ask two or three lenders for a Loan Estimate on the same day for the same loan. The lender must send it within three business days of your application. Compare the rate, the lender fees and the cash needed to close, line by line.

8

Get a full pre-approval

A pre-approval means the lender has checked your income, assets and credit. It tells you your price range and shows sellers you can close. Have pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, and bank statements ready.

9

Save for more than the down payment

Plan for closing costs, prepaid property taxes and insurance, the inspection and appraisal, moving costs and a cushion for the first repairs. Keep the money in your accounts; lenders need to see where large deposits came from.

House hunting

10

Choose an agent who explains things

Since August 17, 2024, agents who belong to a multiple listing service need a written agreement with you before touring homes. Read it, ask how your agent is paid and choose someone who answers your questions patiently. That's most of the job for a first-time buyer.

11

Make a must-have list and a nice-to-have list

Bedrooms, commute, yard, garage, one level or two: decide what you can't live without and what you could add later. It keeps you focused when you see a home with a great kitchen and a bad commute.

12

Compare homes by their monthly cost

Property taxes and HOA fees can make two homes with the same price very different each month. Look up the taxes on the listing or the county website, and see property taxes by county.

13

Look past paint and furniture

Paint, carpet and light fixtures are easy to change. The roof, foundation, windows, furnace and layout are not. A first home with good bones and dated finishes is often a better buy than a flipped home with hidden problems.

Offer to keys

14

Write an offer with your agent

Your agent will look at recent sales to choose a price, and help you decide on earnest money, the closing date and contingencies for financing and inspection. The purchase agreement is a binding contract, so read it and ask about anything you don't understand.

15

Inspect, and test for radon

Go to the home inspection and ask questions. The Minnesota Department of Health estimates about 2 in 5 Minnesota homes have radon levels that pose a significant health risk, so test during your inspection period. For older homes, consider a sewer line camera inspection.

16

Lock your rate and keep your finances steady

Ask your lender when to lock your interest rate. Until closing, don't open new credit, make large purchases or change jobs without talking to your lender.

17

Read your Closing Disclosure

You must receive your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Compare it with your Loan Estimate and ask your lender about any change. Confirm wiring instructions by calling the title company at a number you looked up yourself.

18

Apply for homestead after you move in

In Minnesota, your home can be classified as a homestead, which can lower your property taxes. You must apply with your county assessor; to count for the following year's taxes, apply by December 15.
