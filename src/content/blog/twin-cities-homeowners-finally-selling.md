**If you bought in 2020 or 2021, your mortgage rate probably starts with a 2 or 3, and maybe the house doesn't fit anymore. Trading that rate for 6.5% feels like a trap. Then June happened. Rates didn't fall, but something else broke the standoff, and it's something you already own.**

These figures are from the June market reports, as I covered in the video above (August 2026).

## Why the market froze

Move a loan from 3% to 6.5% and your principal and interest payment can climb roughly 35% to 40%, before taxes and insurance. So people stayed put. When enough owners ran the same math at the same time, the market seized up. Buyers fought over scraps, offered over asking, waived inspections they should have kept, and still lost. Meanwhile, owners lived in homes that stopped working for them years ago.

## June broke it open

- **New listings across the Twin Cities jumped about 10% to 12%**, with no rate cut and no policy change.
- Metro inventory reached close to **11,000 homes**, and the statewide count of just over 19,000 was a **7-year high**.
- Metro supply rose to **2.8 months**. That's not a flood, but it's a big change from springs with lines out the door at open houses.
- It showed up across the metro, not just in one hot pocket.

## Rates never helped

Freddie Mac's weekly average stayed elevated straight through June. It dipped to about 6.43% for one week in early July, then climbed back to around 6.6%. For two years, forecasts promised lower rates, and most were wrong. The lock-in effect didn't disappear. It just stopped being the only number that mattered.

## What broke the standoff: equity

If you bought in 2021, the metro median is up about **17%** since then. That equity isn't real until you sell, but it changes the math on the next house: a bigger down payment, a smaller loan, and a payment that can work even at 6.5%.

The data lines up: statewide pending sales for homes **under $300,000 rose 5.2%**, while pending sales for homes **over $1 million rose 42%**, consistent with owners who had more equity and more options moving first.

And life doesn't wait for the perfect rate: a new job, another kid who needs a room, a shorter commute. After four years of postponed plans, enough owners decided the numbers worked well enough.

## Prices didn't crater

The fear was that a wave of listings would tank prices. It didn't:

- **Pending sales rose 9.7%**, the second strong month in a row.
- **Closings rose 10.7%**, with about 5,600 homes closed in June.
- The **metro median hit $410,000**, a June record. Statewide it was $375,000.

Both supply and demand grew, and buyers who'd lost bidding wars for years finally had real choices.

## But selling got harder

- Homes averaged **42 days on market**, up from 39.
- Sellers got **99.6%** of original list price, down from 100%.

Well-priced, well-presented homes still move fast. Homes that test the market with an ambitious price sit. The leaders of both the Minneapolis Area Realtors and Minnesota Realtors described a healthier balance, with appropriately priced homes still selling. Neither called it a buyer's market.

## What it means for you

If you're selling, every one of those 11,000 listings is a house a buyer could choose instead of yours. This market doesn't punish sellers; it punishes low-effort sellers. Price honestly from day one, and take care of the small stuff: fresh paint, a clean yard and photos that showcase the home.

Whether now is your time comes down to three questions:

1. **What do you still owe?**
2. **What would you realistically buy next?**
3. **How does your home stack up against what's on the market now?**
