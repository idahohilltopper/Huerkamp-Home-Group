**Minneapolis just ranked #3 in the country for best places to retire. So why would I make a video about reasons *not* to retire here? Because that ranking will bring people who aren't prepared for what they're walking into. Some will love it, and some will be miserable. The difference is whether you went in with the full picture or just the highlight reel.**

This isn't a hit piece. Minneapolis is one of the best cities in America. But the best city for a 35-year-old and the best city for a 65-year-old aren't automatically the same. Figures are as of my video above (April 2026).

## 1. Winter isn't a vibe; it's a full-time job

Dealing with cold on the way to work is different from cold being your whole life. Think about winter three ways:

- **Physically.** The biggest winter hazard for older adults here is **black ice**, a thin, nearly invisible coating after light snow, a freeze-thaw cycle or freezing fog. Falls on ice are one of the leading causes of serious injury for adults over 60. Sudden storms (one March storm dropped almost 10 inches overnight at the airport) are exactly when it forms.
- **Financially.** In older or less efficient homes, a cold snap can produce a heating bill that makes you look twice.
- **Socially.** When it's below zero and the roads are glazed, you don't meet a friend for coffee or drive to the gym. Isolation compounds fast, and loneliness is a real health risk.

Minneapolis has mastered winter living better than almost any city, with heated parking everywhere and real community spirit around winter. And summers here are pure paradise. Just know winter's physical side, especially as mobility changes.

## 2. The tax picture isn't retiree-friendly

Minnesota's top income tax rate is **9.85%**, one of the highest in the country. Most retirees won't hit the top bracket, but the middle brackets are still higher than many states people compare.

Minnesota also **taxes Social Security**, with conditions. For the tax year discussed in the video, benefits were fully exempt from state tax for married couples with income of **$108,320 or less** and single filers with **$84,490 or less**. Above those lines, the exemption phases out quickly. Check current thresholds with the Minnesota Department of Revenue, as they're adjusted each year.

Property taxes rise too: Minneapolis's city levy was expected to go up close to 7%, roughly $170 more a year on a median-value home. On a fixed income, that adds up.

**The silver lining:** much of the Minneapolis market has shifted toward buyers, with downtown West prices down nearly 10% year over year, so your buying power is stronger than it's been in years.

## 3. The city doesn't hand you its appeal; you have to go find it

Your first few months can feel like a mismatch, and some people tap out before they find the good stuff. There's a lot to find:

- **The Guthrie Theater** (with reduced rates for seniors) and the **Minnesota Orchestra**, consistently ranked among the best in the country.
- A restaurant scene that competes with cities twice our size.
- Lakes everywhere, so you're never far from a kayak or a sunset on a dock.
- **MSP Airport**, ranked the #1 mega airport in North America for traveler satisfaction, a Delta hub with nonstop flights to about 165 destinations. When February gets to you, two weeks somewhere warm is easy to book.

Building friendships from scratch in retirement takes real effort and time. If you want built-in community, a **55+ community** can do a lot of that work for you; retirees often tell me they've made some of their best friendships in those settings.

## 4. Health care: where Minnesota dominates

For retirees, health care access may be the single most important factor, because a medical crisis somewhere with a weak health system can upend everything. Here, Minnesota shines:

- **Mayo Clinic** in Rochester has been named the #1 hospital in the world for the eighth straight year.
- Minnesota's health care system ranks consistently in the **top five** nationally.
- **Abbott Northwestern** in the Twin Cities ranks in the global top 100 for cardiac care and neurosurgery, and **M Health Fairview** ranks among the top regional hospitals.

**What to know before you arrive:** several Medicare plan providers, including UCare and Humana, are scaling back or ending some Minnesota plans heading into 2026, so review your coverage options. On the plus side, Medicare Part D now caps yearly out-of-pocket prescription costs, at **$2,000 in 2025 and $2,100 in 2026**.

For many retirees, paying more in state taxes for access to Mayo and Abbott Northwestern is a trade-off worth making.

## 5. The day-to-day stuff nobody mentions

- **Mosquitoes.** The summer of 2025 set a record for West Nile virus in the Twin Cities (42 cases), with mosquitoes at their highest levels since 2020 after a wet summer. Metropolitan Mosquito Control does excellent work, but plan for your evenings outside.
- **Allergy season** in spring and fall hits harder than people expect.
- **You'll probably need a car.** Transit isn't there yet for most neighborhoods. The Green Line Extension (14.5 miles of new light rail) is expected to open in 2027. The upside: commutes are manageable compared with Austin or Atlanta. If you're driving more than 40 minutes anywhere in the Twin Cities, that's a location choice.
- **Minnesota doesn't market itself to retirees like Florida does.** There aren't billboards for retirement communities at every exit. The 55+ communities here are high quality and less crowded, and you live in a real city with people of every age. But if you want the full Sun Belt retirement ecosystem, Minneapolis may feel like the wrong fit.

## The bottom line

Minneapolis isn't right for every retiree. It's absolutely right for retirees who go in prepared: who've done their financial homework, understand the trade-offs and are willing to engage with a city that gives back as much as you put in.
