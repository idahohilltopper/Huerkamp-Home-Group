**Thinking about selling in the Twin Cities? You have a decision to make: sell this fall, or wait for spring. Waiting can feel safer, but a few extra months could cost you more than you gain. Here are the numbers to compare before you pick your listing month.**

Figures are as of my video above (October 2026).

## You get to choose when you sell

Spring feels like the obvious choice: green lawns, buyers out, new listings everywhere. But it isn't automatically best for you. Start with your break-even point this fall, then compare it with selling in April. Your rate, your tax notice and your timeline all matter.

## Your local market matters more than headlines

The market isn't cooling everywhere. In **Dakota County**, homes were selling in about **31 days** at **99.9%** of list price, with sales up **11%**. In the city of **Minneapolis**, homes took **53 days**, and the slowdown is mostly in the city, especially condos. That's almost three more weeks of payments, insurance and taxes, and three more weeks of showings with no guaranteed offer. (More in [buyer's market or seller's market?](/blog/minneapolis-buyers-market-or-sellers-market).)

Early fall brings more listings than an average week; spring brings even more.

## Rates shrink the buyer pool

Mortgage rates were back above **7%**. Some buyers who qualified for your home in spring don't now, and others qualify for less, so your buyer pool shrinks even though your house hasn't changed. The Fed meets October 27-28 and December 8-9, and you can plan around those dates but not control them. Fannie Mae expects rates near **6.7% through 2027**, so the forecast gives little reason to wait for much lower rates. Meanwhile your payment, insurance and rising taxes keep running.

## Does the election matter?

We hear it every cycle. Home sales dip from October to November every year, and Bright MLS's analysis of national data found the dip looks about the same in presidential election years. Fall price growth was about **4.4%** in presidential election years versus **4.5%** in other years. 2026 is a midterm year, so it's not a direct match, but the takeaway holds: the fall dip is seasonal. Waiting just because of November may not help.

## Know your property tax number before you price

**Proposed property tax notices arrive between November 10 and 24.** Most sellers barely glance at theirs, but buyers read them closely and factor them into their monthly payment. A higher tax bill means a higher payment even at the same price, which can change what a buyer offers. Ask your agent for the estimate; it takes minutes and can reshape the pricing conversation.

## Winter vs. spring

**Winter** has fewer buyers, but also fewer competing listings, and the buyers who are out often have a firm deadline (a new job or a relocation) and move faster. A home that needs updates can stand out more with fewer homes to compare it to.

**Spring** brings the most buyers and the best curb appeal, but also the most competition, and national forecasts show price growth slowing into next year. Spring suits homes that show best in good weather (big yards, natural light, curb appeal) and sellers without a firm deadline.

## Three questions to decide

1. **How firm is your timeline?** If a job, a move or a purchase sets the date, that decides most of it.
2. **How does your home compare with nearby listings** in size, condition and presentation?
3. **What's your net now versus spring, including holding costs?** If holding your home costs, say, $3,000 a month, five months of waiting is about $15,000, and your spring price has to cover that just to break even.

**My take:** if your timeline is firm, list now and don't wait on a maybe. If your home needs updates, winter can help you avoid a wave of polished spring listings. If your home shines in good weather and you have time, spring may fit. Two sellers on the same block can choose differently and both be right.
