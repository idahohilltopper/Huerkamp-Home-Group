**Every buyer wants to jump straight to touring houses, and so do I. But when you find the perfect house, will you be ready to act? Here are the steps to buying a home in Minnesota (and most of the country), in order.**

## 1. Get pre-approved

Before anything else, get **pre-approved** with a lender. We're happy to refer you to lenders we trust, and you can also check with your own bank or credit union. Getting more than one opinion is smart. Mortgage credit checks within a short shopping window are generally treated as a single inquiry, so comparing lenders has very little effect on your score. Whoever you choose, read their online reviews. Our [affordability calculator](/calculators/affordability) gives you a starting point.

## 2. Sit down with your agent

Once you have a pre-approval letter, meet with the agent you've chosen. Buying a home isn't as simple as it looks; there are a lot of steps, and a good agent makes them as easy and painless as possible. This meeting sets expectations on both sides. Today, you'll also sign a **written buyer agreement** before touring homes, which spells out what your agent does and how they're paid. Ask questions until it makes sense.

## 3. Narrow your area and start touring

The greater Minneapolis area is huge, from Faribault to Blaine and Waconia to Hudson, with an enormous number of homes. Narrow it down as much as you can. For most of our clients, it comes down to **where they work** (especially if they go into an office) and how far they're willing to commute, especially when it's snowing. Our [commute tool](/commute) and [home search](/home-search) can help.

## 4. Write a strong offer

A great offer depends on the home, how long it's been on the market and how many other offers there are. Price is only one piece; terms, timing and contingencies can make your offer stand out. See [questions to ask before making an offer](/blog/2025/3/24/questions-to-ask-before-making-an-offer-on-a-house).

## 5. Offer accepted: financing, appraisal and inspection

Once your offer is accepted:

- **Financing:** your purchase agreement goes to your lender, who orders the **appraisal** and keeps the loan moving.
- **Inspection:** a home inspection, ideally showing nothing major. If it does find issues, that's what the inspection period is for. We may be able to negotiate repairs or credits with the seller; if not, you can typically cancel and keep looking.

Our goal is always to be **advisors, teachers and advocates**, not pushy salespeople. This is usually the biggest financial decision of your life. More on inspections in [scary Minnesota home inspection findings](/blog/scary-minnesota-home-inspection-findings).

## 6. Close

Closings usually happen at a **title company**. The title companies we work with will come to your home or office if needed, but closing at their office is often easiest, because if anything in the paperwork needs a quick fix, they can handle it on the spot.

## There's more to each step

This is a simplified version; there's a lot more to each step. That's why we encourage every buyer to sit down with a real estate professional first.
