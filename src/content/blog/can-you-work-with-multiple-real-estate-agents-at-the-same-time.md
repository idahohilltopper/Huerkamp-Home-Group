**Yes, you can. But *should you*?**

When you're buying a home, it can be tempting to think that working with several real estate agents will give you a huge advantage. More agents could mean more listings, more connections, and more chances of finding the right property, right?

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="/blog/can-you-work-with-multiple-real-estate-agents-at-the-same-time/1.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="800" height="1199" /></figure>

Well, not necessarily.

  
While it's technically legal to work with multiple agents in certain situations, it’s not always ethical or effective. In most situations, choosing one agent you trust and working closely with them is the better approach. And depending on any agreement you've signed, you may already have obligations that limit your ability to work with another agent.

  
In this blog, we’ve detailed what buyers need to know about working with more than one real estate agent, and why it isn’t usually as clever a strategy as you might have thought.

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="/blog/can-you-work-with-multiple-real-estate-agents-at-the-same-time/2.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="800" height="219" /></figure>

Let's say Agent A sends you a listing on Monday. You decide to attend an open house over the weekend and mention the property to Agent B. Then you ask Agent B to arrange a second showing and help you prepare an offer. Now two agents have been involved with the same property, and expectations about representation can quickly become unclear.

A single point of contact keeps the process much simpler. Your agent knows which homes you've toured, what you've liked or disliked, what you've already discussed, and where you are in the decision-making process.

A buyer-broker agreement is a contract you need to sign in the early stages of your relationship with your agent. While agreements vary between brokerages, they generally state that you and the agent both agree to an *exclusive* working arrangement for a period of time, typically six months.

  
Once you sign this contract, working with another agent may conflict with the terms you agreed to, and there could be questions about compensation depending on the circumstances. Read this document carefully before involving another agent. Pay attention to the duration of the agreement, the geographic area or properties it covers, how compensation works, and the process for ending the relationship.

One of the biggest misconceptions first-time home buyers have is that every agent has a completely different collection of homes available to buyers. Yes, agents may hear about opportunities through their professional networks, but working with two or more agents doesn't automatically give you more homes to choose from.

The bigger difference is often the quality of the service you receive: how well an agent understands your priorities, how quickly they respond when a suitable property appears, and how effectively they help you evaluate it.

A good buyer-agent relationship gets more useful over time. Suppose you initially tell your agent you want a three-bedroom home with a large backyard. After touring several properties together, your agent realizes that what matters most to you isn't actually the size of the yard. You care more about having a quiet home office, a short commute, and a kitchen that doesn't require an immediate renovation.

That information helps your agent refine the search. But if you're splitting your search among several agents, each one may only see part of the picture. You may spend more time explaining the same preferences repeatedly and touring properties that aren't a good fit, wasting both of your time and resources.

If an agent believes they're representing you while you're simultaneously asking several other agents to do the same work, it can create misunderstandings about the relationship instead of making it better over time.

Would you want to discuss your home budget and preferences repeatedly to three different people? Adding several agents to the process can make it harder to remember who you've talked to and who knows what. 

Since buying a home involves numerous showing appointments, lender conversations, disclosures, inspections, deadlines, negotiations, and plenty of questions, having multiple agents by your side makes the process even more stressful and complicated.

### **When Working With More Than One Agent Can Make Sense**

Of course, there are situations where having multiple agents may be reasonable. If you're searching for a home in two distinct geographic markets that require different local expertise, you can rationalize working with two different agents. You could also be considering very different property categories that call for specialized knowledge. In that case, working with an experienced local agent in each market could be perfectly practical. 

  
The key here is transparency and communication. Tell each agent that you're working with another professional, explain why, and make sure the arrangement doesn't conflict with anything you've signed.

### **What If You're Unhappy With Your Current Agent?**

You aren't required to keep working with someone simply because they're the first agent you contacted. But before moving on, determine what kind of relationship you've established and whether you've signed an agreement.

If the problem is communication, availability, or a misunderstanding about your priorities, talk to your agent first. A straightforward conversation may solve it. But should you decide the relationship isn't working, and it's time to break up with your agent, review your buyer agreement first to understand the process for ending it. Make sure you ask the agent or their brokerage about your options before beginning a new relationship with another agent.

Once the existing arrangement has been properly resolved, that’s the time you can look for or work with an agent who is a better fit.

### **One Good and Knowledgeable Agent Is Usually More Valuable Than Several**

Working with multiple real estate agents might sound like a way to widen your home search and get quicker results, but more agents don't automatically mean more opportunities.

Because the stronger strategy is to find one local, knowledgeable, and responsive agent who understands the local market you're looking into and recognizes your preferences and priorities. That’s when you can start building a productive working relationship with them towards finding your dream home.
