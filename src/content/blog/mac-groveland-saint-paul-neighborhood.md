**Highland Park gets the press, and Summit Hill gets the attention. But Macalester-Groveland, "Mac-Groveland," quietly wins on livability. Here are the reasons this Saint Paul neighborhood keeps drawing buyers who started out looking somewhere else.**

Prices are as of my video above (spring 2026).

## Where it is

Mac-Groveland sits in southwest Saint Paul, with the Mississippi River bluffs on one side and Summit Avenue to the northeast. It's home to about **19,700 people**: big enough to feel like a real community, not so big it feels busy. Tree-lined streets and a calm feel are hard to find this close to a major city.

It's a true **15-minute neighborhood**: groceries, coffee, restaurants and river walks are all within reach without a car.

## Two colleges and a new arena

Most Twin Cities neighborhoods have no colleges. Mac-Groveland has two inside its borders: **Macalester College** and the **University of St. Thomas**. That brings events, sports and steady activity to the streets.

St. Thomas moved up to **Division I** in basketball and hockey, and the **Lee & Penny Anderson Arena** opened in October 2025, seating about 4,000 for hockey and 5,500 for basketball. Its classic stone design fits the campus.

## Tangletown

Most of Saint Paul is a straight grid. **Tangletown** isn't. Part of the late-1800s Macalester Park addition, whose land sales helped fund the college, its streets curve and wind with the land. Huge trees line every block, and it almost feels European. Most homes were built **between 1910 and 1940**: **craftsman bungalows, American foursquares and Tudors**, each a little different. That kind of character can't be recreated with new construction, and it shows up in pricing.

## Grand Avenue

Step outside and **Grand Avenue** is right there, one of the most walkable, active corridors in the city. **Cafe Latte** has been a staple for years (baked goods, soups and salads). When the **Tavern on Grand** closed in 2024 after 35 years, **W.A. Russell's** opened in the space with an upscale 1920s-style interior, and it kept the famous walleye on the menu. Trader Joe's and Kowalski's are close by.

Every year, **Grand Old Day** draws over **200,000 people** for a parade, live music and more than 150 vendors.

## What homes cost

Inventory is limited, but buyers have more room than a few years ago. Based on early 2026 data:

- **Median:** mid-$400,000s
- **Entry-level:** low $300,000s
- **Larger historic homes:** high $700,000s to around $1 million, depending on size and location
- Homes typically sell in **two to four weeks**, and turnover is low.

The housing mix includes single-family homes, condos and student rentals near campus. Older homes deserve a careful inspection; see [red flags in old homes](/blog/old-minneapolis-homes-red-flags).

## Location

- **Downtown Saint Paul:** 10 to 15 minutes
- **Downtown Minneapolis:** 15 to 20 minutes
- **MSP Airport:** about 10 to 15 minutes

**Snelling Avenue** runs north-south through the neighborhood, and the **Metro A Line** bus connects to both the Green and Blue light-rail lines. To the west, **Mississippi River Boulevard** has trails, green space and walking paths, one of the city's best hidden lifestyle features.
