**One of these clubs you literally can't buy your way into; you need at least six sponsors just to be considered. Here are my top five golf course communities in the Twin Cities, from a public course with $1,000 membership to a two-time Ryder Cup host.**

Prices and membership costs are as cited in my video above (spring 2026). Clubs change their fees, so confirm current terms directly.

## 5. TPC Twin Cities, Blaine

The PGA Tour owns courses around the country, and this is its only one in Minnesota. The course, designed by **Arnold Palmer** with Tom Lehman as player consultant, covers about 235 acres inside the **Deacon's Walk** community and hosts the **3M Open** every year. You're living next to a real PGA Tour stop.

Because the PGA Tour owns it, it covers most major course projects, so there are **no capital assessments** for members. It recently added pickleball courts and updated dining. The feel is relaxed: smart casual, active and modern.

**Homes:** single-family homes and townhomes, many with fairway or pond views, mostly from the **mid-$500,000s to just under $1 million**. It's a strong entry point into the golf lifestyle. See [Blaine](/communities/blaine).

## 4. Edinburgh USA, Brooklyn Park

Edinburgh is a **public** course that doesn't feel public, with a Scottish-themed clubhouse and a beautifully done layout. Its roughly **40,000-square-foot putting green** connecting holes 9 and 18 is billed as the largest in North America, and the island-fairway 17th is the hole everyone remembers.

**Membership:** the St. Andrews Club has a **$1,000** one-time fee and annual dues of about **$675 to $1,075**.

**Homes:** mostly two-story and executive ramblers built in the 1990s, often over 3,500 square feet, typically **low $400,000s to mid-$500,000s**. You get a lot of space for the money. See [Brooklyn Park](/communities/brooklyn-park).

## 3. Bearpath, Eden Prairie

Bearpath is a **gated** community with a 24-hour guarded entrance: quiet, private and elevated. The course is a **Jack Nicklaus Signature** design, the only one in the Upper Midwest, and it's consistently in excellent condition. Membership is optional for residents, but most join; amenities include a pool, tennis and a fitness center.

**Membership:** about **$45,000** to join a full golf membership, with dues around **$1,165 a month** plus a food and beverage minimum.

**Homes:** custom homes, mostly built from the '90s to mid-2000s, from about **$1 million to $2.5 million**, some over $3 million, with HOA fees around **$350 to $750 a month** covering security and management. I've sold several homes here, and in my opinion it's one of the best values in the Twin Cities for what you get. See [Eden Prairie](/communities/eden-prairie).

## 2. Interlachen, Edina

At **Interlachen Country Club**, money alone doesn't get you in. Membership is **invitation only**, and you need at least six sponsors to be considered. The course, redesigned by **Donald Ross**, dates to the early 1900s; **Bobby Jones** won the U.S. Open here in 1930 on his way to the Grand Slam, and it's widely considered one of Minnesota's top courses.

**Membership:** initiation is typically **$60,000 or more**, with dues around **$700 a month** plus a food and beverage minimum.

**Homes:** nearby homes start around **$1.5 million** and can top **$6 million**. Edina was planning a property tax levy increase of almost 8% for 2026, so factor in ongoing costs. You're about 8 miles from downtown Minneapolis, a 15- to 20-minute drive. Elite golf and location, but access isn't guaranteed. See [Edina](/communities/edina).

## 1. Hazeltine, Chaska

Hazeltine takes the top spot for two reasons: **world-class golf** and **real inventory**. It was built to host majors and has hosted the U.S. Open, the PGA Championship and the Ryder Cup. It's set to host the **2026 KPMG Women's PGA** and the **2029 Ryder Cup**, making it the first U.S. course to host a second Ryder Cup.

Unlike Interlachen or Bearpath, where turnover is low, the Hazeltine area had about **50 active listings** in March 2026, with inventory up about 9% year over year. You'll find townhomes, move-up homes and new construction. Builders like **D.R. Horton** and **Robert Thomas** offered new homes starting in the low $600,000s just outside the main community, while homes within Hazeltine typically run from the **high $800,000s to $3 million+**.

[Chaska](/communities/chaska) has more of a small-town feel, with the **Minnesota Landscape Arboretum** nearby and excellent trails. Minneapolis is about 25 miles away, a 35- to 45-minute drive.
