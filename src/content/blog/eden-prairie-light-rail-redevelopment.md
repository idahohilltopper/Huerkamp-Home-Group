**The Eden Prairie you know is changing. More than $3 billion is being invested to reinvent the city: light rail opens in 2027, the mall is being reimagined, and thousands of new homes are going up near the stations. Here's what's happening and why some buyers are positioning themselves now.**

Figures are as of my video above (March 2026).

## The big unlock: light rail in 2027

The **Green Line Extension** (Southwest light rail) is set to open in 2027. When I made the video it was about 95% done, with tracks in, all 16 stations finished or close to it, and testing underway since October 2025. Delays pushed the cost to about **$3 billion**, up from an early estimate of $1.25 billion.

Once it opens, you can ride from Eden Prairie to downtown Minneapolis in about **35 minutes**, with no transfers and no 494 or Highway 5 traffic, and the line continues on to Saint Paul. Eden Prairie gets four stations: **SouthWest Station** (end of the line), **Town Center**, **Golden Triangle** and **City West**.

More than **$3 billion** in development, including over **8,000 housing units**, is already built, under construction or planned along the line. When rail reaches a built-out suburb, it changes how the land around each station is used and what it's worth. Eden Prairie has always been car-focused; after 2027, it's connected to the region.

## Project 1: Reimagining Eden Prairie Center

The old-school mall model doesn't work the way it used to. The mall's taxable value peaked around $130 million and has fallen to about $90 million. In early talks between the city and owner MetLife (March 2025), the focus was the north end, where JCPenney closed in August 2024, plus its surface parking. Concepts include:

- **750 apartments**
- A large hotel and event space
- Offices and ground-floor retail
- Underground parking to make it walkable

Nothing is formally approved; it's conceptual. In June 2025, the state approved a special provision letting the city create a new **tax increment financing (TIF)** district for the project, but that authority runs out on **December 31** of this year, so it has to move. The site is less than a block from Anderson Lakes Regional Park and about three blocks from the Town Center station. Southdale and Ridgedale have followed a similar playbook.

## Project 2: Homes going up by the stations

Developers aren't waiting for 2027:

- **Fox & Grouse** near Golden Triangle Station: phase one has more than 200 units; phase two (about $55 million) broke ground in August 2025 with almost 200 more.
- **City West Apartments**, on the old Department 56 site: almost 200 units due around 2027, with more than $400,000 in TIF for a trail straight to the station.
- **Elevate at SouthWest Station** (2019): over 200 apartments above about 13,000 square feet of shops and restaurants, Eden Prairie's first true transit-oriented project.

That's more than 800 homes near the light rail from those three alone. Eden Prairie adopted transit-focused rules back in 2016, and the city is now building inward instead of outward.

## Project 3: The Golden Triangle's makeover

The **Golden Triangle** is about 1,000 acres between major highways, with around 25,000 jobs before the pandemic. Many buildings date from the 1970s and '80s, and now the area is being reinvested in:

- **Eldevron**, a biotech company, bought almost 350,000 square feet and is adding about 100,000 more, with plans for roughly **500 high-tech jobs**.
- The former **American Family Insurance** site is becoming almost 890,000 square feet of modern flex industrial space.
- **UnitedHealth Group** is moving its headquarters to the Optum campus here.
- The 60-acre former **Danfoss** site, next to SouthWest Station, was re-guided in June 2025 for almost **2,000 homes**, nearly 100,000 square feet of retail and over 40,000 square feet of offices.

The city is putting money in too: about $1 million for West 70th Street, almost $1.5 million for Shady Oak Road and trails, over $5 million for trails and sidewalks around the stations, and more than $26 million for roads and intersections.

## Why this transformation is different

[Eden Prairie](/communities/eden-prairie) is nearly built out, with almost no farmland left, so it can't sprawl. Every acre becomes more valuable, and the projects feed each other: the rail makes housing valuable, housing makes the job center attractive, and jobs feed demand back into housing. And the city planned this years ago rather than reacting.

## What stays the same

- About **90% of residents** rate city services good to excellent.
- The property tax **levy has averaged about 4%** a year over the last decade, among the lowest for similar-size Minnesota cities. The 2026 levy is up almost 6%, below the roughly 8.5% average for comparable cities, though the city portion for a median-value home rose about 8%.
- More than **170 miles of trails**, plus Anderson Lakes and Purgatory Creek.

## The market

Eden Prairie was still a seller's market: a median from the mid-$400,000s to low $500,000s, about 40 days on market, roughly two months of inventory, and about 30% of homes selling above list. Lending standards are far tighter than before 2008, and most 2026 forecasts called for slower but steady growth of about 2%.

## What it means for buyers

Eden Prairie is shifting from a car-first suburb to a more connected, walkable one, with more apartments and townhomes near the stations. Public and private money this size is a good sign for long-term value, but factor in the tax increases.
