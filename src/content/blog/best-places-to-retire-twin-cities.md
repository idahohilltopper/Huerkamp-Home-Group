**Where you settle for retirement around Minneapolis can make a difference of thousands of dollars every year. After 25 years and more than 3,700 home sales, here's where people like to settle for the long haul, and the one trade-off that trips up almost everyone.**

Prices are as of my video above (summer 2026).

## Five things to look for

1. **Single-level living.** Most people get tired of stairs and want everything on one floor.
2. **Low maintenance.** Who handles the lawn and the snow? That can be easier than you think.
3. **Health care.** A place 30 miles out feels fine in October. In a February snowstorm, that drive to the hospital feels a lot longer.
4. **Getting around.** Walkability is a bonus, and rides from family or Uber fill the gaps.
5. **Lock-and-leave.** Many Minnesotans head south for the winter and want a home they can leave for months.

Get those right and the search gets a lot easier.

## Resort-style communities

The best resort-style 55+ communities feel like a cruise ship without the water: activities, dining, friends and amenities on campus.

- **Bellwether by Del Webb, [Corcoran](/communities/corcoran).** A big clubhouse, indoor and outdoor pools, pickleball, bocce, walking trails, a fitness center and a full-time lifestyle director. All homes are single-level, the community is gated, and lawn care and snow removal are handled. It's about 20 to 30 minutes from downtown Minneapolis. Homes start in the **mid-$400,000s**, and it's the only new-construction Del Webb community still selling in the Twin Cities. More in [Corcoran's growth](/blog/corcoran-mn-growth).
- **Del Webb in [Chaska](/communities/chaska).** It filled up fast, so it's now mostly resale. A 24-hour emergency room is minutes away at **Ridgeview's Two Twelve Medical Center**, and both downtowns are about 30 minutes away.
- **Four Seasons at Rush Creek, [Maple Grove](/communities/maple-grove).** The biggest clubhouse of the group: indoor and outdoor pools, saunas, a learning center, craft hall, spa and card room. It's about 20 minutes from downtown, next to Arbor Lakes and a hospital. These are resale homes, typically **upper $400,000s to mid-$500,000s**, and inventory is tight.

## A pick that isn't a house: Becketwood

**Becketwood** is an active-adult **co-op** on 12 wooded acres on the Mississippi River bluffs in [Minneapolis](/communities/minneapolis), about 15 minutes from both downtowns, with a nearby bus that connects to light rail. You buy a share of the cooperative, not a deed to a unit, so financing works differently. There's a waiting list (with a refundable deposit). For city living without a yard, it's hard to beat.

## Quiet, maintenance-free condos

- **Hidden Pathways, [White Bear Lake](/communities/white-bear-lake).** A small maintenance-free condo community with the exterior handled and underground parking, a solid lock-and-leave option.
- **Bartz Crossing, [Rosemount](/communities/rosemount).** A 55+ condo community with underground parking, where the monthly fee includes gas, heat, internet and cable.

Always confirm the rules and fees before you buy. Our [HOA guide](/blog/2023/8/30/what-home-buyers-should-know-about-hoas) covers what to ask.

## Towns that make retirement easy on their own

No age rule, no membership, no clubhouse:

- **[Wayzata](/communities/wayzata):** the walkable lake pick on Lake Minnetonka, with shops, restaurants, coffee and lake views. Prices are high; you're paying for the lake-town lifestyle.
- **[Edina](/communities/edina):** shopping, dining, parks and golf near the center of the metro, including Southdale, the first indoor mall in the country. **Fairview Southdale Hospital** is inside the city, and there's a 55+ apartment option near Southdale.
- **[Minnetonka](/communities/minnetonka):** quiet, full of lakes and green space, about 20 minutes from downtown. Many homes are older, so check carefully for true single-level living.
- **Maple Grove:** Arbor Lakes plus active-adult housing, with **Maple Grove Hospital** (North Memorial Health) in town.
- **White Bear Lake:** a walkable downtown and an easy pace, with lake homes usually priced below Lake Minnetonka and Saint Paul health care close by.

## The big trade-off: community or do-it-yourself?

In a **55+ community**, one monthly fee can cover lawn care, snow removal, amenities and a social calendar. With a **single-level home and no association**, you have full control, but you pay for lawn and snow yourself. Through a Minnesota winter, that's **a few thousand dollars a year**, and adding a home-watch service while you're away can push it toward **$5,000**.

Once you add everything up, both can cost about the same, and the HOA fee is sometimes a slightly better deal. There's no single right answer. The people who regret their choice usually have one thing in common: they never ran the numbers for both.

## Choose in four questions

1. **Community or DIY?** That narrows the field right away.
2. **Heading south for winter?** Prioritize lock-and-leave. A heated garage is worth a lot in February.
3. **Is staying social the top priority?** Look hard at the resort communities.
4. **Want city access and less lawn?** Look at the urban and co-op picks like Becketwood.

Then **visit before you commit**. No listing photo can show how a place feels.
