If you've driven Excelsior between France and Monterey in the last two weeks, you already know something changed. A candy case appeared in the old Honey & Rye retail bay. A butcher paper sign taped inside a window on Minnetonka Boulevard now reads like a real menu. And on Wednesday nights, the amphitheater lot at Wolfe Park is full again by 6:45.

The short version for residents: the food corridor closest to your house got two locally owned openings in a single season, and the free concert series that anchors your summer has a soft deadline this month. The longer version is more interesting, because it runs against the chain-restaurant story that's about to unfold a mile north on Wayzata Boulevard.

## Two openings on the same walk

The Candy Shop opened at 4615 Excelsior Boulevard on August 1. It took over the retail bay that used to belong to Honey & Rye's Bakehouse, and it's run by Anne Andrus, who built her name on the bakery side of that space. The pitch is narrow and specific: imported Swedish candy sold pick-and-mix, housemade confections, a modern take on Swedish princess cake, and dirty sodas. If you have a kid who has been asking about the Scandinavian candy trend for a year, this is now a twelve-minute walk from Wolfe Park.

About a mile west, at 6318 Minnetonka Boulevard, Sweet Lou's Craft Sausage & Butchery moved into the former Nelson's Meats, Bakery and Deli space this spring. Louisa Farhat and Tim Dubay ran the operation as a farmers market stand, retail-partner supplier, and brewery food truck for years before buying the Nelson's building outright. The brick-and-mortar sells craft sausages, burgers, brats, Italian meatballs, and tenderloin roasts from a butcher counter, with the food-truck menu attached. The Yelp review corner that tracks this kind of thing already describes it in old-school neighborhood-deli terms.

Neither of these is a chain. Neither raised out-of-state capital. Both took over a well-known previous tenant's space rather than building new. That pattern matters, and it's worth holding onto for the next section.

## The concert series has an expiration date

The St. Louis Park Rotary Summer Concert Series runs Wednesdays at Veterans Memorial Amphitheater, 3700 Monterey Drive, and the [City of St. Louis Park](https://www.stlouisparkmn.gov/our-city/summer-concerts) hosts the schedule. Every performance is free.

Two things about the 2026 run are worth knowing if you live here:

1. The series ends August 12. That is the last Wednesday. The [full 2026 calendar](https://www.familyfuntwincities.com/events/st-louis-park-summer-concert-series-free/) runs June 17 through August 12, and there is no makeup week.
2. Programming isn't only the Wednesday evening slot. The [Sun Sailor's preview](https://www.hometownsource.com/sun_sailor/free/st-louis-park-summer-performance-series-returning-for-2026/article_81cad2db-b93a-4c4e-81b4-f536ce67b2e6.html) of the series lists an Open Mic Night on Thursdays and a Kids Summer Concert Series on Wednesday mornings around 10 a.m., which is the schedule most parents of small kids don't know about until year three of living here.

The amphitheater is directly across Monterey from the Rec Center Aquatic Park, which means you can build a Wednesday around the pool closing time and the concert start. That geography is why Parktacular, back in June, worked so well as a four-day festival on the same footprint.

## The chain cluster to the north, and what it doesn't change

If you follow city council agendas, you already know about the four-restaurant development proposed for 5775 Wayzata Boulevard. The [BringMeTheNews coverage](https://bringmethenews.com/minnesota-lifestyle/shake-shack-among-four-new-restaurants-planned-for-st-louis-park) laid out the tenant list: Shake Shack, Sweetgreen, Chipotle, and Snooze an A.M. Eatery, the Denver-based brunch chain making its Minnesota debut. Two single-story buildings, patio seating, a Chipotlane drive-up window. The city council recommended approval and the project has been moving through the pipeline since.

That cluster will draw traffic off I-394 and pull weekday lunch demand out of the West End. It will not, on its own, change how residents south of Highway 7 spend a Saturday. Those trips still route down Excelsior and Minnetonka Boulevards, and this year the anchors on that route are locally owned rather than franchised. The Candy Shop, Sweet Lou's, Copperwing Distillery's cocktail room, Bunny's Bar & Grill for the older residents' standard, and Punch Pizza at the West End for the family default all sit inside a fifteen-minute drive from most of the city.

> The chain cluster is a highway play. The Excelsior spine is a neighborhood play. Both can be true in the same city in the same year, and residents who understand the split get to use both.

## One August evening, mapped

Here is what a Wednesday night between now and August 12 can look like without leaving a two-mile radius:

- **5:00 p.m.** Aquatic Park at the Rec Center, 3700 Monterey Drive, if you have kids or the temperature warrants it.
- **6:00 p.m.** Walk or drive to Sweet Lou's on Minnetonka Boulevard for sausages and brats to eat in the car or on a bench. Order at the counter.
- **6:45 p.m.** Veterans Memorial Amphitheater for the Rotary concert. Bring a blanket. Parking fills the Rec Center lot first, then Excelsior & Grand ramps.
- **8:30 p.m.** The Candy Shop at 4615 Excelsior on the way home for princess cake or a dirty soda before it closes.

If you are hosting out-of-town family in August, that route is the one to run. It reads as a curated neighborhood rather than a strip of national tenants, which is the exact impression the West End cluster is not designed to produce.

## What this means for people who own here

If you're the current homeowner who reads market posts because you're curious about your own block, the useful takeaway isn't a price. It's a supply signal. When independently owned food and retail take over previous local tenants' spaces on the same corridor in the same year, it usually means two things: rents at those addresses are still workable for owner-operators, and the buyer traffic on that corridor is dependable enough that operators are willing to sign leases without a national brand's cushion.

That is a healthier sign for the neighborhood around Wolfe Park than the Wayzata Boulevard cluster is, even though the chain project generates the bigger headlines. Buyers touring in this area in the fall will notice the Excelsior spine before they notice the highway frontage. Sellers on the streets between France and Monterey will be selling into that same first impression.
