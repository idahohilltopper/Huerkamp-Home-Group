**Most people think of Minneapolis as a winter city. Trust me, summer is when this place shows off. Here's what was new for summer 2026: restaurants, major brands, concerts, events, and why the housing market gave buyers a window we hadn't seen in years.**

## New restaurants

Minneapolis has quietly become one of the best food cities in the country, and most people outside Minnesota still don't know it.

- **Dahlia** in Northeast: a long-awaited destination bakery with pastries, croissants, savory food and great coffee, with Minnesota Nice Cream sharing the space at night.
- **Maison Rose**, from the team behind Patisserie 46 and Rose Street Patisserie: a big café, retail shop and production kitchen with French pastries, cakes and bread, plus a bread-lab apprenticeship program.
- **Indigenous barbecue from the Owamni team**: chef Sean Sherman's team is opening a casual, counter-service spot in the old Seward Co-op Creamery building on Franklin Avenue, with bison brisket, smoked game, wild rice and braised greens.
- **Oh No Hawaiian Plates** is expanding from United Noodles to a second spot in the North Loop, with kalbi ribs, whole fried snapper, Filipino empanadas and a tiki bar.
- **Saint Paul's Cathedral Hill** has a new spot for classic Laotian cooking in the former Revival space, after years of pop-ups.
- **LaSalle Plaza**: the old Rock Bottom Brewery space downtown is becoming a **30,000-square-foot food hall** with 16 restaurant stalls, two bars, a self-pour beer wall, a kids' play area, pickleball and shuffleboard.
- **Stock & Bond** steakhouse in the Westin, inside the historic Farmers & Mechanics Savings Bank, with 30-foot ceilings, art deco details and 300-plus bourbons and whiskeys.

## New stores

Big brands don't open first stores in a state unless it has earned it:

- **Mango**, the Spanish fashion brand, is opening two Minnesota stores, including one at the Mall of America.
- **Skims** is opening its first Minnesota store at the Mall of America.
- **Alo Yoga** is opening a 6,000-square-foot Mall of America store, its second in the Twin Cities after the Galleria in Edina.
- **Inkwell Booksellers** is opening on the ground floor of the Hinssen Apartments, with more than 9,000 books, local goods and a café serving Wesley Andrews coffee and Rose Street pastries.
- **K-Pop Nara** brings albums, light sticks and collectibles to the Mall of America.

## Hotels

- **Four Seasons Minneapolis**, blocks from the river, with a spa, a fourth-floor outdoor pool with cabanas, a restaurant by award-winning local chef Gavin Kaysen, and floor-to-ceiling skyline views.
- **Sheraton Minneapolis Downtown**, after a **$40 million renovation**: 321 redesigned rooms, 19 event spaces, Esther's Table restaurant and an indoor pool, near the convention center.

And getting here is easy: MSP was ranked the top mega airport three of the last four years.

## A huge concert summer

- **U.S. Bank Stadium:** Bruno Mars (May 13), WWE SummerSlam (August 1, WWE's first stadium event in Minneapolis) and Ed Sheeran (August 15).
- **Target Field:** Noah Kahan (August 5), Tim McGraw (August 23) and My Chemical Romance (August 24).
- **Target Center:** Tame Impala (August 28).
- **Minnesota State Fair Grandstand:** Bonnie Raitt, "Weird Al" Yankovic and Rod Stewart, among 12 nights of music.
- **First Avenue**, where Prince filmed *Purple Rain*, with shows almost every night, plus Rock the Garden, the Twin Cities Jazz Festival, Surly Festival Field and the Armory.

## Summer events

- **Prince Celebration 2026** (June 3 to 7), marking 10 years since Prince's passing, with events at Paisley Park in Chanhassen, Minneapolis and Saint Paul.
- **Special Olympics USA Games** (June 20 to 26) at the University of Minnesota and the National Sports Center in Blaine: more than 3,000 athletes, about 10,000 volunteers and 75,000 fans.
- **Stone Arch Bridge Festival** (June 20 and 21), with 200-plus artists, musicians and food vendors, and it's free.
- **Twin Cities Pride** in late June in Loring Park, one of the largest free Pride celebrations in the country.
- **Basilica Block Party** in early July, with national headliners.
- **Minneapolis Aquatennial** in mid-July: the Torchlight Parade, Target fireworks over the Mississippi and beach parties.
- **Minnesota State Fair**, opening August 27, 12 days and nearly 2 million visitors, with everything on a stick.

## Lakes and trails

You can leave almost any of these events and be at a lake in minutes. Minneapolis has **22 lakes** inside the city. Bde Maka Ska has three beaches, paddleboard and kayak rentals, volleyball courts and even an archery range. Lake Harriet has free bandshell concerts all summer and sailing lessons. There are 200-plus miles of trails, including the car-free **5.5-mile Midtown Greenway**, and Minneapolis ranks among the top two U.S. cities for biking. The **Mill City Farmers Market** runs every Saturday, May through October, with 115-plus vendors by the Stone Arch Bridge.

## The housing market: a buyer's window

Agents were calling it "the great housing reset." After years of skipped inspections and over-asking offers, the pace cooled: about **2.5 to 3 months of supply**, the most in years, homes averaging about **40 days** on market, and Minneapolis sellers getting about **96% to 97%** of list price. The Minneapolis median was in the low to mid-$300,000s and the metro in the upper $300,000s, with low single-digit appreciation and rates around 6%, the lowest since 2022. When rates drop, competition comes back.
