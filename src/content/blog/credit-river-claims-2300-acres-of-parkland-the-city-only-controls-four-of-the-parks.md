Cleary Lake's swimming beach closes for the season on Labor Day, September 7. After that, the sand goes empty until Memorial Day next year, and the loudest recreational asset in Credit River goes quiet for eight months. That much is routine. What's less obvious is that the beach, the nine-hole golf course next to it, and the ten miles of singletrack a few minutes north at Murphy-Hanrehan Park Reserve are not really Credit River's parks at all. They belong to Three Rivers Park District and Scott County, run under a joint powers agreement that predates the city itself.

Credit River's actual parks, the ones the city government decides, funds, and can redesign, are four smaller neighborhood parks scattered through town. In recent summers, the City Council has run a "neighborhood park Community Conversations" series asking residents what those four parks should become.

That's the split worth understanding if you live here: two enormous, professionally managed regional parks you use but don't influence, and four small city parks that are still being written, right now, in meetings most residents never attend.

## How the Parkland Is Managed

Scott County's economic development arm reports that Credit River sits on [over 2,300 acres of park land](https://scottcda.org/communities/credit-river/). It's a real number, and it's the kind of stat that shows up in every relocation blurb about the area. But almost all of that acreage is Cleary Lake Regional Park and Murphy-Hanrehan Park Reserve, both operated by [Three Rivers Park District](https://www.threeriversparks.org/location/murphy-hanrehan-park-reserve) in partnership with the county. Credit River didn't build the golf course at Cleary Lake or the singletrack at Murphy-Hanrehan, and it can't unilaterally change either one.

What the city actually owns and manages is four neighborhood parks, the kind with a swing set and a baseball backstop rather than a groomed cross-country ski loop. Those are the parks the Community Conversations series is built around, according to local reporting from the Sun Thisweek community desk covering the Credit River area. It's a modest-sounding initiative, but it's the only piece of the 2,300-acre figure that residents can actually shape by showing up.

## What's Still Open, and For How Long

None of that changes what's usable right now. The regional side of the ledger still has real mileage left in it before the calendar turns:

- **Cleary Lake Regional Park's beach** stays open daily from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. through Labor Day, then closes until Memorial Day weekend 2027. The park itself also has paddleboat, canoe, and kayak rentals, a nine-hole executive course and driving range, an off-leash pet exercise area, and a paved loop trail around the lake that stays open well past the swimming season.
- **Murphy-Hanrehan Park Reserve**, a few minutes north, holds more than ten miles of singletrack maintained by volunteers from Minnesota Off-Road Cyclists. According to ride-log data compiled by Trailforks, September is actually the single most popular month to ride there, which means the mountain bike trail's best season is only getting started as the beach crowd packs it in for the year.
- **Golf** doesn't have a closing date the way the beach does. Between Legends Golf Club, Heritage Links Golf Course, Cleary Lake's own nine-hole course, and the private Brackett's Crossing Country Club, there are four courses inside or immediately adjacent to Credit River, and play generally continues into fall as long as the ground stays workable.

The practical read: the water closes on a fixed date, the trail keeps going into the month everyone assumes the outdoor season is winding down, and golf just depends on frost. Three different clocks, all inside the same 2,300 acres, running at three different speeds.

## The Meetings Where the Other Four Parks Get Decided

The city's own description of itself leans hard on this tension between staying rural and continuing to grow. Credit River's website states its aim plainly:

> "Our vision is to build a thriving community where nature, safety, and growth coexist."

That's not just a slogan. Credit River only became a city in 2021, converting from a township, and its population has grown modestly since, from 5,498 at the 2020 census to an estimated 5,634 in 2026. A five-year-old city government is still building the muscle memory of deciding what its own parks look like, which is exactly why the neighborhood park Community Conversations matter more than a routine council agenda item. They're one of the first times residents get a direct hand in shaping infrastructure the city actually controls, rather than infrastructure inherited from a county-level agreement.

The venue for that input is straightforward. The City Council meets the first and third Monday of each month at 6 p.m., and the Planning Commission meets the third Thursday at 6 p.m., both at Credit River City Hall on Meadow View Boulevard, with meetings also streamed live on the city's YouTube channel for anyone who can't make the drive. Compare that to Cleary Lake or Murphy-Hanrehan, where the decision-making body is Three Rivers Park District's board, elected across the whole nine-county park system, not by Credit River residents specifically. If a resident wants a say in how the lake beach or the singletrack trail is managed, the venue is a countywide board. If they want a say in what one of the four neighborhood parks becomes, the venue is a room a few minutes from home.

## Two Kinds of Park, Two Kinds of Say

|  | Regional parks (Cleary Lake, Murphy-Hanrehan) | City neighborhood parks (4 total) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Managed by | Three Rivers Park District / Scott County | City of Credit River |
| Acreage | Most of the county-cited 2,300+ acres | A small fraction of that total |
| Resident input | Countywide park board | City Council and Planning Commission, direct |
| Late-2026 rhythm | Beach closes Sept. 7, trail season peaks in September, golf continues into fall | Subject of the city's recurring "Community Conversations" series |

The table isn't a knock on the regional parks. Cleary Lake and Murphy-Hanrehan are genuinely well-run, and the joint powers arrangement is why a city of under 6,000 people gets access to amenities on the scale of a much bigger municipality. But it's worth being clear-eyed about which parts of that 2,300-acre figure a resident can actually weigh in on, and which parts are governed somewhere else entirely.

## What This Means If You Live Here

If your goal this month is simply to get the most out of the season before it turns, the order of operations is straightforward: get to the beach before September 7, then shift over to Murphy-Hanrehan for the singletrack's best month, and keep the twilight tee times going at whichever of the four courses is closest.

If your interest runs a little deeper, the more useful move is checking the city's meeting calendar for the next neighborhood park Community Conversations session or City Council meeting. Credit River is a young city still figuring out what its own four parks should be, separate from the two big regional parks that get all the acreage credit. Those conversations offer a direct way to contribute to that process.
