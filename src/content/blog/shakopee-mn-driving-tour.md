**Shakopee is one of the oldest communities around the Twin Cities. Settlers arrived by the Minnesota River, back when rivers were the freeways. It boomed until the 2008 housing crisis, cooled off like everywhere else, and has come full circle: it's now one of the metro's busiest cities for new housing permits. Here's a drive through town, with home prices along the way.**

Prices are as of my video above.

## Getting around

**Highway 169** runs right down the middle of Shakopee, so you're rarely more than a few minutes from it. From town, it's roughly:

- **20 to 25 minutes** to MSP Airport
- **25 to 30 minutes** to downtown Minneapolis
- **About 35 minutes** to downtown Saint Paul

That's in normal traffic. Snow days are a different story.

## Valleyfair

**Valleyfair** is Minnesota's only true amusement park, with several big roller coasters (including Wild Thing and a classic wooden coaster), a water park, season passes and **ValleySCARE** around Halloween. It draws visitors from across the region, which means jobs and business for the city. Shakopee has a lot of industry too, so it's not just a bedroom community, and that commercial tax base helps.

## The Landing on the Minnesota River

Right next door, Three Rivers Park District runs **The Landing**, a free park with a re-created 1800s village on the banks of the Minnesota River. This land was home to the Dakota for centuries before settlers arrived. The village has a church, a schoolhouse and homestead buildings, including the **Oliver Faribault house from 1844**, one of the oldest structures in Minnesota. One stone building has 22-inch-thick walls and weighs about 300,000 pounds. A state trail runs right through, and it connects to Shakopee's trail system.

## Canterbury Park and the amphitheater

**Canterbury Park** has been a horse-racing track since the late '80s (racing runs roughly May through September), with a 24/7 card casino and one of the few poker rooms in Minnesota. Next to it is the new **19,000-capacity amphitheater**, which has already drawn headliners like Chris Stapleton and Mumford & Sons. South metro residents can now see a big show without crossing the river.

Around it, about **1,000 new apartments** are going up, along with restaurants like Mr. Pig's and **Badger Hill Brewing**. You can live, work and be entertained in one spot.

## Historic downtown

The original downtown runs along **Highway 101** by the river, with buildings from the late 1800s. It went from quiet to busy again: **Donut Connection** (fantastic donuts), the **Shakopee Public House** (Irish pub food and a good pint), **Turtle's** billiards with an eclectic menu, boutiques, bakeries and a jeweler. There's even a Greyhound stop.

Homes near downtown were mostly built from the **late 1800s to about 1960**, typically **1,500 to 2,500 square feet**, many with original hardwoods. Some have been renovated and some still need it. Prices generally run from the **high $200,000s to mid-$400,000s**, with outliers both ways. Sidewalks line the streets, and Shakopee has **over 70 miles of trails**, not counting sidewalks.

## The 169 commercial corridor

Head south on Marshall Road to the newer commercial district at Marshall and 169: Cub Foods, Target, Marshalls, Applebee's and the usual fast-food spots. Shakopee has about **35 parks** and a newer **community center** with an ice rink, indoor pool, fitness center and an event space for about 200 people, with inexpensive memberships.

**St. Francis Regional Medical Center** is here too: a full hospital with an emergency room and TRIA orthopedics, and the largest hospital in the southwest metro.

Mid-'90s townhomes nearby run about **$225,000 to $280,000**, so parts of Shakopee are still very entry-level friendly, especially compared with places like Edina.

## Lake O'Dowd and Stonebrooke

**Lake O'Dowd** is Shakopee's only full-recreation lake, meaning wake boats, waterskiing and tubing, and it's known for great bass fishing. There's a public boat launch, and some island homes can only be reached by boat or over the ice. Homes around the lake range from about **$800,000 to several million**.

Nearby, **Stonebrooke Golf Course** is the best-known course in town, an 18-hole course with a very good clubhouse restaurant.

## Existing homes and new construction

- Single-family homes built in the early '90s to early 2000s around St. Francis run about **mid-$300,000s to mid-$500,000s**. You get a lot of house for the money compared with some cities just across the river, like [Eden Prairie](/communities/eden-prairie).
- Most **new construction** is south of 169, from national and local builders (including Lennar). New townhomes start in the **low to mid-$300,000s**. New single-family homes typically run **$600,000 to $800,000**, starting in the low $500,000s, with **$750,000 to $900,000** not uncommon.
- You'll still see working farms right next to new neighborhoods.

## Restored prairie and Mystic Lake

The **Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community** owns land in Shakopee and Prior Lake and is restoring much of it to native prairie, complete with grazing **bison**. The Community also owns **Mystic Lake Casino** just south in [Prior Lake](/communities/prior-lake), the largest casino in Minnesota, which hosts comedians and concerts.

## Southbridge

On the east side near [Savage](/communities/savage), **Southbridge** was one of Shakopee's first big master-planned communities, built mostly from the mid-'90s to mid-2000s. Townhomes there run about the **high $200,000s to mid-$300,000s**, and single-family homes about the **high $400,000s to high $600,000s**, some up to 4,000 square feet. Trails connect to the rest of the city, there's an outdoor ice rink, and the commercial area next door has Sam's Club, Walmart, a movie theater, Home Depot, Texas Roadhouse, Copper Pint and plenty more.

## The bottom line

Shakopee has a wide price range, from the mid-$200,000s to multiple millions on Lake O'Dowd, plus connected trails, a hospital, the river, Valleyfair and a big new concert venue, all at a relatively affordable price.
