**Minnesota's housing inventory just hit a 7-year high. If you're a buyer who spent years getting outbid, this may be the shift you've been waiting for. If you're a seller who's been on the fence since 2020, it probably sounds like bad news. It's more nuanced than either, and here are the numbers that tell the real story.**

These figures come from the Minnesota Realtors June report, as I covered in the video above (September 2026). For current data, see our [market page](/market).

## What a 7-year high means

Inventory is simply how many homes are for sale. When it's low, sellers have the leverage and can price high and still get multiple offers. When it rises, buyers get more choices and more time.

The key question is whether buyers are still showing up. If more homes sit and nobody buys, that's a warning sign. If sales rise along with inventory, the market is healthy.

## The numbers

**Statewide (June):**
- About **19,000 homes for sale**, up 7.5% from a year earlier and the highest June inventory since 2019. That's not an all-time high; it's a climb back toward normal.
- **New listings up 11%**, as more owners finally gave up low mortgage rates to move.
- **Pending sales up 8%** (an accepted offer waiting to close).

**Twin Cities metro:**
- Almost **11,000 homes for sale**, up 5.1%.
- **New listings up 10.5%** and **pending sales up 9.7%**.
- Minneapolis sales were up 4.7% and Saint Paul 1.4%, with stronger growth in the suburbs.

**Both sides are moving at once.** More homes are coming on the market, and more buyers are writing offers. Buyers are keeping up with the new supply.

## What it means for buyers

- **More room to negotiate.** You're not competing with five other buyers by breakfast on most homes.
- **Inspection contingencies are back**, and buyers are asking for help with closing costs or repairs again. A year or two ago, that could knock you out.
- **Condos are the most negotiable.** Condo sales fell 2.7% statewide.
- **It's not a lowball market.** Homes averaged 49 days on market statewide and about 42 in the metro, and Twin Cities sellers were still getting **99.6% of their original list price** (98.8% statewide). That figure is measured against the first list price, but it doesn't count seller concessions like closing cost help or rate buydowns, so the real gap is a bit wider.
- **Entry-level homes are still the tightest.** Homes from $250,000 to $350,000 have the lowest supply and the most competition.

Get pre-approved before you tour homes you're serious about. Move-in-ready, well-priced homes in desirable spots still get multiple offers and still sell fast.

## What it means for sellers

You didn't miss your window. Prices haven't dropped: the statewide median was **$375,000 (up 1.4%)** and the metro median **$410,000 (up 2.1%)**. Homes over $1 million were especially strong, with statewide pending sales up 42%.

But your home has real competition again, so:

- **Price it right from day one.** Overpricing and cutting later usually nets you less.
- **Condition and marketing matter more than ever.** Clean up the yard, fix the little things, deep clean, and use professional photos, not phone snapshots.
- **Watch the first two weeks.** If you get little interest, that's the market telling you something. Better to hear it early than after three price cuts.

## The one number to watch: months of supply

Months of supply is how long it would take to sell every home on the market at the current pace. Think of a grocery shelf: more boxes aren't a problem if people keep buying.

- **Statewide: 3.3 months**
- **Twin Cities metro: 2.8 months**

Most agents call 5 to 6 months a balanced market. In my professional opinion, in today's market, 3 to 4 months may be closer to balanced. By that measure, much of the metro is shifting toward buyers.

What would worry me is inventory climbing while sales stay flat. We're not seeing that in Minnesota. One update: the July report showed statewide inventory above 20,000, with pending sales growth cooling to under 3%. Not a crisis, but worth watching.

## The takeaway

Buyers and sellers both have more room to work with again. Buyers: use it, because waiting won't get you your dream home. Sellers: price it right and trust the process.
