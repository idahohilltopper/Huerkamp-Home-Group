**Selling a typical Twin Cities home can cost anywhere from about $15,000 to well over $50,000 to walk away from the house. In the video above I build a real budget line by line, with a low, middle and high case, so nothing surprises you at the closing table.**

All figures are from the video and were current as of November 2026, when I made it. They're an example, not a quote for your home.

## The example home

The median sales price in the Twin Cities metro was **$404,000** in August, according to Minnesota REALTORS®. I round to a **$405,000** example. Your home could be worth a lot more or less, and your mortgage payoff isn't a selling cost; everything below comes out on top of it.

## 1. Commissions: fully negotiable

Rules changed in **August 2024**, when changes from the National Association of REALTORS® settlement took effect:

- Commissions are **fully negotiable**. They aren't set by law and there's no official standard rate.
- Buyers now sign a **written agreement** with their agent before touring homes, which spells out how their agent is paid.
- Offers to pay the buyer's agent **can't go on the MLS** or public websites. Sellers can still offer that compensation outside the MLS, or offer concessions like help with closing costs.

So as a seller you make two decisions: what you pay your own agent, and whether you'll help with the buyer's side. For context, Redfin found the average buyer's agent commission nationally was about **2.4%** in the third quarter of 2025, a little under $10,000 on our example. For the math I use example totals from about **2.5%** (listing agent only) up to **6%** on the high end. Those are examples, not a standard.

## 2. Concessions

That's money you give the buyer toward closing costs, repairs or a rate buydown. Redfin says sellers gave concessions in about **45% of US home sales** in the three months ending in August, the highest August share since 2020 (national data). Here in the metro, sellers got about **98% of list price** in August, so buyers have a little more room to negotiate than a few years ago. I budget **0%, 1% and 2%**: about $4,000 to $8,000 on the example.

## 3. Point-of-sale inspections: the surprise

Some Minnesota cities require an inspection before you sell. Structure Tech, a Twin Cities home inspection company, tracks **16 cities** with a point-of-sale requirement: about nine need a full evaluation and seven only a sewer inspection. **Minneapolis and St. Paul** both have a Truth in Sale of Housing (TISH) inspection, and Bloomington, Richfield, Maplewood and St. Louis Park are on the list too. In St. Paul, the report must be available to buyers within three days of listing and is good for a year. Evaluators set their own fees, so get a quote.

The fee isn't what catches people off guard. It's what the report finds. See the rules for your city: [Minneapolis](/selling-in-minnesota/minneapolis), [St. Paul](/selling-in-minnesota/saint-paul), [Bloomington](/selling-in-minnesota/bloomington), [Richfield](/selling-in-minnesota/richfield), [Maplewood](/selling-in-minnesota/maplewood), [St. Louis Park](/selling-in-minnesota/st-louis-park).

## 4. Prep, staging and photos

Prep swings the most. Placeholder numbers, not quotes: about **$1,000** for touch-ups and cleaning, **$3,000** in the middle, **$8,000** if things really need fixing. Staging is optional: **$0 to $3,000** in the example. Ask your agent what marketing and photography are included. The cheapest way to sell isn't always spending the least; a little in the right spot can save you a bigger price cut later.

## 5. Minnesota-specific costs

- **State deed tax:** **0.33%** of the price, per the Minnesota Department of Revenue, plus **0.01%** in Hennepin and Ramsey counties. The Minnesota Attorney General's Home Seller Guide lists it as a seller cost. On the example: about **$1,340**, or about **$1,380** in Hennepin or Ramsey.
- **Property taxes:** paid in halves in May and October and split by date at closing. Depending on timing you may owe a little, or the buyer may reimburse you.
- **Title side:** abstract or title updates, the closing fee and recording fees to clear your mortgage: **$1,000 to $2,500** in the example. Your title company can give you a real number.
- **Rural property:** sellers must disclose wells (the well disclosure certificate carries a **$54** county recorder fee when filed) and how sewage is handled, including a known septic system. Some local governments require a septic inspection, so check your county.

## 6. Moving and carrying costs

Moving: **$1,000 to $5,000** in the example. Carrying costs are what you keep paying while the house is for sale. In August, Twin Cities homes spent **45 days** on the market, then closing typically takes another month to a month and a half. The Census Bureau puts median monthly owner costs with a mortgage in Hennepin County at about **$2,250**. I budget zero months, 1.5 months and three months.

## The bottom line on a $405,000 home

| Budget | Total | Share of price |
|---|---|---|
| Low | about $14,500 | about 3.6% |
| Middle | about $37,000 | a little over 9% |
| High | about $57,500 | a little over 14% |

That's a gap of more than $40,000 on the same home, and the biggest drivers are **commissions and concessions**, the conversations to have before you list.

**Taxes on your profit:** for a primary home, the IRS lets many sellers exclude up to **$250,000** of gain (**$500,000** married filing jointly) if they've owned and lived in it at least two of the last five years. There are exceptions, so talk to a tax professional. None of this is legal or tax advice.

## Five ways to land near the low end

1. **Negotiate everything up front:** your listing agreement, any offer to the buyer's agent and the marketing included. Get it in writing.
2. **Order a point-of-sale inspection early**, so surprises show up on your timeline, not mid-negotiation.
3. **Fix the small stuff** before you list; a dripping faucet makes buyers wonder what else was skipped.
4. **Price it right from day one.** Every extra week is another week of mortgage, taxes and utilities.
5. **Plan your move around closing**, so you don't pay for two homes at once.
