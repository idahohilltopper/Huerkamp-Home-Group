**For one week in February 2026, mortgage rates dipped below 6%, and it revealed how rate-sensitive the Twin Cities market is. A one-point drop in rates would let about 81,000 more households qualify to buy, almost overnight. Here's what that means, and what to do before it happens again.**

Figures are as of my video above (June 2026).

## What that week showed us

Freddie Mac's average dipped below 6% for a week in February, then moved back up; rates were running roughly 6.5% to 7.5% nationally when I made the video. But Fannie Mae expected rates near **5.9% by the end of 2026**, with other forecasters close to that, so a sub-6% moment will likely return. When it does, the Twin Cities will feel it fast.

## Why the Twin Cities are so rate-sensitive

The **National Association of Realtors' 2026 housing hotspots report** named the Twin Cities among the markets most responsive to lower rates. A one-point drop unlocks about **81,000** additional Twin Cities buyers, compared with about 52,000 in Charlotte and 41,000 in Columbus. Three reasons:

1. **The payment squeeze.** Many buyers are right at the edge of what they can afford, so a half-point move can turn "no" into "yes" for tens of thousands of people at once. As the president of Minnesota Realtors put it, rates and the economy will be key to the market's direction.
2. **Deep pent-up demand.** About 71% of Minnesota households own their homes; owning is part of the culture. Many would-be buyers have been waiting years, held back only by the payment.
3. **Not enough supply.** According to the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, the Twin Cities added about **12,000** housing units in 2025, versus a need of about **18,000** a year, the third straight year short.

A big buyer pool plus too few homes means that when rates drop, demand will hit fast.

## The market today: room to breathe, for now

In April 2026, the metro median was in the **high $300,000s**, and homes averaged about **57 days** on market, a very different world from 2021 and 2022. Sellers are offering rate buydowns and closing costs, and statewide new listings rose about 9% while signed purchase agreements rose almost 7.5%.

But balance isn't the same everywhere. **Woodbury** homes were selling at about **98.9%** of list price with only about two months of supply. Edina, Minnetonka, Eden Prairie and Chanhassen were still moving fast, many with under two months of supply.

## When rates fall

All those waiting buyers come back at once. The first segment to heat up will likely be **suburban single-family homes in the low to middle price ranges**, which can go right back to bidding wars, little negotiating and waived inspections.

**Condos** may recover on a different timeline. The median Twin Cities HOA fee is about **$278 a month**, and rising insurance, reserve needs and dues increases are separate from mortgage rates. New construction permits rose in early 2026, but those homes take 12 to 18 months to finish. Demand can jump immediately; supply can't. That's the time bomb.

## What to do now

**Buyers:**
- This may be the best setup Minneapolis buyers have seen in years. Ask for **seller-paid rate buydowns** and **closing cost credits** on every offer. A **2-1 buydown** lowers your rate two points in year one and one point in year two (see [what is a 2-1 buydown](/blog/2-1-buydown-explained)).
- Study the market neighborhood by neighborhood, because balanced overall doesn't mean easy everywhere.
- **Get fully underwritten pre-approval now**, not after rates drop. By then you're behind.
- Buying now at a slightly higher rate can lock in today's price and terms; you may be able to refinance later.

**Sellers:**
- Listing **before** the rate-driven surge can mean more buyer attention with less competition. When those buyers return, a wave of sellers will too.
