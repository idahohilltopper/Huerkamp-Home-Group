**Minneapolis has great health care, amazing parks and friendly people; Minnesota nice is real. But every city has good and bad, and most people only talk about the good. Here are the real pros and cons, including the things that could make or break your decision to move.**

Figures are as of my video above (January 2026).

## Pro 1: Quality of life

- Minneapolis ranked **#30 among the world's happiest cities** in 2025, earning gold status from the Institute for Quality of Life.
- It ranked **3rd** in the 2025 Trust for Public Land ParkScore, ahead of San Francisco, Boston and Portland.
- The League of American Bicyclists named it a **Gold-level Bicycle Friendly Community**, with about 90 miles of trails and 36 miles of curb-protected bikeways.

The infrastructure is well planned: wide streets and public spaces that work the way they should. Quality of life comes down to walking out your door and having access to what you need, and Minneapolis delivers.

## Pro 2: Outdoor living year-round

The Minneapolis Park and Recreation Board manages more than **185 park properties** covering about **7,059 acres**. The **Chain of Lakes** (Bde Maka Ska, Lake Harriet, Lake of the Isles and Cedar Lake) is connected by trails, and the **Grand Rounds Scenic Byway** is a loop of about 55 miles with roughly 51 miles each of walking and biking trails. Summer means kayaking, paddleboarding, biking and running; winter brings skating on frozen lakes, snowshoeing, cross-country skiing and fat-tire biking, and the city maintains trails all year.

## Pro 3: Affordable compared to other big metros

The Minneapolis median was about **$350,000**, up 8.1%. Compared with Chicago, Denver or Seattle, you get more space and value. Midwest home prices don't swing as wildly, with fewer extreme highs and lows, which matters if you're thinking long-term. Families relocating from the coasts say the same thing: their money goes much further.

## Pro 4: Health care and higher education

Minnesota ranks among the best states for health care. **Mayo Clinic** in Rochester is just over an hour away, and people come from around the world for treatment, while major hospitals in Minneapolis provide top care in the city. The **University of Minnesota** brings research, cultural events and a college-town feel to parts of the city.

## Pro 5: Arts, food and music

The **Guthrie Theater**, **First Avenue** (the iconic venue tied to Prince, whose hometown legacy is everywhere, from murals to tribute shows), farm-to-table restaurants, craft breweries and James Beard-nominated chefs. The Twin Cities food scene keeps earning national attention.

## Pro 6: A clean, well-planned city

You notice it right away: not much litter, organized streets, and public services from trash pickup to snow removal to park maintenance that work the way they're supposed to. The freeway rings around the city keep traffic manageable for its size.

## Con 1: Summers are amazing but short

You get maybe three to three and a half months of real summer. The cold season runs from about November through mid-March, and seasonal affective disorder is common; many people use light therapy lamps. Personally, I love having four real seasons, but they come with a cost.

## Con 2: The cost of living is creeping up

The city median rose 8.1% to about $350,000. In desirable areas, prices are higher and rising faster: the **Linden Hills** median was around **$698,000**, and neighboring **Edina** was about **$760,000** (up 8.2%). **Uptown**, with its mix of condos and houses, was about $232,000, down 8%. It's still affordable compared with Denver or Seattle, but not as cheap as it used to be, and first-time buyers feel it near downtown and the lakes.

## Con 3: Potholes and road construction

Locals say there are two seasons: winter and road construction. Water freezes in cracks and breaks pavement apart. Minneapolis committed more than **$225 million** to infrastructure in 2025 for streets, bridges and utilities, which is good long-term, but expect orange cones and plan alternate routes.

## Con 4: High taxes

Minnesota's top income tax rate is **9.85%**, one of the highest in the country, and the state ranked **44th** in the 2026 State Tax Competitiveness Index. Coming from Texas, Florida or Nevada, you'll notice. Those taxes fund schools, parks, health care and infrastructure; whether that trade is worth it is your call.

## Con 5: Brutal winters

Long, cold and dark. The record is **68 days below zero**, in the winter of 1874-75, and average snowfall is about **52 inches**. Temperatures can stay below freezing for weeks. With the right gear and mindset, it's not as bad as people make it out to be; many people ice fish, snowshoe, ski and snowmobile. Those who stay learn to love it and appreciate summer all the more.

## Is Minneapolis right for you?

Incredible quality of life, along with high taxes, rising prices, tough winters and endless construction. Minneapolis is perfect for the right person.
