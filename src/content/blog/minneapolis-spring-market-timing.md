**Everybody talks about the Minneapolis spring market like it's one thing. It's not. There's an early spring market, where smart buyers have leverage and prepared sellers win, and a late spring market, where great homes get bidding wars and average homes get buried. Here's where the pressure lands first and which window fits you.**

Figures are as of my video above (February 2026). For more on overpricing, see [the biggest mistake sellers are making](/blog/biggest-mistake-minneapolis-sellers-pricing).

## The snapshot

- About **7,700 homes** were available, more than the roughly 5,000 to 6,000 in 2021 and 2022, but still short of a balanced market.
- Homes were sitting about **50 days**, so buyers had time to compare, inspect and negotiate.
- The median sale price was in the **mid-$300,000s to low $390,000s**, up about **3%** year over year.

Technically, it's still a seller's market, but leverage is shifting home by home, not evenly. Price, condition and location all matter again.

## Where spring pressure starts

Picture an updated home near a lake, listed March 1, with multiple offers by March 5 and selling over asking. Meanwhile, a comparable home three miles away with dated finishes sits for 60 days. That's spring.

Demand lands first on **well-located, move-in-ready homes**: walkability, lake access, the Chain of Lakes and **Grand Rounds** trails, or **Northeast Minneapolis** with its hundreds of artist studios, Art-A-Whirl in May and plenty of breweries. After a long winter, buyers don't want a project. When people say "the market is heating up," they usually mean the **top third** of listings.

## Where it falls apart

Spring exposes three things:

1. **Unrealistic pricing.** Sellers price off what homes sold for last May or June. A home 10% to 15% above what the market will bear doesn't get showings and goes stale week by week.
2. **Deferred maintenance.** Outdated baths, old carpet and a 1980s kitchen make buyers do the math ("I need $30,000 to $50,000 to make this livable") and offer low or move on.
3. **Chasing last year's numbers.** If your neighbor got $425,000 last spring but the market says low $400,000s now, fighting it costs time and momentum.

A home that might have sold in January with some forgiveness gets passed over in April when inventory ticks up. Spring doesn't fix problems; it exposes them faster.

## Who has leverage

Right now, buyers can ask for repairs, negotiate price and keep contingencies. As spring goes on, **great homes gain leverage fast and average homes don't**. By April, an updated home in a top location may bring multiple offers and waived inspection contingencies. But an average home's buyer often gains leverage through spring, because that home is competing with 20 new listings. The top tier is hot; the middle and lower tiers stay negotiable.

## The spring calendar

- **Late February through early March:** spring starts with snow on the ground. It's the best balance of choice and leverage, with fewer competing buyers and the most motivated ones.
- **Late March through April:** the listing surge. Buyer activity spikes and pricing mistakes get punished fast. List at the wrong price and you're buried by 50 other homes. (And give the house a real spring cleaning first.)
- **May through June:** peak competition for the best homes, with faster decisions and less room to negotiate. Sellers have historically received 100% or more of list price in this window versus about 97% to 98% in fall and winter. Many buyers want to be settled by summer.

**For sellers:** with a top-tier home, late March or April can work in your favor. With an average home, listing in **late February or early March** means less competition. See [selling in fall vs. spring](/blog/sell-house-fall-or-spring-twin-cities).

## What to do

**Sellers:** spring rewards preparation, not patience. With more homes projected to sell in 2026 than in 2025, the competition you avoid today is unavoidable in April. Paint, update, stage, clean and price right from day one. If comparable homes are sitting 40, 50 or 60 days, that's telling you something. [Get a home value estimate](/home-estimate).

**Buyers:** acting early avoids bidding wars, and flexibility creates leverage. Get pre-approved now and know your numbers. In March and April, hesitation costs you homes; in May and June, it costs you money. Be flexible on location: if [Edina](/communities/edina) is tight, [St. Louis Park](/communities/st-louis-park) has homes from the $350,000s to the $500,000s and the Green Line extension coming in 2027. Or trade new construction for an updated older home in Northeast with character and walkability.

Rates were projected to ease through 2026, but prices were still rising modestly, so what you save on interest you may lose on price. A perfect moment with no competition, low rates and falling prices has never existed. The right time is when you find the right home at a price you can afford.
