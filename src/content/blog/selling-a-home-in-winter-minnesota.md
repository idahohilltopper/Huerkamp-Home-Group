**Every season has pros and cons for selling, but when you're ready to sell, there's really no bad time of year. You just play to the strengths of the season. Here's how that works in a Minnesota winter.**

## The pros

**Less competition.** Fewer homes are listed in winter, so yours stands out, and in a typical market it can sell faster than it would in spring or summer.

**Serious buyers.** Nobody tours homes in a Minnesota January to kick tires. Winter buyers are often relocating, on a deadline or ready to buy now.

**A chance to show off energy efficiency.** Heating bills are front of mind in winter, so highlight:

- A smart thermostat
- Newer dual- or triple-pane windows and good window treatments
- Upgraded attic insulation
- A newer furnace or HVAC system, or recently cleaned ducts
- A tankless water heater
- Solar panels

These are expensive to replace, so newer ones are a real selling point.

**More agent focus.** Agents are juggling fewer clients in winter, so your listing can get more attention.

**Homes can show beautifully**, especially when they're decorated for the holidays (keep it tasteful and uncluttered).

## The cons

**More prep work.** Curb appeal still matters. Keep the driveway and walks plowed, shoveled, salted and sanded so no one slips. Clear away dead plants and stray snow piles.

**Leaving for showings.** It's harder in the cold, but you shouldn't be home for showings, inspections or appraisals. Buyers feel uncomfortable when the owner is there, and a little inconvenience usually nets you more money.

**Fewer bidding wars.** With fewer buyers, multiple offers are less likely, though correctly priced homes can still draw them. Nothing's guaranteed in real estate.

**Mid-year moves can be harder** if your household is juggling school-year schedules and activities.
