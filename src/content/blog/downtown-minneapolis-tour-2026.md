**Most people have no idea what downtown Minneapolis looks like today, and even people who've visited haven't seen how much it's changed. Old warehouses are now lofts, the food scene is world-class, and it's one of the most walkable cities in the country, even at 20 below, thanks to the skyways. Here's the tour from my video above.**

## Why Minneapolis is so walkable

Downtown has three major sports venues, a deep arts scene (from the Guthrie to the Walker Art Center), and a skyway system that lets you walk everywhere indoors in January. Outside, the city has **22 lakes, thousands of miles of trails and about 18,000 acres of parkland**. If it weren't called the City of Lakes, it could be called the City of Trees.

## Minnehaha Creek and the Chain of Lakes

Minnehaha Creek flows out of Lake Minnetonka and through south Minneapolis, lined mostly with parkland, walking and biking trails, and tree-lined streets. It feels like a park even though you're in the middle of a big metro.

The **Chain of Lakes** is Lake Harriet, Bde Maka Ska (formerly Lake Calhoun) and Lake of the Isles, with walking paths around each one. Homes here range from apartments, if you want a taste of lake living, all the way to mansions. In winter, Lake Harriet freezes thick enough that people drive on it. You'll also see some teardowns and rebuilds, which are happening all over the city.

On the east side, **Lake Nokomis** has trails and several parks around it. Prices there are a little more affordable, and the architecture is more consistent than around the lakes to the west.

## From the lakes to downtown

It's an easy trip from the lakes, mostly in south Minneapolis, to downtown. Along the way:

- **The Foshay Tower**, for years the tallest building west of the Mississippi, now has offices and a speakeasy-style bar up top with views over downtown.
- **The Walker Art Center and Minneapolis Sculpture Garden**: 11 acres, home of the *Spoonbridge and Cherry*, one of the most photographed sculptures in the country.
- **Dunwoody College of Technology**, more than 100 years old, which trains electricians, plumbers and HVAC pros.
- **Kenwood**, on a hill overlooking downtown. Entry-level homes are in the high six figures, and some reach several million. It's mostly single-family homes, many from the late 1800s to the 1930s, plus historic water towers the city has kept.

Minneapolis has homes at nearly every price point, from a couple hundred thousand dollars to the mansions around the lakes.

## Downtown landmarks

- **First Avenue**, privately owned and open since 1970, hosts every genre. Its outside wall has stars for artists who've played there; Prince has the only gold one.
- **Target Center** (Timberwolves), **Target Field** (Twins) and **U.S. Bank Stadium** (Vikings, on the old Metrodome site, connected by skyway).
- **Minneapolis City Hall**, which honestly looks a little like Hogwarts.
- Classic steakhouses: **Murray's** and **Manny's**. The city and suburbs have plenty of places that have been on *Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives*, too.

## The Mill District and the riverfront

The **Stone Arch Bridge**, once James J. Hill's railroad bridge between the mills, is now a walking path over the Mississippi. The **Mill City Museum** sits in the ruins of a flour mill and hosts weddings and events in its open-air courtyard. Newer towers like **Eleven** sell from the high hundreds of thousands to many millions, with lots of amenities.

A few more details:

- **Mill District** lofts run about 1,400 to 3,000 square feet, with a Walk Score of 91 and a Bike Score of 93.
- **Elliot Park** is walking distance to U.S. Bank Stadium, the Guthrie and Hennepin County Medical Center, and connects to the skyways.
- **Loring Park** has one of the city's largest parks and hosts the Twin Cities Pride festival. Its Walk Score is 91, Bike Score 86 and Transit Score 78, a strong pick if you want to live car-free.
