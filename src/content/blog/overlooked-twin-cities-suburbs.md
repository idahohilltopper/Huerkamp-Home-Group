**Five Twin Cities suburbs are hiding in plain sight: first-ring locations, hundreds of acres of parks, nearly 100 miles of connected trails, and river views most people don't know exist, often for less than buyers expect. Here's where to look.**

Prices are as of my video above (March 2026).

## West St. Paul

A lot of buyers skip [West St. Paul](/communities/west-st-paul) completely, which is why it stays underrated. Just south of Saint Paul, minutes from downtown, it's established and easy: mature streets, solid homes, and a city that isn't trying to reinvent itself every five minutes.

- **Dodge Nature Center:** over 460 acres, free, open sunrise to sunset, with trails, wildlife and programs for all ages. It feels like something much farther out.
- **16 city parks**, an indoor ice arena, an athletic dome and a community pool, so things don't shut down in winter, plus **Thompson County Park** nearby.
- **South Robert Street** and 600+ local businesses for groceries, restaurants and errands: convenient, not chaotic.

**Homes:** mostly in the **mid-$300,000s**, sometimes into the low $400,000s. For a first-ring suburb, that's a fantastic deal and one of the best entry points around.

## Savage

[Savage](/communities/savage), about 15 to 20 miles southwest of downtown Minneapolis in Scott County, has almost **100 miles of connected trails and sidewalks**, and most people don't realize it.

- **23 parks**, about 30 miles of paved trails and 60 miles of sidewalks that actually connect the city.
- **Murphy-Hanrehan Park Reserve**, which you can bike to without dealing with busy roads.
- Parks spread evenly through neighborhoods: Savage's own **Canterbury Park** (not the racetrack) for ball fields and hockey, **Dakota Landing** for boating, canoeing and fishing, **Glendale Park** for sand volleyball, and **South Park Drive** for soccer, lacrosse, ice rinks and pickleball.
- A small **downtown** with a better food scene than you'd expect, including award-winning spots.
- The **Port of Savage** on the Minnesota River, which brings jobs and steady activity.

It's tucked between **Highway 169 and I-35W**, so getting around is easier than people expect. **Homes:** low to mid-$400,000s, and the market has been steady.

## Richfield

[Richfield](/communities/richfield) is surrounded by Minneapolis and Bloomington, about 7 miles from downtown and only about 7 square miles. You're never far from anything: about five minutes to the Mall of America, close to the airport and an easy drive to both downtowns. It's clean, well run and still feels like its own place.

- About **450 acres across 23 parks**.
- **Wood Lake Nature Center:** about 150 acres of trails, boardwalks, water and wildlife, with 3 miles of paths, winter ski trails and an outdoor amphitheater. The main building is being redone in late 2026, but the trails stay open.
- An ice arena, pool, mini golf, farmers market, arts programs and a skate park.
- **Best Buy's headquarters**, one of the city's largest employers.

**Homes:** mostly in the **mid-$300,000s**, up about 3.6% over the past year.

## Mendota Heights

[Mendota Heights](/communities/mendota-heights) is the only place in the Twin Cities where you can stand and watch the **Mississippi and Minnesota rivers** come together. Between Minneapolis and Saint Paul in Dakota County, it has big views, bluffs and room to breathe, minutes from both downtowns. It is a relatively young city, but people have been drawn to this land, near **Fort Snelling**, for a very long time.

- **Oheyawahi (Pilot Knob):** a sacred site with deep Dakota history, on the bluff with views toward Fort Snelling and downtown Minneapolis. It's quiet, meaningful and open to everyone.
- **16 parks** with ballfields, courts, rinks, walking paths and ponds.
- A mix of local shops and restaurants: not loud, just close by.

**Homes:** mostly single-family, plus some condos and lower-maintenance options. Prices run higher than the others here, usually **high $500,000s to mid-$600,000s**, with many above that.

## Golden Valley

[Golden Valley](/communities/golden-valley) may be the easiest place on this list to live day to day. Downtown Minneapolis is about 5 miles away, so you can head in for lunch, a meeting or a game without planning your day around it.

- **Brookview:** a 33-acre park with walking paths, courts, grills and volleyball, plus an indoor play space, event rooms, a golf course, lawn bowling and a restaurant.
- **Schaper Park:** trails, softball fields, an all-abilities play area and a fitness course.
- **Theodore Wirth Park** next door: over 750 acres of trails, woods, biking, golf and cross-country skiing.

**Homes:** older ramblers, mid-century moderns and some newer homes in established neighborhoods, mostly in the **mid-$400,000s**, up about 3.6% over the past year.
