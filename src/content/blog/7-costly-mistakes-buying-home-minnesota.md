**Whether you're buying in the Twin Cities, Duluth, Rochester or out in the country, these are the seven mistakes I see buyers make most often in Minnesota, and how to avoid each one.**

## 1. Underestimating winter maintenance

- **Snow load:** in heavy-snow years, some roofs need to be raked or shoveled.
- **Ice dams:** poor attic insulation and ventilation let heat melt snow that refreezes at the eaves, which can push water into the house.
- **Driveways:** a long driveway takes a long time to clear, and plowing gets expensive if you don't do it yourself.

Ask the sellers what they spend on winter upkeep so you can plan.

## 2. Skipping the sewer or septic inspection

In a competitive market, it's tempting to skip inspections to make your offer stronger. Don't skip this one. Many sewer lines in older Twin Cities neighborhoods are **clay pipe**, which cracks like a clay pot, and tree roots grow into those cracks. A **sewer scope** runs a camera down the line from the house to the street, and you get a video report. If the home is on a **private septic system**, get it inspected. See [septic, radon and wells](/blog/minnesota-homes-hidden-costs-septic-radon-wells).

## 3. Assuming the home is energy efficient

Homes built since about 2000, and especially 2010 and newer, are generally fairly efficient, with house wrap and better windows. Homes built **before 1990** can vary widely. A renovated 1890s home may be as efficient as a new build, while a 1960s or '70s home may have thin attic insulation, older windows and an old HVAC system. Ask for the sellers' gas and electric bills, but remember that you don't know where they kept the thermostat.

## 4. Ignoring flood zones

Flooding isn't a huge issue in most of Minnesota, but it happens. Check **FEMA flood maps**, or ask us to check for you. A flood zone can affect insurance cost and availability; see [what raises home insurance premiums](/blog/minnesota-home-insurance-premiums-tips).

## 5. Not checking property taxes

Always know the property taxes on a specific home before you buy. They vary widely by county, city and taxing district, and they're often higher inside the seven-county metro than outside it. Commercial development matters too: my own town's taxes run a bit higher partly because it has little commercial tax base. Use our [affordability calculator](/calculators/affordability).

## 6. Ignoring commute time, especially in winter

On a snow day with six inches or more, a commute can take **three times** as long. The farther you live from work, the more that matters. Flexibility to work from home on bad days helps a lot. Try our [commute tool](/commute).

## 7. Falling in love too fast

In a competitive market, buyers sometimes decide very quickly and then have serious buyer's remorse. A little second-guessing is normal, since it's your mind checking your work. A lot of it means you moved too fast. Slow down enough to think it through.
