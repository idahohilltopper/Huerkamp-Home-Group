**If you're searching for the best suburbs in the Twin Cities, pause for a second. Those lists don't tell the whole story. Some suburbs look perfect online, and six months after moving, day-to-day life looks different than expected. Here are five that show up on almost every "best places" list and still surprise people moving from out of state, and who each one is right for.**

None of these are bad places to live. Each one has the right buyer. The problem is choosing the image instead of the fit. Prices below are as of my video above (spring 2026).

## 1. Maple Grove: great, but you'll live in your car

Online, [Maple Grove](/communities/maple-grove) looks perfect: new homes, wide streets, cul-de-sacs, trails, and the Arbor Lakes district with nearly every store and restaurant you could want. Builders like Lennar are still building new neighborhoods.

**The surprise:** it's one of the most car-dependent suburbs in the metro. It's about 13 miles from downtown Minneapolis, roughly 20 to 25 minutes in light traffic, but I-94 and 494 get busy at rush hour, and transit is limited. Most errands mean car keys. Buyers coming from places like Chicago or Denver often feel the sprawl more than they expected.

**Prices:** median sale price in the mid-$400,000s; new construction from the mid-$400,000s to mid-$500,000s.

**Best for:** buyers who want newer homes, big parks (Elm Creek Park Reserve is one of the largest in the metro) and great shopping, and who are fine driving everywhere.

## 2. Eden Prairie: budget for the full monthly cost

[Eden Prairie](/communities/eden-prairie) is a very well-run city, with a strong job base in the Golden Triangle business area, easy highway access, lakes, trails and established neighborhoods. The photos aren't lying.

**The surprise:** the total monthly payment. Homes generally run from the mid-$400,000s to the mid-$500,000s, with some neighborhoods well above that. Property taxes are in the low 1% range, above the national average, and recent levy increases have pushed bills higher. Townhomes and association communities often add HOA dues in the low to mid-$100s a month. Add mortgage, taxes and dues together, and buyers who focused only on the list price can be caught off guard.

**Best for:** buyers who want stability, amenities and long-term value, and who budget for the whole cost of owning. Try our [affordability calculator](/calculators/affordability) first.

## 3. Shakopee: more house, longer commute

[Shakopee](/communities/shakopee) stands out for price: typical values in the low to mid-$400,000s, which can feel like a discount compared to suburbs closer in. Entertainment is growing, with Canterbury Park, Valleyfair, the Canterbury District redevelopment and the new Mystic Lake Amphitheater (nearly 19,000 seats, opening summer 2026).

**The surprise:** it's about 24 miles from downtown Minneapolis, mostly on Highway 169. That's about 30 minutes in light traffic, but 45 to 50 minutes at rush hour, and longer in snow. Bus trips to Minneapolis can take over an hour, some closer to an hour and 45 minutes with a transfer.

**Best for:** people who work nearby or remotely and want more house for the money.

## 4. Brooklyn Park: good value, older homes

[Brooklyn Park](/communities/brooklyn-park) gets attention for price and location: homes often in the low to mid-$300,000s, often with more square footage, and a reasonable drive to downtown. Homes often go under contract in about 30 days, and the city is investing in infrastructure and redevelopment.

**The surprise:** much of the housing was built between the mid-1960s and early 1980s, some earlier. Electrical, plumbing, windows and roofs may be near the end of their life, and listing photos don't show that. Not every house has problems, but inspections sometimes turn up expensive updates.

**Best for:** buyers comfortable evaluating older homes, with a strong inspection. (See [7 reasons not to skip the home inspection](/blog/2022/10/27/7-reasons-why-buyers-and-sellers-shouldnt-ditch-the-home-inspection).)

## 5. Woodbury: not a quality problem, a geography problem

[Woodbury](/communities/woodbury) is clean, newer and very well kept, with strong shopping and parks. Homes typically sit in the mid-$400,000s, from the low to high $400,000s, and newer homes can reach $500,000 or more.

**The surprise:** Woodbury is east of Saint Paul, not west or north of Minneapolis like many buyers assume. Getting to downtown Minneapolis means driving through Saint Paul on I-94: about 30 minutes in light traffic and 45 or more at rush hour, with slowdowns near 35E. The Gold Line bus rapid transit (opened 2025) reaches downtown Saint Paul in about 30 minutes, but Minneapolis still takes a transfer.

**Best for:** people who work in Saint Paul or on the east side of the metro.
