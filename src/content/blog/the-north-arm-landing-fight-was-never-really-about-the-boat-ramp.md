Hennepin County describes the work as an infrastructure repair. Orono City Council has also discussed how a public boat landing on Lake Minnetonka is used and who makes those decisions since last fall.

Those questions don't have clean answers yet. And if you launch a boat, fish from shore, or just live within earshot of North Shore Drive, the unresolved parts matter more than the new pavement will.

## What's Already Changed This Season

You may have noticed it without knowing why: North Arm is now a pack-in, pack-out landing. Hennepin County put that trash policy into effect for the 2026 boating season, part of a broader push to deal with litter at a site whose infrastructure has been wearing down for three decades.

That's the small, already-live change. The bigger ones are still being negotiated. Hennepin County's redesign plan, most recently presented to the Orono City Council on April 13, 2026, called for widening the boat ramp itself to make room for three simultaneous users, up from the current 36-foot, two-lane setup, along with new fishing piers, a picnic area, realigned parking, and one of the only ADA-accessible boat ramps in the metro area.

The city pushed back, specifically on ramp width. After a round of additional questions from the council on launch width, trash management, and parking lot use, Hennepin County agreed in a May 26 memo to hold the ramp at its existing 36-foot, two-lane width rather than expand it. The city council took up the project's remaining variances on June 8.

Construction is scheduled for fall 2026 through fall 2027, done in phases specifically so the landing stays usable through the busiest stretches of fishing and boating season. In plain terms: this is likely your last full, uninterrupted summer at North Arm before the work crews show up.

## Before and After: What the County Backed Off

The back-and-forth between the county and the city is easier to see side by side.

| Feature | County's original proposal | What's actually happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ramp width | Widen to 45 feet for 3 simultaneous users | Held at current 36 feet, 2 lanes |
| Second launch | A separate "vertical" access to split commercial and recreational traffic | Dropped after planning commission pushback; replaced with a picnic area |
| Trash | Standard bins | Pack-in, pack-out policy, already in effect for 2026 |
| ADA access | New ADA-accessible ramp | Still included, one of the few ADA ramps on the metro's lakes |
| Parking | Angled stalls, realigned lot | Retained in current plan |

The pattern worth noticing: every time the county proposed something that expanded capacity or split off a lane for commercial-style use, Orono's planning commission and city council pushed back, and the county revised. The Orono Planning Commission didn't recommend approval of the county's first design after a November 17, 2025 hearing. The second design, presented in April, still needed another round of concessions before the council would move forward.

## Questions About Lake Access Rules

The redesign also raises broader questions about lake access. Lake Minnetonka has 14 shoreline cities, a county that owns some of the public landings, the Lake Minnetonka Conservation District, and the Minnesota DNR, and none of them has settled how commercial boat traffic should be regulated at a public launch built for recreational use.

North Arm became the test case almost by accident. The county's original design would have added a second launch specifically to separate commercial operators from everyday boaters. Orono's mayor, Bob Tunheim, told the [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/lake-minnetonka-north-arm-public-access/601503966) that the concept conflicted with the city's own zoning, which bans commercial and industrial use in residential districts. The county dropped it.

In a [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/lake-minnetonka-north-arm-public-access/601503966) report, Hennepin County Commissioner Heather Edelson, who represents most of the lake, said:

> "North Arm got caught in the middle of a larger issue."

She's now pushing for consistent rules across all the lake's public landings rather than a patchwork that changes at every city line. It's not a small ask. A legal review already found that charging user fees at North Arm would require sign-off from Carver County, the DNR, and the LMCD, a process long enough that the county has decided not to pursue it for now. Minnetonka, a few miles down the shore, already restricts some commercial activity at its Gray's Bay landing through city zoning. Orono hasn't gone that route, but the door isn't closed.

## Who Actually Enforces the Rules You're Following

This is where it gets practical if you're the one at the landing on a Saturday morning. Orono Police Chief Correy Farniok told the council directly that his department doesn't have the staffing to consistently monitor and enforce the rules already on the books at North Arm, let alone new ones. He's floated a county-city contract to fund dedicated patrol staff specifically for landings like this one. Hennepin County's Land and Water Supervisor, Kris Guentzel, has suggested the DNR could step up its own inspection visits in the meantime.

None of that is settled yet either. So the answer to "who's watching the rules at North Arm" this season is that consistent monitoring has not been arranged, although the rules remain in place.

One number worth sitting with if traffic safety is your concern: residents have raised worries about the North Arm entrance and exit onto North Shore Drive, but county transportation staff report only one crash near the boat launch driveway in the past ten years. It's on their list to keep monitoring, but it isn't currently treated as a priority fix, and the crash data is the reason why.

## What This Means If You're Out on the Lake This Month

Most of your boating routine stays the same right now. The ramp is the same width it's always been, the ADA access and ongoing parking arrangement are unchanged, and construction doesn't start until fall. The one real difference already in effect is the trash policy: bring a bag, take it with you.

If you want to document North Arm and the rest of the lake before the redesign moves forward, the timing lines up with this year's Lens on Lake Minnetonka photo contest, open through August 31 to shutterbugs of all ages and experience levels. It's a low-key way to have a record of the landing as it exists today, backing turn and all, before the phased construction starts changing the shoreline.

After a morning on the water, Orono and the surrounding lake towns aren't short on places to land. Birch's on the Lake Brewhouse & Supperclub and Bucks Pub are both regular stops for the after-boating crowd, and Primo Plates & Pours has become one of the names locals mention first when someone asks where to eat near the lake. None of that changes because of a construction schedule two miles away.

What does change, eventually, is the landing itself, and more importantly, who's responsible for policing what happens there. That's worth tracking even if you never launch a boat, because questions involving city zoning, county ownership, DNR oversight, and LMCD rules can affect projects built or repaired near this shoreline. North Arm is one place where those questions have come into focus.
