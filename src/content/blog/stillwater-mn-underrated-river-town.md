**Stillwater was one of Minnesota's first big towns, booming before Minneapolis really got going. Today, most people moving to the Twin Cities drive right past it. After more than two decades selling homes here, here's why this river town is one of the most overlooked places to live in the metro, and who it fits best.**

Prices are as of my video above (June 2026).

## The story

Stillwater calls itself the **birthplace of Minnesota**, and that's real: the 1848 convention that started the push for statehood was held here. The money came from white pine up north. Logs floated down the **St. Croix River** and were cut, processed and sold here, and by the 1850s Stillwater was one of the biggest towns in the territory. The lumber era ended in the early 1900s when the last logs came through.

What's left is a downtown of old brick buildings, many former mills, warehouses and shops, and Stillwater kept them. There are brewery caves carved by hand into the sandstone bluffs in the 1800s, and an antique shop that used to be a sawmill. Nothing torn down, everything reused. That's why it feels different from anywhere else in the metro.

## What makes it special

In most small towns, you've seen everything in 45 minutes. Not here.

- **Downtown sits at the water**, and the neighborhoods climb about 100 feet up the bluffs behind it, so it doesn't feel like the flat suburbs.
- **Five historic staircases** from the 1800s, built so workers could reach the mills, now double as morning workouts.
- **The St. Croix is a protected National Wild and Scenic River**, so the shoreline stays natural.
- **The lift bridge** to Wisconsin is now for walking and biking only. Cross it, follow the bluffs and come back over the newer St. Croix Crossing bridge for a full loop.
- **Well over 100 locally owned shops, restaurants and breweries.** **Midtown Antique Mall** has vendors across three floors, and Fodor's has ranked Stillwater among the country's top antiquing towns. **Lift Bridge Brewing** has a dog-friendly patio, and **River Siren** took over the old Maple Island space.
- **Sandstone caves** that brewers used to keep beer cold before refrigeration are now part of the **Lora Hotel**. Ask about access when you visit.

## Outdoor life from Main Street

In most suburbs, you load the bike and drive to a trailhead. Here, you walk out of a coffee shop and you're on a trail in about two minutes.

- **Brown's Creek State Trail** starts by the train depot and connects to the **Gateway Trail**, so you can bike from the St. Croix all the way to Saint Paul.
- **The St. Croix Crossing loop** crosses the river and returns on the other bridge.
- **Two full-service marinas:** Wolf Marine, about a mile north of downtown, and Sunnyside Marina near the newer bridge, a short walk from restaurants and breweries. There's also a newer public river access in [Oak Park Heights](/communities/oak-park-heights).

## The commute is real

I won't sugarcoat this. Heading to Minneapolis, you take **Highway 36**. In good traffic, downtown Minneapolis is about **35 to 40 minutes**, longer at rush hour, and some stretches still back up at peak times. A planned project at Lake Elmo Avenue should help one of the slow spots, but it may get worse before it gets better. If you drive downtown every day with no flexibility, that adds up. If you work from home or go in two or three days a week, it feels very different.

## Who it fits

- Buyers who want **older homes and a walkable downtown** without top Twin Cities prices. You won't find this setup in Eden Prairie or Maple Grove.
- **Remote and hybrid workers.** A few drives a week is a fair trade for a river view.
- People who love **boating, biking, running and kayaking**, with everything close together.

## What homes cost

- **Entry-level:** low to mid-$400,000s
- **Historic in-town homes:** mid to high $500,000s
- **Bluff-view homes:** into the low $600,000s and up

That's competitive for what you get, but inventory is tight and the market leans toward sellers, so don't wait too long on one you love. **Washington County** property taxes also tend to run lower than Hennepin or Ramsey, which helps the monthly payment.
