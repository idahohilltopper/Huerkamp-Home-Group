**Homes over a million dollars are having a moment in the Twin Cities. So I gave myself a challenge: if I had a million bucks, where would I put it? In the video above I picked six suburbs, found a real listing in each, and told you where I'd put my own money.**

The listings are examples from when I made the video in November 2026; they may have sold or changed price since. Figures are as stated in the video.

## Why the top of the market is moving

Across the Twin Cities, the typical home sells for right around **$410,000**, so a million dollars is more than double that. Pending sales over $1 million jumped **42% across Minnesota** in June compared with the year before. That pace has cooled since, but the top of the market is still moving. Last time I did this challenge it was with three-quarters of a million; see also [what $1 million buys, suburb by suburb](/blog/what-1-million-buys-twin-cities-suburbs).

## Lakeville: a lot of new construction

The listing I found was **just under $1 million**: five bedrooms, five baths and almost **4,400 finished square feet**, a big modern home. Lakeville also has acreage at that price, but the tradeoff with new builds is simple: **smaller lots**. If land matters more than square footage, keep that in mind. I've also seen local builders get aggressive on price lately; one just cut a home by $150,000, so there are deals out there. See [Lakeville](/communities/lakeville) and [new construction](/new-construction).

## Prior Lake: new build, more room

About **$1,050,000** for five bedrooms, five baths and roughly **4,300 finished square feet**, about the same size as Lakeville, on **more than half an acre** in a golf community with walking trails. Real acreage runs about the same price as Lakeville. **Lakefront** is a different game: in Lakeville and Prior Lake, think roughly $1.5 million to $2 million as a starting point on many lakes, with Prior Lake a bit more expensive. I've lived on Prior Lake and on Lake Marion in Lakeville, so we know these lakes. See [Prior Lake](/communities/prior-lake) and [lake homes](/lake-homes).

## Plymouth: location over land

Plymouth is nearly built out, so acreage is tough to find; a million dollars buys **location**. My pick was **$950,000**: four bedrooms, four baths, just over **4,100 finished square feet**, a 1960s colonial on **two-thirds of an acre**, minutes from Wayzata and Lake Minnetonka. An established neighborhood, very different from a new build. See [Plymouth](/communities/plymouth).

## Blaine: new construction, more finishes

Blaine leans hard into new construction. The listing: **$975,000**, six bedrooms, four baths, about **4,250 finished square feet**, built in 2023, with a pond view and a **four-car heated garage**. Blaine sits a little farther out, so your money goes further and you can expect nicer finishes than closer in. Most new construction is on city-size lots, the same tradeoff as Lakeville. See [Blaine](/communities/blaine).

## Lake Elmo: two kinds of homes in one city

Lake Elmo mixes older country-style properties with real land and newer construction near the highway. My pick was **just under $1 million**: about **3,400 finished square feet**, four bedrooms, four baths, with a nice open floor plan. Most new construction is on regular lots, but bigger custom homes with more land and space between neighbors are there too. Our team has sold homes in Lake Elmo, including to clients who moved from Stillwater for newer construction. See [Lake Elmo](/communities/lake-elmo).

## Faribault: the most house and land

South of the metro on I-35, Faribault was the **cheapest on the list at $939,000** and also the **biggest**: more than **5,300 square feet**, five bedrooms, four baths, on **2.5 acres on Circle Lake**. (Fun history: Circle Lake is where Jesse James is said to have hidden out after the Northfield bank raid, which Northfield still marks every September with Defeat of Jesse James Days.) Out there, this budget buys serious land, lakefront or a big custom home. To be honest, the lakes in that farm country aren't as clear as many metro lakes, or the lakes up in cabin country. Faribault and nearby Dundas both give you more house and land for the money; the tradeoff is distance. See [Faribault](/communities/faribault).

## Where I'd put my own money

If I had to choose right now, it would be **Credit River** or **Prior Lake**, depending on what I wanted:

- **Lakeshore with less house:** Prior Lake. You'll be closer to neighbors, and plan on about **$2 million** for something really nice on the water.
- **More land and a bigger house** (maybe a pool): Credit River, where about **$1.2 million to $1.5 million** buys a lot of home, and I think there's real value right now.

A million dollars never plays out the same way twice. Where you put it decides the life you get. And a bigger budget doesn't protect you from a bad investment, so look closely at any home before you buy.
