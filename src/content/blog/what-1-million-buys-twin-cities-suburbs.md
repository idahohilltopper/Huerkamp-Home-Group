**Quick answer:** at each community's price per square foot over the past year, $1 million buys about **2,000 square feet in Wayzata**, about **3,400 in Edina**, about **3,900 in Minneapolis** and about **5,600 in Burnsville**. Across the 16-county Twin Cities region, it's about 4,600 square feet. Lake Minnetonka towns are the most expensive per square foot by a wide margin.

The figures below divide $1,000,000 by each community's price per square foot for homes that sold in the 12 months through September 2026, from the [Minneapolis Area REALTORS® Local Market Update](https://maar.stats.10kresearch.com/reports/lmu) (NorthstarMLS data). They're rounded to the nearest 10 square feet.

## What the numbers say

**The lake costs more than the house.** Wayzata, Orono, Deephaven and Excelsior, all on Lake Minnetonka, top the list at $389 to $507 a square foot. Wayzata's price per square foot is more than double the region's $217, and all four towns sit on Lake Minnetonka.

**Edina and North Oaks are the next tier.** At about $290 to $300 a square foot, $1 million there works out to roughly 3,400 to 3,500 square feet. Edina's median sale price ($696,250) means most of its homes sell for well under $1 million.

**Minneapolis is pricier per foot than most suburbs.** At $255 a square foot, Minneapolis costs more per foot than Minnetonka, Eden Prairie or Plymouth, even though its median sale price ($353,500) is lower.

**In the south and east suburbs, $1 million goes furthest.** In Lakeville, Woodbury, Eagan, Apple Valley and Burnsville, $1 million works out to roughly 4,900 to 5,600 square feet at the price per foot of homes sold there. Fewer homes there are listed at $1 million or more; the [luxury list](/luxury-homes-for-sale-mn) has current counts by city.

## How to read these numbers

Each community's price per square foot covers every home that sold there, from starter homes to estates. A $1 million home in a given town can cost more or less per foot than its town's figure: newer construction, lakeshore, acreage and a walkout lower level all change it. Use the table to compare towns, not to price a specific house.
