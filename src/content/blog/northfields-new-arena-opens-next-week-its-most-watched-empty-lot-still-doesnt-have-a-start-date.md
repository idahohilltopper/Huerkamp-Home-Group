Two of Northfield's biggest municipal projects are moving in opposite directions in 2026. On September 16, the city cut the ribbon on a $26.5 million ice arena that came in on schedule and, by the city's own account, under budget. Meanwhile on Division Street, the lot where the Archer House Inn burned down in November 2020 is still fenced off, still empty, and still awaiting a revised financing agreement nearly six years later.

Same city government, same stretch of years, two very different outcomes. The reason isn't luck, and it isn't really about hockey versus history. It's about who was on the hook for each project, and what happens when a single private balance sheet is the only thing standing between a vacant lot and a finished building.

## What Opened September 16

The Hasse Ryt-Way Arena at 1900 Cannon Road replaces a rink that had served Northfield since 1977. The new building runs 52,000 square feet, roughly double the old facility's 26,000, and seats 499 with standing room the previous arena never had. Eight-foot tempered glass panels replace netting on the spectator side. A 4,000-square-foot dry-land training room, funded through Northfield Hockey Association fundraising rather than city dollars, will host stick handling, shooting drills, agility work, and batting cages when the ice comes out.

The building also carries a piece of local art history built into its walls. A roughly 900-square-foot tile mosaic by local artist Michael Sweere sits in the upper level, made from donated and salvaged materials collected specifically for the project.

> "We were lucky to have ice before. But this is what a gift for the community to have now for hockey, for figure skating, for other events they can host here."

That quote comes from a [Northfield News report](http://www.southernminn.com/northfield_news/news/take-a-look-inside-northfields-new-haase-ryt-way-arena-opening-next-month/article_2dd8aea2-c430-4520-b274-7f5ff47ceb6d.html), in which Sarah Belcourt, president of the Northfield Hockey Association, described the new lobby during a construction tour earlier this month. The arena is scheduled to open for the 2026-2027 hockey season, and the city's own site lists open skate as available most Mondays, Thursdays, and Sundays, open to all ages once the season gets rolling.

Even the arena's name took a while to land. Northfield's own communications have used two spellings, Hasse and Haase, and on a KYMN Radio segment this summer, city officials acknowledged they'd only just confirmed the correct one that morning.

## Who Actually Paid For It

Part of why the arena hit its target has to do with how the risk was spread before a single beam went up.

| Partner | Contribution |
| --- | --- |
| City of Northfield | Public financing, project ownership |
| City of Dundas | Cost-sharing partner municipality |
| Northfield School District | Facility partner and user |
| Northfield Hockey Association | Private fundraising for the dry-land training room and related amenities |

Four institutions, each with its own reason to see the project finish, split both the cost and the political exposure. When Xcel Energy initially denied the arena's solar interconnection application over a technical capacity issue this summer, the city and utility worked through it and found an alternate electrical feeder, and the project stayed on schedule and under budget, according to the city's own project updates. Nobody's single signature could sink the whole thing.

## The Lot That's Been Empty Since 2020

The Archer House site doesn't have that structure. The building that stood at 212 Division Street South for more than a century burned in November 2020. The city designated the site for potential tax increment financing that same year, and in November 2024, the council approved a TIF agreement and developer agreement with the site's owner, Manawa LLC, for a five-story mixed-use building called The Archer: roughly two dozen apartments, nineteen extended-stay units, about 8,500 square feet of ground-floor commercial space, and improvements to the riverfront walkway. Construction was supposed to start in 2025, with an opening tentatively set for spring 2026.

That deadline passed without a groundbreaking. Then, on October 29, 2025, the development team working the project, Rebound Partners, told city stakeholders in writing that the plan as designed was not financially viable, citing financing gaps the group hadn't been able to close. The project went back to the drawing board.

By August 2026, the council was reviewing a revised proposal from Manawa LLC and discussing changes to the 2024 TIF agreement to match it. At the August 3 meeting, Community Development Director Scott Wopata told council members something that hadn't been public before: the city has had the legal option to declare the developer in default since the end of 2025, when the original construction deadline passed, and simply hasn't used it. As of that meeting, the city's working target is a groundbreaking in mid-November 2026, with an opening sometime in winter 2027 or 2028. Parking, long a sore point downtown, is supposed to get addressed through a mix of underground spaces, street parking, and an agreement with Carleton College, plus a longer-term plan to redevelop the city block at 304 Washington. Whether local contractors get hiring protections in the new agreement is still unresolved, with the council split on the question.

Nearly six years after the fire. Almost two years past the original TIF approval. Still no shovel in the ground.

## Why the Rink Beat the Riverfront

The arena worked because no single entity was betting its own survival on it. The city, Dundas, the school district, and a hockey association with a built-in donor base and a captive group of families who wanted ice time badly enough to fundraise for it. If one partner had wobbled, the others were still there.

The Archer House redevelopment rests on one company's ability to make the numbers work in a construction and interest-rate environment that's been unfriendly to exactly this kind of project since before the fire happened. The city's only real leverage is the TIF agreement and a default clause it has, so far, chosen not to pull. That's not a criticism of anyone involved. It's just a different risk structure, and different risk structures move at different speeds. One kind of public investment in Northfield just proved it can finish on time. The other is still finding out whether it can finish at all.

## What This Means If You're in Northfield This Month

If you're planning around the arena, the grand opening was scheduled for 4:30 to 8:00 p.m. on September 16 at 1900 Cannon Road, and open skate slots start rolling out with the new season on the city's published schedule.

If you're one of the people who has been watching the Archer House lot for six years and wondering when something will actually happen there, the honest answer right now is mid-November at the earliest for a groundbreaking, and that date has moved before. The city council's meetings are open to the public, and the most current information tends to show up first through Northfield's local coverage rather than any single announcement, so that's the place to watch if you want to know before the fencing changes.

Downtown Northfield isn't standing still while it waits. Bridge Square, the Riverwalk, and the rest of Division Street keep running their own calendar regardless of what happens on one lot. But the Archer House site sits on some of the most visible real estate in the city, and what eventually gets built there, and when, will shape how downtown Northfield looks and functions for a long time.
