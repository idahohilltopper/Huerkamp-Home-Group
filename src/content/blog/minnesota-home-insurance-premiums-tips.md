**Homeowners insurance keeps getting more expensive in Minnesota, and some of it is in your control. I sat down with Jess Gyllen, who owns a Goosehead Insurance franchise, to talk through five things that drive premiums up, and what buyers should think about before they make an offer.**

Examples are as discussed in my video above (2025). Every carrier has different rules, so get quotes on a specific home before you commit.

## 1. Location

- **Carrier availability varies by county.** Some carriers simply won't write policies in certain counties, which limits your options and can raise prices.
- **Flood zones.** Homes in a FEMA flood zone, or with a recent flood history, can be harder to insure. You can check FEMA's flood maps yourself, and your agent can help.
- **Distance to a fire hydrant and fire station.** Carriers often look for a hydrant within about **1,000 feet** and a fire station within about **5 miles**. On a roughly $1 million home, Jess saw premiums around **$5,600** a year inside those limits versus about **$13,000** outside them. If you're shopping for acreage, get an insurance quote early.

## 2. Your insurance score and claims history

Your credit helps, but carriers also look at your **insurance score**, which reflects your claims history (home, auto, boats and snowmobiles) and things like driving violations. Even **calling to ask about a claim** can be noted on your file. Claims typically follow you for three to five years.

**Don't get claim-happy.** Water damage you could have prevented, like an ignored roof leak or a failed sump pump, counts against you more than events out of your control like hail, tornadoes or fire. If a claim is $3,000 and your deductible is $1,000, you net $2,000, but the higher premiums (or even non-renewal) that follow can cost more than that. Small water issues are often better handled out of pocket.

## 3. The home itself

- **Roof age.** Many carriers want to see a roof **10 years old or newer**; older roofs may get limited coverage or none.
- **Cedar shake roofs** can be hard to insure, even when fairly new, because they're expensive to replace. If one is mossy or worn, get a specialist's inspection and factor it into your offer.
- **Knob-and-tube wiring**, common in homes from roughly the 1880s to 1930s, is a fire risk, and some carriers won't insure it at all. Older homes on streets like Summit Avenue in Saint Paul can still have it.
- **Wood or coal secondary heat sources** (beyond a standard fireplace) are seen as higher risk than gas or electric.

## 4. Coverage and deductibles

Carriers use a **replacement cost estimator** based on square footage, finishes, craftsmanship and age. If you think that number is low, **extended dwelling coverage** (often 25% or 50% more) is a good safety net.

Then pick your deductible. A **higher deductible means a lower premium**, and the industry is moving that way anyway. Deductibles of **$5,000 or $10,000** are increasingly common on higher-value homes, and many carriers now use a separate **wind/hail deductible of about 1% of dwelling coverage** ($5,000 on a $500,000 home).

Why? Minnesota has been a money-losing state for insurers in recent years, largely because of hail. Roof replacement costs have soared, and for a while, some neighborhoods seemed to get new roofs, siding and gutters every few years. Premiums rose for everyone.

## 5. How you use the home

- **Short-term rentals.** If any part of the home is rented for less than 30 days, tell your agent. Many carriers avoid Airbnbs, but specialty carriers cover them. Hiding it can mean a denied claim, a cancelled policy and even premiums clawed back.
- **Pets.** Some dog breeds or any bite history must be disclosed. It doesn't always mean a denial, but it can change your liability coverage.
- **Trampolines and pools.** Disclose them. Safety nets, in-ground trampolines, pool fences and locking covers help. Carriers send inspectors and increasingly use drones, so they'll find out.
- **Hot tubs.** Disclose them too, especially if you want one covered.

Honesty really is the best policy here.
