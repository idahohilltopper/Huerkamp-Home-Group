**For the same monthly cost as a nice downtown condo, you could have a suburban home with twice the square footage, and that's not an exaggeration. After 25 years and more than 3,700 closings, here's the city vs. suburbs breakdown in every category buyers care about. No agenda.**

Figures are as cited in my video above (August 2026).

## Cost: sticker price vs. monthly payment

On paper, the city looks cheaper. The Minneapolis median sits in the **high $300,000s**, while [Maple Grove](/communities/maple-grove) is closer to $400,000, [Plymouth](/communities/plymouth) is in the high $400,000s and [Eden Prairie](/communities/eden-prairie) is usually in the mid-$500,000s.

That flips when you look at what you get. City homes are older, lots are smaller and many need updates. A nice **1,800-square-foot downtown condo** can run around **$6,000 a month** once you stack the mortgage, taxes, HOA and parking. That same monthly cost can buy a suburban home with **four bedrooms, a real yard and a three-car garage**.

Taxes vary too. In the video, I cited effective property tax rates of about **1.17%** around Eden Prairie and Chanhassen versus about **0.99%** in Dakota County, which adds up on a mid-priced home over time. Run your numbers with our [affordability calculator](/calculators/affordability).

## Space and character

A suburban evening: hit the garage opener, park inside, walk into the kitchen while the dog runs around a real yard. The city version: circle the block for parking, walk up to a smaller condo, maybe no garage at all. A **$450,000 to $530,000** home in Eden Prairie or Plymouth usually gets you four bedrooms, a two- or three-car attached garage and a floor plan built in the last 30 years.

But the city has something the suburbs can't copy: **character**. Homes built roughly **1880 to 1930**, with original woodwork, detailed staircases and real craftsmanship. Outside of Stillwater, you don't find much of that elsewhere near the Twin Cities.

## Getting around

Most people assume the whole city is walkable. Outside a few neighborhoods like **Uptown, Lyn-Lake and the North Loop**, people drive a lot, just like in the suburbs.

The good news: the Twin Cities has one of the shortest average commutes of any major metro, about **24 minutes**. Typical drives to downtown Minneapolis in normal traffic:

- **Uptown or Northeast:** 15 to 20 minutes
- **Eden Prairie:** 25 to 40 minutes
- **Maple Grove:** 30 to 45 minutes
- **Lakeville and the outer south suburbs:** 40 to 45 minutes, up to an hour on a bad day

Many jobs have moved out of downtown, too. Optum, C.H. Robinson and General Mills have major suburban offices. If you work downtown, the city can win; if you work at a suburban campus, the suburbs win. The **Green Line extension**, planned to open in 2027, will connect Eden Prairie to downtown by light rail. See [the Green Line extension and home values](/blog/green-line-extension-home-values) and our [commute tool](/commute).

## The outdoors

Relocating buyers assume the city wins on outdoor life. That's only half right. The **Chain of Lakes** is one of the best urban park systems in the country, with 13 miles of walking paths and 15 miles of bike trails. But for total outdoor space, the suburbs are a different story:

- **Eden Prairie:** 1,000+ acres of parkland and 225+ miles of trails and sidewalks.
- **Plymouth:** the new **Northwest Greenway**, about 315 acres of preserved green space with almost 8 miles of paved trails.
- **Maple Grove:** 55+ miles of trails, seven recreational lakes and **Elm Creek Park Reserve** (about 4,900 acres, the largest in the Three Rivers system).
- **[Minnetonka](/communities/minnetonka):** next to **Lake Minnetonka**, over 14,000 acres of water with dozens of bays.

The city still wins on walking to a park; in Linden Hills, Lake Harriet is a few minutes away. For acreage, trails and lake access, the suburbs have the edge.

## New construction

If you want a brand-new house and not a condo, the suburbs are essentially the only option. Under **2%** of Minneapolis home sales each year are new builds. In [Rogers](/communities/rogers), [Dayton](/communities/dayton) and [Corcoran](/communities/corcoran), more than half of sales last year were new construction, and in [Lakeville](/communities/lakeville), about a quarter. Prices start under $350,000 in the outermost suburbs and go well past $1 million in places like Wayzata, Medina and Eden Prairie. Builders include **Lennar, D.R. Horton, Pulte, M/I Homes and David Weekley**.

Note that Lakeville approved a **one-year moratorium on new plat applications** in April; projects already approved can keep moving.

## Lifestyle in 2026

I saw pre-COVID downtown up close: buzzing streets and packed coffee shops. Today isn't the same, and I won't sugarcoat it. Downtown office vacancy was around **30%** in 2025, with millions of square feet empty. There's some momentum: Target brought its corporate team back three days a week, and other employers are following, just slower than hoped.

The city still has real community character. Northeast is known for its arts district, Linden Hills has a village feel, and Uptown has tree-lined streets and independent businesses. Suburbs are built around trails, parks, community centers and neighborhood events, a quieter, more laid-back vibe. Learn more in [why Minneapolis is the opposite of other cities](/blog/why-minneapolis-is-opposite-of-other-cities).

## Resale: the day you sell

Smart buyers think about who will buy from them in 5 or 10 years.

- **Suburbs** like Eden Prairie, Plymouth and Minnetonka have shown steady growth, with a wide buyer pool: relocating buyers, move-up buyers and investors.
- **The city** has done well in strong pockets like Linden Hills, Northeast and Longfellow, but it varies a lot block to block.
- **Downtown condos** are the softest part of the metro market right now, with some sitting over 100 days. You may not get back what you paid in the short term.
- **New construction** in fast-growing areas carries resale risk if you need to sell quickly, because builders can offer rate buydowns and incentives a private seller can't match.
