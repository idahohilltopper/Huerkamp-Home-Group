What does a great weekend look like when you live in Rosemount? Picture a quick coffee downtown, kids laughing at the splash pad, a lazy hike by the river bluffs, and dinner at a local pub before an outdoor concert. If you want small‑town ease with big‑nature access, you’ll feel right at home here. In this guide, you’ll learn where to play, eat, and explore, plus how neighborhoods line up with weekend amenities. Let’s dive in.

## Start your morning downtown

### Central Park splash pad and playground

Kick off with a relaxed stroll to **Central Park**, Rosemount’s downtown gathering spot. On warm days, the kids can cool off at the [interactive splash pad](https://rosemountmn.gov/849/Splash-Pad), then hit the playground and picnic area. It’s an easy, low‑cost way to spend a few morning hours. Bring a towel, sunscreen, and a change of clothes to keep the day rolling.

### Coffee and boutiques

From the park, pop over to a local bakery or café for coffee and a treat. Hours can change seasonally, so check before you go. Afterward, browse downtown boutiques like A Bushel & A Peck for gifts and home accents. The storefronts feel friendly and curated, which makes a quick stop turn into a pleasant hour.

## Midday parks and play

### Schwarz Pond Park and skate park

If you want a nature break without leaving town, head to **Schwarz Pond Park**. The park covers about 62.6 acres with walking trails, a fishing pier, and a nature‑based play area. It also includes Rosemount’s public skate park and often hosts kid‑friendly activities. Check out the [park details and features](https://www.rosemountmn.gov/facilities/facility/details/Schwarz-Pond-Park-26) before you go.

### Soccer at the Ames Complex

Weekend soccer is a big part of Rosemount life. The **Ames Soccer Complex**, a DCTC and City partnership, runs youth and adult games and frequent tournaments. Expect a lively scene and extra traffic when matches are underway. You can learn more about the complex and fields through the [facility overview](https://www.dctc.edu/about-us/facilities/ames-soccer-complex/).

## Big nature close by

### Spring Lake Park Reserve

For a longer afternoon outing, drive to **Spring Lake Park Reserve** for miles of trails and bluff‑side views of the river. The park’s trail system is a favorite for walking, biking, and quiet nature time. Preview routes with the county’s [summer trail map](https://www.co.dakota.mn.us/parks/Documents/SpringLakeSummerTrailMap.pdf) to pick a loop that fits your group.

### Lebanon Hills nearby trails

Prefer lakes and winding wooded paths? **Lebanon Hills Regional Park** sits a short drive away and offers a large network of trails that are great for hiking and casual rides. It’s an easy add to your weekend plan when you want a scenic change of pace.

## Rain or snow plans

### Community Center and Ice Arena

When the weather turns, the **Rosemount Community Center & Ice Arena** saves the day. You’ll find open skate times, learn‑to‑skate sessions, leagues, and multipurpose spaces for weekend programs. Check current schedules on the [Community Center page](https://www.rosemountmn.gov/853/Community-Center) before you head out.

### Arts and local culture

For a quieter afternoon, look for arts programming and small performances at the Steeple Center. Seasonal events and rotating exhibits keep the calendar fresh, and many shows are family friendly.

### Library quiet time

If you want something low‑key, the Robert Trail library offers weekend storytimes and public events. It’s a comfortable place to unwind, study, or entertain little ones for an hour.

## Eat, shop, and unwind

### Casual lunch and dinner

Craving an easy bite after the park? Neighborhood staples like Carbone’s Pizza & Pub or Celts Irish Pub & Grill make lunch simple, and you can pair dinner with a movie at the Marcus Rosemount Cinema. The scene is relaxed, affordable, and close to home.

### Small retail finds

Downtown shops add to the small‑town feel with curated merch, gifts, and seasonal décor. Expect friendly staff and one‑of‑a‑kind finds rather than big‑box speed. It’s the kind of browsing that fits perfectly between morning park time and an evening event.

## Events to plan around

Rosemount’s community calendar makes weekends lively year‑round. The City’s Parks & Recreation page keeps a current list of programs, festivals, and family events, so start with the [special events calendar](https://www.rosemountmn.gov/204/Special-Events/1000) for dates and details.

- Leprechaun Days: The city’s signature summer festival features a parade, fishing derby, live music, and fireworks. See current schedules on the [official Leprechaun Days site](https://www.leprechaundays.org/).
- ArtBlast and the Rosemount Country Faire: Early‑summer arts programming with exhibits and performances across town.
- Seasonal highlights: Rosemount Expo, holiday markets, park programs, and more appear throughout the year on the City calendar.

## Sample weekend itineraries

- Family morning: Coffee and a treat downtown, then the splash pad and playground at Central Park. Picnic under the trees and wrap with a short stroll.
- Active day: Youth game or tournament at the Ames Soccer Complex, lunch at a local pub, and an afternoon skate at the Community Center.
- Outdoorsy afternoon: Hike Spring Lake Park Reserve for river views or head to Lebanon Hills for lakeside loops. Cap the day with a simple dinner nearby.
- Arts & small‑town stroll: Late‑morning boutique browsing, a light lunch, and an afternoon arts event. During summer weeks, catch an outdoor concert or festival.

## Neighborhoods and housing

### Established areas near downtown

Older neighborhoods near **Central Park** include many single‑family homes with mature lots and short trips to shops and parks. If you like being close to weekend staples, these blocks can offer convenient access. Short drives still cover most errands, but the central location helps you make the most of a busy Saturday.

### Amber Fields and new options

Rosemount is growing fast. The city’s 2024 population estimate is 30,581, up from 25,650 in 2020, reflecting rapid momentum for new housing and amenities. The large **Amber Fields** development is planned to deliver roughly 1,700 to 2,000 homes across single‑family, townhome, and multifamily options, along with new parks and trails as buildout continues. You can read more in the [Star Tribune’s coverage of Amber Fields](https://www.startribune.com/sale-of-435-acre-site-opens-door-for-massive-residential-development-in-rosemount/600135240/).

### Market snapshot mindset

Aggregators sometimes show different median prices, and they change quickly. Recent snapshots suggest an active market with some softening in late 2025 and early 2026. For the most accurate read, ask for a current NorthstarMLS data pull on the day you plan to tour. If you want non‑car options, the city’s greenway vision aims to link neighborhoods to parks and regional trails over time, as outlined in the [Rosemount Greenway master plan](https://www.co.dakota.mn.us/parks/About/TrailPlanning/Documents/RosemountMasterPlan.pdf).

## FAQs

### Is Rosemount walkable on weekends?

- Downtown around Central Park offers short, pedestrian‑friendly strolls to parks and shops, while most errands still require quick drives; planned greenways aim to increase non‑car connections over time per the city’s trail planning.

### What are the best kid‑friendly parks in Rosemount?

- Central Park is great for the splash pad and playground, and Schwarz Pond Park adds trails, a fishing pier, a nature play area, and a public skate park suitable for older kids and teens.

### Where can I find indoor activities on bad‑weather days in Rosemount?

- The Community Center & Ice Arena runs open skate and programs, the Steeple Center hosts arts events, and the Robert Trail library offers weekend storytimes and public programs.

### Which annual events should I watch for in Rosemount?

- Leprechaun Days is the headline summer festival, while ArtBlast, the Rosemount Country Faire, and city‑run programs fill out the calendar; check the City’s special events page for dates and details.

### What housing options are near parks and trails in Rosemount?

- Established neighborhoods near Central Park place you close to downtown and park amenities, while newer areas, including Amber Fields, plan on added parks and future trail links that connect across the city and to regional systems.
