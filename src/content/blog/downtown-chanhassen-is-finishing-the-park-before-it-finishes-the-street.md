If you've spent this summer rerouting around orange cones on Market Boulevard, you've probably assumed that once the street work wraps up, downtown goes back to normal. It won't, not exactly, and not on the timeline you'd guess. The new City Center Park is on track to finish before the street does. The Thursday concert series that got bumped to Lake Ann Park this year, though, isn't coming back to that finished park in 2026 anyway. The two projects are running on different clocks, and untangling them tells you more about what your fall actually looks like than either one does alone.

## The park that's actually getting done this year

The Civic Campus Phase II rebuild of City Center Park has been visible from the road since early 2026. City crews and contractor Kraus-Anderson poured concrete for a new picnic shelter, concessions building, and band shell back in May, with the splash pad and playground going in shortly after. The finished park is supposed to include a playable splash fountain, pickleball courts, a refurbished skate park, and a performance plaza designed for exactly the kind of Thursday-night gathering the city has been running at Lake Ann all summer instead.

The city's own project page lists a summer 2026 completion target, with an asterisk. That asterisk matters. Summer, by the calendar, runs through late September, and a project that finishes at the tail end of that window finishes into a very different downtown than one that wraps in June.

## The concert series already made its choice for 2026

Here's the part that catches people off guard. Even if City Center Park is buttoned up by September, that doesn't put the Thursday concerts back on that stage this year. The 2026 Summer Concert Series schedule ran June 11 through July 30, entirely at Lake Ann Park. That season is already over. There isn't a mechanism for the series to relocate mid-summer once a show schedule is printed and sponsors are locked in.

What that means practically: the park can be finished, ribbon-cuttable, technically open, and the payoff for Chanhassen residents who've been driving out to Lake Ann all summer still doesn't land until next year. There's no published date yet for when the series moves back downtown, and even in the best case, that's the 2027 calendar, not this one.

That's not a knock on Lake Ann as a substitute. It's a 102-acre park with its own concession stand, canoe, kayak, and paddleboat rentals, and it's already where the city holds its Fourth of July fireworks every summer. It's a genuinely good trade for the season. It's just not the same as getting your downtown gathering space back, and the timing gap between "park is done" and "programming resumes" is the part nobody's calendar actually shows you.

## The intersection that closes next

While the park finishes, the street gets worse before it gets better. The Market Boulevard Improvement Project has been running in three phases since late May, and each phase has hit a different stretch of the corridor.

Phase 1 wrapped before the Fourth of July, covering Chan View between the post office and fire station, plus the sections of Market Boulevard between Market Street and West 78th, and between Highway 5 and West 79th. Phase 2 started right after the holiday and has been working the stretch nearest the railroad tracks, including underground utility work and construction of a downtown water reuse pump station. As of early July, that section was still closed from Market Street South to West 79th, with the city projecting it open by mid-September.

Phase 3 is the one to watch. It starts in mid-September, runs roughly six weeks, and targets the West 78th Street and Market Boulevard intersection itself, the main entry point into downtown. That's also the address of Chanhassen Dinner Theatres, at 501 West 78th Street, which means the corridor construction and the theater's fall opening are happening at the same corner at the same time.

Here's how the three overlapping projects line up:

| Project | What it changes | Status as of August 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civic Campus Phase II (City Center Park) | Splash pad, band shell, pickleball courts, skate park, performance plaza | Targeting summer 2026 completion; concessions and shelter structures poured in May |
| Market Boulevard Improvement Project | Full corridor rehab, utility work, railroad crossing upgrades | Phase 2 closing near the tracks through mid-September; Phase 3 hits the 78th/Market intersection mid-September through late October |
| Roers Companies redevelopment (Harlow / Bennett) | Two mixed-use buildings replacing the old cinema and hotel site | Harlow (east building) targeting December 2026; Bennett (west building) targeting September 2027 |

If you're planning a fall evening downtown, the practical read is that the worst traffic weeks land right when the park itself is closest to finished, which is an odd kind of consolation prize.

## The corner everyone's watching for December

The fourth piece of this puzzle sits on the four acres that used to hold the Country Inn & Suites and the closed Chanhassen Cinema, at the corner of West 78th Street and Market Boulevard. Roers Companies is building two structures there. Harlow, the east building, is targeting a December 2026 opening. Bennett, the west building, follows in September 2027. Between them, the project adds close to 15,000 square feet of ground-floor commercial space across five tenant slots, plus roughly 310 market-rate apartments and 408 structured underground parking stalls, some of which will be open to the public patronizing whatever moves into those five storefronts.

Nobody's announced tenants yet. That's worth sitting with for a second. A December building opening doesn't mean a December restaurant opening. Historically, ground-floor retail in projects like this lags the residential move-in by months while leases get finalized and buildouts happen. If you're hoping for a new coffee shop or dinner spot on that corner by the holidays, the more realistic read is sometime in 2027, once Harlow's residential floors have been open long enough to draw the leasing conversations that fill in the storefronts underneath them.

## What this actually looks like week to week

Here's the short version for anyone trying to plan around all of this between now and Halloween:

- Through mid-September: expect continued closures near the railroad tracks on Market Boulevard, with access available from the north near the Wendy's and Jimmy John's corner, and from the south off Highway 5.
- Mid-September through late October: the West 78th/Market Boulevard intersection becomes the active construction zone. If you're headed to Chanhassen Dinner Theatres for the October 2 preview performances of ANNIE, budget extra time for that specific corner.
- By late October: Phase 3 should be substantially done, putting the main downtown intersection back to normal well ahead of the holiday season and Harlow's targeted opening.
- City Center Park: watch for a completion announcement late in the season. Even once it lands, don't expect programming to shift back this year. The next Thursday concert series booked for that stage will be the 2027 season.

## What this means if you live here

The headline most people would write is "downtown construction is almost over." The more accurate one is that Chanhassen is completing an amenity this year that won't actually change anyone's Thursday nights until next year, while the busiest stretch of construction still has to move through downtown's single most important intersection before the season's over. Two genuinely good things are happening. They're just not happening on the same clock, and knowing which one lands when is the difference between planning your fall around downtown and being surprised by it twice.
