For more than 50 years, the Shakopee Gravel pit supplied sand and rock for the roads and developments that helped Shakopee grow. Now the dirt is moving the other way. On Nov. 10, the City of Shakopee posted [a short update video, "The Dig: Shakopee Gravel Pit"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TG_u1Rueu_Q), saying grading has started on the 140-acre site and is expected to continue through 2027.

What's planned afterward is a whole new neighborhood: single-family homes, townhomes, apartments, shops and offices, all around a city park with a man-made lake. Here's what the city has said, what was approved in October, and what it means if you're buying or selling in Shakopee.

## What the city says is planned

In the video, the city describes the site as being reclaimed for [a proposed $650 million development](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TG_u1Rueu_Q) that would create a new urban neighborhood. Current plans, as the city lists them:

- **155 single-family homes**
- **184 townhomes**
- **Up to 534 apartments**
- **About 22 acres of commercial and office space**
- **About 24 acres of open space**, including a city park with large water features

The city also gives a long timeline: [full build-out is estimated to take five to seven years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TG_u1Rueu_Q). Grading comes first, and in the video's description the city says that after the site has settled, roads and infrastructure will be installed. In other words, nobody is moving in next spring. The first visible change is the ground being reshaped.

## How the plan has changed since 2022

The idea isn't new. In 2022, the city sought special legislation to use tax increment financing to redevelop the pit, and its [one-page flyer for legislators](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2022/Subproperty/20220420/subproperty_20220420_TIF-Legislation-Shakopee-Gravel-Flyer.pdf) described a concept on about 150 acres with **215 to 235 single-family homes**, **125 to 140 townhomes**, commercial space, multifamily housing, and "a manmade lake, one-mile walking trail, large public park." The flyer said that without special legislation, "redevelopment will not be a financially viable option."

Compare that with the city's numbers today: fewer single-family homes (155), more townhomes (184) and up to 534 apartments. The lake, the trails and the park have stayed at the center of the plan throughout.

## October 6: the park moves into design

On Oct. 6, the Shakopee City Council approved [a contract to design the park](https://d3n9y02raazwpg.cloudfront.net/shakopeemn/46feebeb-be7b-11f0-a7da-005056a89546-8fb33f66-95ae-40d0-8a5d-26e6ee90535f-1791312256.pdf#page=73) as part of its consent agenda, which [passed 5-0 according to the meeting minutes](https://shakopeemn.granicus.com/MinutesViewer.php?view_id=1&clip_id=344&doc_id=752eead9-d491-11f1-89ef-005056a84798). According to the city staff report:

- The redevelopment includes "a new public park and trail system surrounding a man-made lake" that will "connect surrounding neighborhoods to the existing trail network."
- The park program includes a **playground, four-season warming house and pavilion, lake dock and skating area, pedestrian bridge, hammock groves, splash pad, canoe and kayak launch, sledding hill**, trail benches, picnic shelters, wayfinding signs and opportunities for public art. The staff report adds that "the final selection and configuration of improvements will be refined through design and City review."
- The landscape architecture firm **Confluence** will lead the design. It will coordinate with **Hill Incorporated**, the civil engineer for the surrounding residential development.
- The city has to approve each design phase before the next one starts: schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding, then construction oversight.
- The design contract is capped at **$201,804** in professional services plus **$2,700** in estimated expenses. The staff report puts estimated park project costs at **about $3.5 million**, "all to paid for by Park Development Fees."

The staff report also confirms the work on the ground: "The site is currently being graded," and the landscape architects will work with the civil engineer to make sure the park areas are graded and sized to be usable for park activities.

What the October action did *not* do: approve construction of the park or set an opening date. Those come later, after design is finished and the project is bid.

## What it means if you're buying or selling nearby

- **Buying in Shakopee in the next year or two?** The new homes here are years out. Grading runs through 2027, then roads and utilities, and the city estimates five to seven years for full build-out. If you need a home soon, this is a neighborhood to watch, not one to wait for.
- **Want a new build near a park and lake?** Ask builders, once lots are released, which lots face the lake, the park or the trails, and what's planned behind each one. A trail or a splash pad behind your yard is a plus for some buyers and a minus for others.
- **Living near the site?** Expect truck traffic and dust while grading continues. Long-term, the park and trail connections are planned to tie into surrounding neighborhoods, which can make nearby homes more convenient to the new amenities.
- **Selling a home in Shakopee?** The market is active. In October 2026, Shakopee's median sale price was **$475,000**, up 13.1% from a year earlier, and **$434,990** over the past 12 months, up 8.9%, according to the [Minneapolis Area REALTORS® Local Market Update for Shakopee](https://maar.stats.10kresearch.com/docs/lmu/2026-10/x/Shakopee) (NorthstarMLS data). Sellers received **97.6% of their original list price**, homes took **44 days** on the market until sale, and inventory rose 16.2% to **215 homes for sale**, a **3.7-month supply**. With more homes on the market than a year ago, buyers have more to compare, so pricing it right from day one matters.
