**After the 2008 housing crash, Shakopee lost its momentum and fell off most buyers' lists. Then everything changed: new restaurants, big-name concerts and construction everywhere you look. Here's what's driving the growth, and whether it might fit you.**

Details are as of my video above (May 2026). For a street-level look, see my [Shakopee driving tour](/blog/shakopee-mn-driving-tour).

## The turnaround

Affordable land brought builders, people followed the builders, and restaurants and hotels followed the people. Shakopee's population has more than doubled since 2000, from about **20,000 to nearly 47,000**, and **Scott County** is one of the fastest-growing counties in Minnesota. Five years ago, few people were talking about Shakopee. Now it's one of the most in-demand suburbs in the metro, and the investment and infrastructure are real.

## Entertainment that changed the conversation

- **Mystic Lake Amphitheater:** the Twin Cities' first big outdoor amphitheater, with room for nearly **19,000** people, operated by Live Nation. The 2026 lineup included Guns N' Roses, Iron Maiden, Lynyrd Skynyrd, Mumford & Sons, Chris Stapleton, Dave Matthews Band and more, with an expected economic impact of about **$138 million a year**.
- **Mystic Lake Casino Hotel**, just next door in Prior Lake, is the largest casino in Minnesota, open 24/7, with hundreds of hotel rooms, restaurants and its own showroom.
- **Valleyfair**, Minnesota's only major amusement park, has been on Highway 169 since **1976** and celebrated its **50th season** in 2026, with 75+ rides, eight roller coasters, a water park and ValleySCARE every fall.
- **Canterbury Park** runs about 51 days of live horse racing from late May through September. Next door, **Canterbury Commons** is one of the biggest projects in city history: about **140 acres** of shops, dining and housing with more than **$400 million** in private investment. **Boardwalk Kitchen & Bar** just opened with a big trackside patio.

All of it is within a few minutes of each other. Most suburbs don't have one of these; Shakopee has all of them.

## History meets new growth

Shakopee is one of the oldest settled areas in Minnesota. **The Landing** (formerly Murphy's Landing), a living history museum on the Minnesota River, is on the National Register of Historic Places and includes homes dating to 1844. You can buy an 1890s home downtown or a brand-new build across town.

Downtown is coming alive, too. Shakopee became only the **second city in Minnesota with a social district**, where you can carry a drink between participating spots outdoors, running May through October. It includes **Shakopee Brew Hall, Mana Brewing, Badger Hill Brewing and O'Brien's Public House**. There's a free **Rhythm on the Rails** concert series on summer Wednesday nights, and the **Minnesota Renaissance Festival** runs here late August through early October.

## Access and outdoors

Shakopee is about 25 miles southwest of Minneapolis, typically **30 to 35 minutes** via Highway 169, which usually moves well. The **Minnesota Valley National Wildlife Refuge** stretches along the river for hiking, birding, kayaking and cross-country skiing. Inside city limits, there are **100+ miles of trails** and over 1,100 acres of parks, plus **SandVenture Aquatic Park**, the community center (indoor pool and ice arena) and **Quarry Lake Park**. **St. Francis Regional Medical Center** is in town, with a new mental health and addiction treatment center planned on campus.

## Why it's different

Many fast-growing suburbs are just subdivisions and strip malls. Shakopee has real reasons to be there: Valleyfair, the amphitheater, Mystic Lake, the refuge and a historic downtown. It also still has land to grow into, unlike much of the area inside the 494-694 loop, and its city tax rate is among the lowest in the seven-county metro.

## What homes cost

Based on early 2026 data:

- Homes were selling in the **mid to high $400,000s**, with prices moving up.
- About **44 to 53 days** on market, with a sale-to-list ratio close to 100%: competitive, not crazy.
- **New construction:** about nine builders across 16 communities, including **Lennar, Pulte, D.R. Horton and David Weekley**. New homes start in the **mid-$300,000s**, and in the $500,000s you can get four or five bedrooms and a three-car garage.

## The next 10 years

The city isn't done. More land means more people, the amphitheater will keep drawing national acts, Canterbury Commons is still building out, and the **Huber Park riverfront** project is underway.
