**Inventory is rising, bidding wars are fading, and more than half of Twin Cities homes now sit 25 days or longer. Something real has shifted. Here's why the market feels different, where it's softest, where it's still hot, and what smart buyers and sellers are doing.**

Figures are as of my video above (late 2025 into January 2026).

## Inventory: real choices again

The metro had **more than 10,000** active listings in mid-November 2025, about **2.7 months of supply**, still well below the 5 to 6 months of a balanced market, but enough to give buyers real choices for the first time since the pandemic. The wild 2021 bidding wars are mostly over, though they still pop up in hot spots like Edina.

- **Minneapolis and Saint Paul** had the longest days on market, about **50 days**.
- Move-in-ready homes in suburbs like **Edina, Eden Prairie and Wayzata** were still selling in **under 30 days**.
- By type: single-family about **2.5 months** of supply, townhomes about **3**, and condos about **5**, the softest segment.

## What's driving it

**Rates.** About 6.3% for a 30-year fixed. That's below the roughly 7.25% 50-year average, but buyer fatigue has softened showing activity.

**Seller expectations.** Many sellers are still mentally in 2021, when homes sold 5% to 10% over asking. That market is gone.

**Builder incentives.** About two-thirds of builders offer strong incentives, including rate buydowns to around **5%** versus resale rates of 6.25% to 6.5%, often worth **$20,000 to $40,000** or more. Resale sellers can't match that, and homes needing updates get hit hardest, since buyers don't want to finance renovations at 6% to 8%. (See [builders slashing prices](/blog/builders-cutting-prices-twin-cities).)

## Where it's softest, and where it's hot

**Softest:**
- **Condos and townhomes**, especially downtown. Minneapolis condos and townhomes had a median around $320,000; downtown Minneapolis condos sat **54-plus days**, and downtown Saint Paul condos about **99 days**, versus a city average of 26. Many condo owners who bought from 2018 to 2020 were selling for less than they paid.
- **Dated homes.** Homes with '70s finishes were sitting 50 to 60 days longer.
- **$500,000 to $800,000 homes** that aren't move-in ready, though the $600,000 to $800,000 range saw activity increase from the prior year.
- **Areas near new construction**, which compete with builder incentives.

**Still hot:**
- **Luxury over $1 million**, where days on market fell about 10%.
- **Move-in-ready homes in popular suburbs**, selling in **14 to 23 days** when priced right.

## For buyers: you're back in the driver's seat

- **Concessions are back:** closing cost credits, repair credits, home warranties and real inspections.
- **A seller-paid rate buydown can beat a price cut.** A $15,000 buydown might help you more than a $30,000 price drop.
- **Target homes on the market 25 to 40 days**, when sellers get serious.
- **Compare monthly payments, not list prices.** A builder's 5% rate versus a 6.5% resale rate makes a big difference.
- **Budget for new-construction extras** like a deck, landscaping, blinds and sprinklers, often $10,000 to $25,000.
- **Get pre-approved with more than one lender.** Even a quarter-point difference saves real money. The average Minnesota mortgage payment was about **$2,637**, roughly $1,000 more than in 2021, and applications jump about 18% when rates dip below 6.5%, so be ready to move fast.

## For sellers: pricing is everything

- **The first two weeks decide most of the outcome**, because the most motivated buyers look in days 1 to 14.
- **Overpricing backfires.** Homes priced 5% above market sit far longer, and in September 2025 many Minneapolis listings sat 90-plus days and needed price cuts, which invites lower offers.
- **Presentation pays.** Paint, light fixtures and carpet can return 100% to 300%.
- **Compete with builders**: price $20,000 to $40,000 below comparable new construction, or offer a rate buydown, closing cost credit or home warranty.
- **Be show-ready every time**: lights on, soft music, freshly vacuumed.

Equity is strong (a record $35.8 trillion in U.S. homeowner equity), lending is strict, and only about 2% of sales were distressed, so this isn't a crash. Nearly half of owners with 2.5% to 3.5% mortgages won't move, because the same house would cost $500 to $1,000 more a month.

## The new normal

It's no longer a buyer's market or a seller's market; it's case by case. Expect average days on market of 40 to 48 rather than 2021's 20, with results varying by price, type, location and condition. NAR says sales could rise 14% if rates reach the low 6s, and builders are the wild card. Success now comes from strategy, not luck.
