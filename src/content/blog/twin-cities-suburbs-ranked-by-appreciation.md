**A home bought in one Twin Cities suburb in 2015 for about $300,000 was worth roughly $270,000 more by 2025. I pulled closed-sale data zip code by zip code and ranked seven popular areas by appreciation. The winner is one most buyers never even considered.**

Figures are median sale prices as cited in my video above (June 2026). Past appreciation doesn't guarantee future results.

## The dollar countdown

| Rank | Area | 2015 median | 2025 median | Gain | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Minneapolis (citywide) | $230,000 | $315,000 | $85,000 | ~$700 |
| 6 | [Bloomington](/communities/bloomington) | $200,000 | ~$345,000 | $145,000 | ~$1,200 |
| 5 | [Burnsville](/communities/burnsville) | $220,000 | $370,000 | $150,000 | ~$1,250 |
| 4 | [Apple Valley](/communities/apple-valley) | $225,000 | $380,000 | $155,000 | ~$1,300 |
| 3 | [Prior Lake](/communities/prior-lake) | $300,000 | $500,000 | $200,000 | ~$1,650 |
| 2 | [Lakeville](/communities/lakeville) | $315,000 | $520,000 | $205,000 | ~$1,700 |
| 1 | [Dayton](/communities/dayton) | $300,000 | $570,000 | $270,000 | ~$2,250 |

**7. Minneapolis.** It trailed every suburb on the list, partly because many buyers chose space, land and newer homes outside the city. The citywide number also hides much stronger pockets inside the city. And $85,000 over a decade on a home you're living in is still a good outcome.

**6. Bloomington.** It had the lowest entry point on the list. The Mall of America, the airport minutes away and one of the metro's densest job corridors kept demand strong. See [living in Bloomington](/blog/living-in-bloomington-mn).

**5. Burnsville.** It sits where 35W and 35E split, with easy access to both downtowns. Buyers priced out of closer-in suburbs landed here and stayed. It's quiet and steady, not flashy. See [living in Burnsville](/blog/living-in-burnsville-mn).

**4. Apple Valley.** It had an affordable 2015 entry point, a solid retail corridor, well-kept parks and steady demand: no big headlines, just consistent gains. See [living in Apple Valley](/blog/living-in-apple-valley-mn).

**3. Prior Lake.** People buy here for the water and the feeling of being somewhere that doesn't look like every other suburb. That draw never faded, in good years or slow ones. See [living in Prior Lake](/blog/living-in-prior-lake-mn).

**2. Lakeville.** My hometown had the highest starting price in the group. It was still being built out the whole decade (and still is), with new neighborhoods and steady demand. See [why Lakeville is the most underrated suburb](/blog/lakeville-mn-most-underrated-suburb).

**1. Dayton.** Nobody saw this one coming, including me. Dayton had development expansion, major infrastructure investment and room for new construction in a metro running out of it. It wasn't on most people's radar in 2015.

## The percentage ranking

Flip to percentage gain and the order changes:

| Area | 10-year gain |
|---|---|
| Dayton | ~90% |
| Bloomington | ~73% |
| Apple Valley | ~69% |
| Burnsville | ~68% |
| Prior Lake | ~67% |
| Lakeville | ~65% |
| Minneapolis | ~37% |

Lakeville, number two in dollars, drops near the bottom in percentage terms because its higher starting price left less room to multiply. Prior Lake is the same story. Three of the top four by percentage started under about **$230,000**. Buyers who stretched for pricier areas got larger dollar gains; buyers who came in at the lower end grew their equity faster in percentage terms. A buyer with a $200,000 budget in Bloomington in 2015 outperformed nearly every higher-priced suburb by percentage.

## What this means for buyers in 2026

Seven areas, ten years, and **not one went backwards**. Over this decade, the market didn't reward people who researched forever and tried to time the bottom. It rewarded people who committed and bought.

People ask me which suburb will win the next decade. They all did well in the last one. The real question is which place fits your life: match your budget to the right area, find the lifestyle that makes sense, and buy when you're ready. Nobody can promise the next ten years will look the same, but that framework held up.
