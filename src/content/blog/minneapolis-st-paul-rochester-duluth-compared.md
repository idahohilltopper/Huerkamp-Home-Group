**Moving to Minnesota? You probably assume Minneapolis is the best place to live. But biggest doesn't mean best. Here are Minnesota's four major cities (Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Rochester and Duluth) head-to-head on home prices, jobs, commutes, outdoors and daily life.**

Figures are as of my video above (October 2026).

## Location and population

| City | Population (about) | Change since 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| Minneapolis | 430,000 | +400 (essentially flat) |
| Saint Paul | 306,000 | −4,700 (about −1.5%) |
| Rochester | 124,000 | +2,900 (about +2.4%) |
| Duluth | 88,000 | +1,500 (about +1.7%) |

**Minneapolis and Saint Paul** sit across the Mississippi, 15 to 20 minutes apart, anchoring a metro of about 3.7 million. **Rochester** is about 90 minutes southeast, and **Duluth** about two and a half hours north on I-35, at the western tip of Lake Superior.

I think of Rochester and Duluth as **island cities**. Rochester has one main suburb (Byron) and Duluth has Hermantown, with no big suburban ring. The Twin Cities are the opposite: suburbs all around, so people can live in one place and work somewhere else entirely.

## Home prices and cost of living

- **Minneapolis:** mid to high $300,000s, with the **deepest luxury market** of the four, especially around more than a dozen lakes in the city. It's had more $1 million-plus sales than Saint Paul over the last year.
- **Saint Paul:** right around **$300,000**, with a much smaller high-end market.
- **Rochester:** mid to high $300,000s, sometimes higher than parts of the Twin Cities metro, though everyday costs run a little lower.
- **Duluth:** around **$300,000**, with the lowest overall cost of living of the four.

## Jobs

- **Twin Cities:** the widest mix. Minnesota's 18 Fortune 500 headquarters are mostly here (UnitedHealth, Target, Best Buy, 3M, U.S. Bancorp, General Mills), spread across health care, food and banking. If one industry slows, you have options.
- **Rochester:** health care drives everything, with Mayo Clinic as the main employer. That focus brings **stability**, since people need care when the economy slows. Mayo's roughly **$5 billion downtown expansion** covers about 2.4 million square feet across five buildings through about 2030, and state estimates put it near 6,000 new jobs.
- **Duluth:** tourism, manufacturing, shipping on Lake Superior and mining. The **Mesabi Range** to the north once produced a huge share of the world's iron ore, but mining runs in cycles, so Duluth sees bigger economic ups and downs. **Cirrus Aircraft** is headquartered here, with close to 1,600 employees.

Your job may narrow your choice quickly.

## Commuting and travel

The Twin Cities are the only one of the four with real traffic, and even that is mild compared with similar metros, let alone the coasts. If you have an hour commute here, it's usually because you chose to live far out. Road construction is the wild card. (Two seasons: winter and road construction.)

Rochester and Duluth have barely any traffic. **Advantage: Rochester and Duluth.** But the Twin Cities give you **MSP Airport**, about 10 miles from both downtowns and ranked the #1 mega airport in North America by J.D. Power two years running. Rochester and Duluth have regional airports with far fewer flights.

## Outdoor access

- **Duluth** may have the best setting of all. It's the gateway to the **North Shore**, about 150 miles along Lake Superior to the Canadian border, past waterfalls, state parks and **Split Rock Lighthouse**. Plenty of Twin Cities residents vacation here.
- **Rochester** is near **Bluff Country** around Winona and La Crosse, with river valleys and limestone bluffs, plus the paved Root River Trail nearby, but fewer lakes.
- **The Twin Cities** put nature inside the city: Minneapolis has close to 180 parks (about 7,000 acres) linked by the roughly 50-mile Grand Rounds. Cabin and lake country starts about two hours north.

## Things to do

- **Twin Cities:** every major pro sport (the Vikings, Twins, Wild, Timberwolves and Minnesota United), plus the Lynx and the Frost in women's pro sports. Add the Guthrie, the Walker, the Mall of America and the biggest restaurant scene.
- **Duluth:** Canal Park and year-round outdoor recreation.
- **Rochester:** quieter and compact, centered on downtown and the medical campus, with a dining and events scene growing alongside the city.
- **Saint Paul:** a denser historic core, including **Summit Avenue**, the country's longest preserved stretch of Victorian homes at about 4.5 miles.

## What each city is built around

- **Minneapolis:** the widest mix of jobs, lakes inside the city and the widest range of homes and prices.
- **Saint Paul:** government and institutions, with older architecture, the **same job market as Minneapolis at a lower price**, and only 10 to 15 minutes from downtown Minneapolis. Don't leave it off your list.
- **Rochester:** health care, which means predictability year to year, with less variety.
- **Duluth:** Lake Superior and cyclical industries, with the lowest prices, the best scenery and the most economic risk.

There's no single best city, just the one that fits what matters most to you: price, jobs, stability or the outdoors.
