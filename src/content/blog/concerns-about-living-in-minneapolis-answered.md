**People have a lot of concerns about moving to Minneapolis: the winter, the taxes, the mosquitoes. Those are fair. But a lot of what people worry about is overblown, and some of it is just wrong. Here are honest answers to the questions people actually Google.**

Details are as cited in my video above (June 2026).

## Can you afford to live here?

Yes. Minneapolis ranks among the more affordable big metros, and housing, utilities, health care and transportation all track below many comparable cities. Homes in the city core have been selling in the **mid-$300,000s**, with [Woodbury](/communities/woodbury) and [Lakeville](/communities/lakeville) in the mid-$400,000s and [Maple Grove](/communities/maple-grove) in the low $400,000s and up. Compared with Chicago (higher property taxes and cost of living) or Denver (prices per square foot well past ours), that's strong value. Austin has better weather, at least part of the year, and a lot more traffic.

## Why are property taxes so high?

Two big reasons. First, **freeze-thaw cycles** wreck roads every winter, so repairs never stop. Minnesota had more than 200 road and bridge projects planned for 2026, and Minneapolis has topped $1 billion in construction value for 15 straight years. Second, Minnesota funds many public services at the county level. What you get back is real: well-kept roads, top-tier health care, and parks the **Trust for Public Land** ranks among the best in the country, with nearly every resident within a 10-minute walk of a park. Blame Mother Nature as much as the legislature. More in [7 mistakes people make moving to Minneapolis](/blog/mistakes-moving-to-minneapolis).

## How bad is winter?

It's a real winter. January highs average in the mid-20s, lows hit single digits, and wind chills go lower. Snowfall averages around 50 inches a year. But Minneapolis may be the best-equipped city in the country for it. A **snow emergency** triggers a three-day plowing schedule, and you can get a foot of snow and be driving at highway speed the next day. Downtown, the **skyway system** connects many blocks, so you can live, work, shop and eat without going outside.

## Is winter driving dangerous?

Two words: **slow down**. The real hazards are black ice and freezing rain. MnDOT pre-treats major roads with brine before storms and plows fast. Your job: manage your speed and get **dedicated winter tires**, which make all the difference in stopping.

## Do you need a car? Does transit work?

Transit keeps improving. The **Blue Line** runs from downtown Minneapolis to the airport and Mall of America, the **Green Line** connects the two downtowns through the University of Minnesota, and the **Gold Line** opened in 2025 in the east metro. The Green Line extension to Eden Prairie is planned for 2027. In the urban core and inner-ring suburbs, transit works well; in the outer ring, you'll want a car. The metro's average commute is short, in the low 20s of minutes, and you can get almost anywhere in the Twin Cities in under an hour.

## Are suburban commutes manageable?

Come in early and leave early, or come in late and leave late. Typical drives to downtown Minneapolis in normal traffic: **Woodbury** and **Maple Grove** about 25 to 30 minutes, **Lakeville** about 30 to 45 minutes on I-35. Try our [commute tool](/commute).

## Will the roads keep up with growth?

Austin grew fast and skipped the infrastructure part. Minneapolis has invested consistently. **MSP Airport** has repeatedly been rated the top mega airport in North America by J.D. Power.

## Is Minneapolis just a transplant city?

No. The economy is homegrown. The metro has more Fortune 500 headquarters per capita than any major metro, including **UnitedHealth Group, Target, Best Buy, General Mills and Ameriprise**, all built here. That creates stability and civic pride, and people who move here tend to stay. More in [why Minneapolis is an underrated economic powerhouse](/blog/minneapolis-underrated-economic-powerhouse).

## Does "Minnesota nice" actually exist?

Yes. People are warm, polite, helpful and community-minded. But there's a saying, "Minnesota nice, Minnesota ice": surface friendliness everywhere, deep friendships slower to build. That's partly a compliment, because a lot of people have deep roots and long friendships here. Join a league, show up to neighborhood events and be consistent; that's how you break in.

## Two downtowns?

Yes. Minneapolis and Saint Paul are about 8 miles apart with completely different characters. **Downtown Minneapolis** is more urban and corporate, with the Four Seasons, U.S. Bank Stadium, Target Field and Target Center; game days bring real energy. **Downtown Saint Paul** is more historic, river-oriented, quieter and relaxed. See [Saint Paul vs. Minneapolis](/blog/saint-paul-vs-minneapolis).

## Is there anything to do?

Plenty. Minneapolis was included in the **Michelin Guide's** new American Great Lakes region in 2026, and the city already has many James Beard–recognized chefs. The **Minneapolis Institute of Art** is free, the **Walker Art Center** and Sculpture Garden (home of *Spoonbridge and Cherry*) are next door to each other, the **Guthrie** is one of the country's most respected theaters, and **First Avenue** is part of Prince's legacy. The Mall of America is about 15 minutes from either downtown. Summers bring the Aquatennial, Stone Arch Bridge Festival, the Renaissance Festival and concerts all season.

## How bad are mosquitoes and humidity?

It depends where you live. The **Metropolitan Mosquito Control District** treats hundreds of thousands of acres in the metro, so mosquitoes in the city are manageable; out in the country, they can be intense. Summer humidity is real (July dew points can rival the South), but summer is vibrant and the lakes are right there to cool off.

## Are the lakes worth the hype?

Yes. The **Chain of Lakes** sits inside city limits, with about 13 miles of walking paths, 15 miles of bike trails and public beaches. Many lakes are connected by channels, so you can kayak from one to the next, and the city monitors beach water quality.

## Is downtown walkable and lively?

It's getting there, but it isn't fully there yet, and I'll be honest that it has a ways to go. The skyways make daytime walking weather-proof, **Downtown East** has drawn billions in investment since 2014, and Nicollet Mall is being revamped. Compared with other Midwest downtowns, it holds up well. See [downtown Minneapolis in 2026](/blog/downtown-minneapolis-tour-2026).

## How far are other cities?

Minneapolis is fairly isolated by car: Des Moines is about 4 hours, Milwaukee about 4.5 to 5, Chicago about 6 to 6.5 and Kansas City about 7. But MSP has nonstop flights across the country and to Europe, including newer routes to Copenhagen and Rome.

## Will growth ruin what makes it special?

This isn't a city that bulldozes first and apologizes later. Growth has been steady rather than boom-and-bust, and big redevelopment sites go through long community input before plans are released.
